## B1. Scope map (no prediction)

Model: the bivariate AR(1) of the review's item 5 (family F3): two unit-variance AR(1) processes with common coefficient a and innovation correlation q, whose lag-0 cross-correlation is q and lag-1 cross-correlation a·q, so the 4-vector [x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}] has correlation matrix S₄ = [[1, q, a, aq], [q, 1, aq, a], [a, aq, 1, q], [aq, a, q, 1]] and r₁ = a. All 16 Gaussian-MMI atoms are closed-form functions of (r₁, q) through S₄. Computed on the grid r₁ ∈ [0, 0.95] (step 0.01) × q ∈ [−0.6, 0.6] (step 0.01): sts, xtx + yty, sts − (xtx + yty), ∂sts/∂r₁ and ∂sts/∂q (central differences, h = 0.001), the region where |∂sts/∂r₁| > |∂sts/∂q| and its boundary. Overlay: every pair's (r₁, q) for subject 1 (index 0), ts_gsr and ts_demean, DMT and placebo, in each pre-injection window (1–4) and at the peak window (window 6, TRs 301–360: the first window after the excluded injection window, with the lowest group-mean DMT sts of the run and the highest mean intensity rating outside window 5 — bins 11–12, 8.3 of 10); r₁ of a pair = mean of its two regions' within-window lag-1 autocorrelations, q = the pair's within-window lag-0 correlation. Reported: the fraction of real pairs in the autocorrelation-dominated region, per window, condition and variant; the same map for xtx + yty so the sts ≈ xtx + yty identity is shown as a function of the parameters.

## B2. MMI or Gaussian PID? (prediction recorded)

Step 1, verification of phyid's CCS double redundancy against the reference: (a) the reference MATLAB implementation of Mediano et al. (the PhiID toolbox) is fetched and its CCS logic transcribed independently to Python and compared with phyid on random Gaussian pairs; (b) a case with a hand-computable answer (binary variables with a known joint distribution, where every local MI and co-information term is a ratio of tabulated probabilities) is computed by hand from the reference definition and compared with phyid. If both agree, phyid's CCS is used as is; if not, Gaussian CCS is implemented directly from Ince (2017) — redundancy = local co-information where the signs of the constituent local MIs and of the co-information agree, zero otherwise — and verified the same way.

Step 2: the primary sts contrast and the full 16-atom table under the verified CCS, both variants, W = 60 and global fit; the per-subject correlation between the CCS sts DiD and the autocorrelation contrast; the CCS analogue of the sts ≈ xtx + yty structure (the four MMI mirror atoms and R_xytab = min(I_xtab, I_ytab) do not exist under CCS, so what is checked is whether CCS sts is numerically close to CCS xtx + yty and whether the CCS sts level tracks r₁ across windows and pairs).

Prediction, fixed now: if sts under CCS also tracks r₁ (per-subject correlation of the CCS sts DiD with the autocorrelation contrast above 0.8) and also has the sts ≈ self-prediction structure, the artefact is a property of Gaussian PID at high autocorrelation and the claim is broad; if the CCS sts is small, near zero, or uncorrelated with r₁, the artefact is MMI-specific and the claim is narrow. A sign flip of the CCS sts DiD relative to MMI is not by itself evidence of anything, since the earlier CCS result was withdrawn on exactly that ground.

## B3. Lag dependence (prediction recorded)

τ = 2, 3, 5 on ts_gsr, W = 60 and global fit: the primary sts contrast (full engine), the 16-atom level and DiD table, the per-subject correlation of the sts DiD with (i) the lag-1 autocorrelation contrast and (ii) the lag-τ autocorrelation contrast (same construction at lag τ), and where the real pairs sit on the scope map at lag τ (for the AR(1) model the lag-τ 4-vector has the same S₄ with a replaced by the lag-τ autocorrelation, so the map of B1 applies with r₁ → r_τ).

Prediction, fixed now: r_τ falls with τ and the artefact weakens (smaller sts level, smaller |DiD|). The useful output is whether any τ moves the real pairs into the region of the scope map where q contributes comparably (|∂sts/∂q| ≥ |∂sts/∂r|), and what the sts contrast looks like there. If no τ does, that is a limitation to state, not a result to hide.

## B4. The diagnostic (prediction recorded)

Per pair, subject, condition and window (W = 60, τ = 1; and the global fit at the bin level using the run's r₁ and q), the closed-form sts of the diagonal VAR(1) with the pair's two measured lag-1 autocorrelations (a_x, a_y) and measured lag-0 correlation q — S₄ = [[1, q, a_x, a_y q], [q, 1, a_x q, a_y], [a_x, a_x q, 1, q], [a_y q, a_y, q, 1]] — is the predicted sts "from measured r₁ and q alone". Residual = observed sts − predicted sts. Reported: the residual's magnitude relative to the observed sts (mean and per-pair R² across pairs and windows); the whole-brain mean residual per window and its DiD with the primary tests (sign-flip, bootstrap, FD residualisation; the phase-randomised null on the TR-local observed sts series minus the window's predicted value); the per-subject correlation between the residual DiD and the autocorrelation contrast; and a check of the AR(1) lag-1 cross-correlation assumption (observed corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) against a_y q).

Prediction, fixed now: on these data the residual DiD is expected near zero, which would be the demonstration that the observed synergy change is accounted for by autocorrelation change. A residual DiD that is significant and uncorrelated with r₁ would be the first evidence of an autocorrelation-independent component and would be reported as such, exploratory, with no interpretation attached. Dependence on the AR(1) assumption is to be stated: the review's item 5 showed that the real fMRI autocorrelation function decays faster than AR(1) (lags 1–6 of the pooled placebo ACF 0.868, 0.539, 0.172, −0.085, −0.174, −0.145 against AR(1) 0.868, 0.753, 0.654, …), and the prediction uses only lag 1.
