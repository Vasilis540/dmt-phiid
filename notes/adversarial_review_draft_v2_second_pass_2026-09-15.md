Nothing in the folder was rewritten. Computations of my own are listed in the Appendix; the two that matter are a re-run of the finite-sample null (it reproduces to four decimals) with two of its free choices varied, and a recomputation of the split-half statistics from the committed arrays (they reproduce; the rule's pass threshold, 0.407, lies above the reliability ceiling the paper itself computes, 0.386).

**Verdict first.** The revision is a real one: of the 54 findings of the second review, 47 are addressed in the text, 6 partially and 1 not at all (the optional regional test), the numbers remain clean (every value I traced in the Abstract, Tables 1–6, Results 1–6 and the S-pointers matches its file, with one trivial exception), the closed form is right (I checked it against `phyid` on simulated AR(1) pairs), and the CCS-definition and coupled-family additions were run exactly as their pre-run entry says. Four things are wrong, in descending order. (1) The paper states that the finite-sample null was one of the additions "with their rules recorded before they were run (record, 'Part B, items 6–8')"; the record contains no entry for it, it was the reviewer's own computation with its outcome in the review's Appendix B before any entry existed, and the sentence is false. (2) The split-half test could not have returned its "signal" branch: the rule's threshold (half the within-half correlation, 0.407 on `ts_gsr`) exceeds the maximum cross-half correlation two perfectly correlated reliable components could produce at the observed reliabilities (0.386, the paper's own number), so "failed a pre-registered split-half test" (Abstract) and "the shared component is estimation noise" (Results 4) report an artefact of the rule; the observed cross-half correlation (0.32, disattenuated 0.82) is exactly what a fully shared reliable component would give. (3) Results 3 states "sts fell in proportion to TDMI" and uses it as a confirmation of the mechanism, dropping the one of four pre-specified cells in which the ratio moved significantly — the global fit on `ts_gsr`, +0.0204 [+0.0048, +0.0340], p = 0.025, "less than proportional" — the estimator the same paper recommends as "the better estimator"; `draft.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and Table S8 all carry it. (4) "The other half [of the residual DiD] ... are a systematic departure of about −0.01 nats from the AR(1) cross-lag structure" is asserted where the paper has just said the observed interval contains the null value; the null's "half" is one specification (varying its heterogeneity and ACF-shape choices moves the null DiD between +0.0037 and +0.0077, a third to two-thirds of the observed +0.0115), it is reported without uncertainty, and the run-level comparison sets a stationary null against runs that are non-stationary by design. The central claim — MMI-sts contrasts are dominated by lag-1 autocorrelation change where r₁ differs — is supported in the form the Conclusions give it; the title's "reports ... as" and "demonstration" are stronger than that, and the pre-specified demonstration criterion (residual DiD near zero) failed and the word was kept by redefining what is demonstrated.

## Task 2. Fresh review of draft_v2, in order of severity

### 1. Claims not supported by the numbers

**1a. The finite-sample null is presented as pre-registered.** Methods (Residual diagnostic): "Two additions were made in the present revision, after the second review and with their rules recorded before they were run (record, 'Part B, items 6–8'). First, a finite-sample null of the residual". The record's Part B items 6–8 entry: B6 CCS definition, B7 split-half, B8 coupled family; no null, no rule. This is the one statement in the paper about its own provenance that the record contradicts, in a paper whose case rests on provenance.

**1b. The split-half verdict.** Abstract: "the shared component failed a pre-registered split-half test, so no autocorrelation-independent component is reported". Results 4: "Under the rule fixed before the test (cross-half mean below half the within-half mean) the shared component is estimation noise common to the same windows, and the residual and the CCS-sts change are reported as null with respect to an autocorrelation-independent component, with this test as the evidence". Discussion: "that shared component did not carry across disjoint windows". The numbers: cross-half correlations +0.160 and +0.478, both positive; ceiling 0.386; threshold 0.407. The component did carry across disjoint windows, at the maximum the reliabilities allow; the test "failed" only because its pass line was drawn above that maximum. The paper's own next sentences concede "the observed 0.32 sits at that ceiling" and "not that no reliable shared part exists" — and then the Discussion says "the test can show only that no reliable shared component is detectable", which is the opposite of what a cross-half correlation at the ceiling shows. Proper statement: the test is uninformative at these reliabilities; the reliable parts of the two quantities are small (reliabilities 0.49 and 0.30) and their correlation is unresolved (disattenuated 0.82, N = 14).

**1c. Proportionality.** Results 3: "In a post-hoc check with its rule recorded before it was run, sts fell in proportion to TDMI: the ratio sts / TDMI did not change (DiD +0.0005 [−0.0081, +0.0083], p = 0.91; `results/proportionality.csv`). Proportionality is what the map predicts for an r₁-driven change". `results/proportionality.csv` has four pre-specified cells; the global fit on `ts_gsr` gives ratio DiD +0.0204 [+0.0048, +0.0340], p = 0.0248, sts's share of the TDMI drop 0.658 [0.486, 0.817] against a baseline share of 0.908 — "less than proportional" under the recorded rule, and the only significant cell. Table S8 carries it, `draft.md` reported it in full ("three cells are proportional and one, the global fit on `ts_gsr`, is significantly ..."), `CLAUDE.md` flags it in bold; draft_v2 dropped it from the main text and states the W = 60 cell as the result. Since Results 6 calls the global fit "the better estimator" for this contrast, the cell that fails proportionality is the cell the paper prefers. Either all four cells go in Results 3 with the split (proportional at W = 60 on both variants and at the global fit on `ts_demean`; less than proportional at the global fit on `ts_gsr`), or the confirmation sentence goes.

**1d. "A systematic departure ... exists."** Results 4: "So the direction and about half of the residual DiD are what the cross-lag assumption produces under finite sampling, and the observed interval contains the null value; the other half, and a run-level residual three times the null's, are a systematic departure of about −0.01 nats from the AR(1) cross-lag structure that is not located." Discussion: "a finite-sample null accounts for about half of it and the run-level residual is three times the null's, so a systematic departure of about −0.01 nats from the AR(1) cross-lag structure exists and is not located." Three objections. (i) For the DiD, "the observed interval contains the null value" and "the other half ... [is] a systematic departure" cannot both be claimed; the first is true. (ii) "Half" is one specification of a null with free choices; a third to two-thirds is the range over two of them (Task 1, finite-sample null), and no uncertainty is attached to the null value or to the run-level residual (−0.0137, a mean over runs reported without a CI in `diag_tables.md`). (iii) The run-level comparison (data −1.1 % vs null −0.34 %) sets a stationary null against runs that contain an injection at TR 240 in one arm and a documented r₁ and variance drift in the other (window variance / run variance 1.28 → 0.89 on DMT, 0.94 → 1.06 on placebo, review section 6). A single Gaussian fitted to a non-stationary run pools segments with different (a, q); the pooled cross-lag correlation is the mean of a_i q_i, which differs from the product of the pooled a and q by the covariance of a and q across segments — and a and |q| fall together after injection. That is a candidate for a negative run-level residual with zero mean deviation at the window level, it is testable from the run-level mean cross-lag deviation the paper does not report, and until it is tested "exists and is not located" should read "is consistent with one stationary null at the window level and exceeds it at the run level, where the run is not stationary".

**1e. "both of which DMT produced".** Discussion: "ΦR = rtr = −½ ln(1 − r₁²q²) on the symmetric family, which falls with a fall in either factor, both of which DMT produced". Results 2: on `ts_demean` "the signed mean pairwise correlation rose from 0.190 to 0.233 on DMT (DiD +0.0526 [+0.0073, +0.0976], p = 0.0470, 28 % of its baseline)". DMT produced a fall in |q| on `ts_gsr` only.

**1f. The partial correlation is quoted under the wrong mask.** Results 4: "the relation survives partialling out the autocorrelation DiD (partial r = +0.69 and +0.82, computed with `phyid`'s CCS in the review's Appendix A)". Under the published definition the paper committed to (record B6: "every CCS number in the manuscript ... is replaced by the published-definition value"), the full-set partial correlations are +0.83 and +0.86, and R² of the residual DiD on the autocorrelation DiD rises from 0.61 to 0.88 (`ts_gsr`) and 0.36 to 0.83 (`ts_demean`) when CCS-sts is added. The under-report is in the direction of the paper's preferred null.

**1g. The global fit is recommended with one hand and discounted with the other.** Results 6: "For a between-state contrast that does not need time resolution within a run, the global fit is the better estimator: it gave the same contrast (−0.0801 against −0.0809)". Results 3, on why the CCS-sts global cells are not read: "the global-fit cells ... are local atoms under a single run-level covariance and are exposed to the within-run variance change on the DMT run (window variance / run variance 1.28 in the pre-injection windows against 0.89 after ...), which moves every local mutual information". The MMI-sts global-fit contrast is the same kind of quantity under the same exposure (its bins correlate with the variance ratio at +0.37 per run on average, review section 1 caveat), and the paper's own DiD is a within-run pre/post contrast, i.e. exactly the case the recommendation excludes ("does not need time resolution within a run"). The global-fit "confirmation" of the W = 60 contrast and the recommendation both need the caveat the paper applies to CCS, and the recommendation should be restricted to contrasts between separately fitted runs or groups.

**1h. ### 4. The Varley 2024 positioning, and the contribution beyond it

The positioning is now accurate. I read the arXiv text (2407.16601): the synergy is defined as I(X_t; X_{t+1}) − max_i I(X^i_t; X_{t+1}); the disintegrated-system result I_Syn(X⊥) = I(X²_t; X²_{t+1}) follows from additivity under independence and the MMI maximum, with nothing discrete-specific in the step; Eq. 13 gives the change on disintegration in terms of I(X¹_t; X²_t); the HCP check is 1,000 pairs with r = 0.77, 0.97 and 0.64 and 37.5 % of pairs; the conclusion "should probably not continue to be used in its current form" is quoted correctly; the named MMI-ΦID users (Luppi, Gatica, Mediano, Menesse, Varley) are as the paper implies. Varley's text does not mention individual ΦID atoms, lag-1 autocorrelation, lag-0 correlation, Gaussian closed forms or autoregressive processes. On the paper's family at q = 0, Varley's identity is str + stx + sty + sts = S, which the atoms satisfy, and the paper says so.

What is real beyond Varley, in my reading: the sixteen-atom closed form on the AR(1) family with sts = xtx + yty + rtr and the four negative mirror atoms; the derivative ratio as a function of (r₁, q) and the operating point of BOLD pairs; the wrong sign of the excess on real data (a fact about the estimator on BOLD that no prior paper has); the per-pair diagnostic and its finite-sample behaviour; the coupled-family statement that lagged interaction moves sts at fixed (r₁, q); the lag dependence; the CCS comparison under the published definition, including the mask discrepancy in `phyid`; and a within-subject drug contrast with the r₁ contrast beside it. Two positioning gaps remain. Mediano et al. (2021) themselves work a coupled AR pair as their example (ΦWMS and ΦR against noise correlation, per the second review's reading of the arXiv text, section 4), so "the ΦID authors' own worked example is an AR pair" belongs in the Introduction beside "what these do not give". And the paper's "self-information" language inherits Varley's framing without stating the obvious consequence of its own identity: on the family the *entire* TDMI is self-prediction (2S, independent of q), so the question a reader will ask — whether an r₁ change should be called an artefact of sts or a change in the predictable information of each region that sts correctly reports as a change — is answered by fiat ("reports autocorrelation change as synergy change"). The Discussion's "it says nothing about whether synergy in the intended sense is present in the brain" is the right sentence and should come first.

### 5. Is CCS-sts reported at the right weight?

By volume, yes: one Results paragraph with every cell, Table 4, one Abstract sentence, one Discussion mention, and the reasons and rule for not reading it. By framing, no, in three places. (i) The Abstract's "the shared component failed a pre-registered split-half test" presents an unpassable test as a failed one (item 1b); the honest Abstract sentence is "a split-half test could not resolve whether the shared component is signal or window noise". (ii) "CCS-sts does not track r₁" rests, in its strong form, on per-pair correlations of −0.011 and −0.018 for a quantity whose per-subject split-half reliability is 0.30 and whose pair-level reliability is not reported; a zero correlation for a quantity with little reliable variance is not evidence of independence, and the paper's own per-subject numbers are negative in all four cells (−0.28 to −0.43) and the 28-window correlation is −0.64. "Does not track r₁ the way MMI-sts does" is supported; "does not track r₁" is not established. (iii) Results 3 says "Its per-subject relation to the diagnostic residual, and the test that resolves it, are in Results 4"; nothing is resolved.

Two things the paper does right and should keep: the sign is stated to be the originally pre-specified direction; and the closure rule is quoted as the reason no DMT claim is made, rather than an evidential judgement dressed as one.

### 6. Is the split-half null over-read?

Yes, in the Abstract ("failed a pre-registered split-half test"), in Results 4 ("the shared component is estimation noise common to the same windows ... reported as null ... with this test as the evidence"; "the test shows that most of the within-half correlation is shared window noise and that the reliable part of each quantity is small") and in the Discussion ("did not carry across disjoint windows"; "the test can show only that no reliable shared component is detectable"). The ceiling is stated, but the fact that the pass criterion lay above it is not, and the verdict is repeated as if the test could have gone the other way. "Most of the within-half correlation is shared window noise" is correct (0.815 against a ceiling of 0.386); "the reliable part of each quantity is small" is correct; "no autocorrelation-independent component is reported" is a permissible decision; "estimation noise" as a finding is not. The record's own outcome entry is more careful than the paper ("it cannot establish that no reliable shared component exists").

### 7. The applicability section

No named paper is treated unfairly in the harsh direction, and two are treated generously (Luppi 2024/2026: "the r₁-dominance result for sts does not carry over to ΦR"). Four precision problems.

- **Luppi et al. (2022)** receives the sharpest sentence in the section ("by the map it is the most exposed dataset in the table (implied r₁ ≈ 0.97 at TR 0.72 s ...)"). `partB5_literature.md` row 1: "band not determined for this study"; the 0.97 assumes the 0.008–0.09 Hz band of another study. The main text should carry that assumption. "its gradient replicates without deconvolution" is a supplementary figure of that paper (S1D per B5), while its primary analysis is deconvolved; the paper's own evidence that deconvolution leaves the mechanism in place is from TR 2 s data with the Wiener step taking r₁ to 0.79, which does not transfer to TR 0.72 s.
- The "implied r₁ 0.78–0.93" of the in-scope studies are ideal band-passed white-noise values, i.e. lower bounds (this repository: 0.82 implied, 0.866 measured); Results 2 says "those datasets sit at ratios of 25–60", which presents a lower bound as a location.
- **Down et al. (2026)**: the q-signature reading ("a pattern of synergy and redundancy moving in opposite directions ... is the q signature or a mixture and not the r₁ signature") is the family's statement. On the paper's own data the family's r₁/q dichotomy holds only loosely: MMI rtr fell by 10 % of the sts fall on `ts_gsr` (−0.0078 against −0.0809) where the family gives 1 %, and on `ts_demean` the +0.05 rise in q predicts an rtr rise of +0.006 to +0.011 and the observed rtr DiD is +0.0005. The reading is fair as a family statement and should be flagged as one.
- **Liardi et al. (2025)** is called "the standard remedy for MMI's pathologies" and "null-model normalisation of atoms" by "the ΦID authors"; B5 records it as a PID (not ΦID) null on MEG (not BOLD). The sentence that matters — "a null that preserves total mutual information does not preserve autocorrelation" — is right.

"The reader should apply it to all of them or to none" is not a position. The paper has chosen "all" (every in-scope row and Luppi 2022 get the sentence); say so.

### 8. The three criticisms most likely from a hostile reviewer at a Q1 journal

**8.1 "This is Varley 2024 with a two-parameter map and a substitution."** The reviewer will say the atom identity is a corollary of Eq. 12 plus the MMI lattice, the map is the gradient of a two-line closed form, and the diagnostic replaces two matrix entries by a product and calls the difference a residual. What answers it is already in the folder and should lead: the sixteen-atom structure with four atoms at −0.5 nats offsetting the synergy block (Fig. 1a), the wrong sign of the excess on BOLD, the coupled-family result that interaction moves sts at fixed (r₁, q) with slope −1.8 against +6 for r₁, and the `phyid` mask discrepancy — all facts no prior paper has. Drop "demonstration" from the title and state the contribution as an account plus a bounded test.

**8.2 "Your own data contradict the account at every joint."** Wrong-sign excess; a residual that is significant on all four windowed cells and time-locked to the drug; CCS-sts up in 13–14 of 14 at the global fit and in the pre-specified direction; sts falling less than proportionally to TDMI at the estimator you recommend; a split-half test whose pass line was above its ceiling; r = 0.95 that drops to 0.85 without subjects 8 and 14. What answers it: report the residual as consistent with the finite-sample null within its interval and give the null's range; report all four proportionality cells; call the split-half test uninformative and propose the test that is not (odd/even TRs within windows, or the independent dataset already promised); restrict the global-fit recommendation to between-run contrasts; and give the leave-two-out correlation beside the 0.95.

**8.3 "The design and the record do not support the inference."** N = 14; ~20 effective samples per 4 × 4 fit at W = 60; 40 % of the primary DiD from a placebo drift; an autocorrelation contrast that fails the temporal null on the primary cell (p = 0.073) while the sts contrast passes at 0.002; a within-run pre/post contrast under a single global fit offered as confirmation; every Part B plan written after the review had shown the outcomes; the three additions pre-registered the morning they were run and one of them not pre-registered at all; the analyses, the reviews and the drafts produced by an AI under the author's direction; a conflict of interest with the group whose estimator is analysed. What answers it: the effective-sample size in the Abstract; the W = 60 windowed contrast as the only within-run estimator, the global fit for between-run use only; the History as candid about the null's provenance as it now is about 33f0b33; and the independent-dataset test as a stated next step rather than a closing clause.

### 12. Results stated as null or unresolved where the numbers justify more, and the reverse

1. **The split-half test**: stated as a null; the numbers justify "undetermined, with the reliable components' correlation unresolved (disattenuated 0.82, N = 14)".
2. **The DMT effect on the autocorrelation function** is largest at lag 3, not lag 1 (Table 5: r_τ DiD −0.0146, −0.0468, −0.0681 at τ = 1, 2, 3; the in-band spectral centroid rises by +0.0023 Hz, p = 0.022, review section 1). The paper reports these numbers and never says what they mean: DMT changes the shape of the ACF (faster decay), of which r₁ is the τ = 1 summary that the τ = 1 estimator happens to see. That is a stronger statement about the data than "DMT changes r₁", and it bears on why τ = 3 keeps a significant sts contrast that r₃ only partly tracks (r = 0.626).
3. **The residual within the DMT run alone** is +0.0077 (p = 0.080), the placebo run's −0.0038 (p = 0.19), placebo share 0.33 (`inference_rows_diag.csv`); "time-locked to the drug" rests on the post-hoc early set (p = 0.0012) and a third of the DiD is the placebo run. This is the reverse case: stated more firmly than the within-run number supports.
4. **CCS-sts at the global fit** (p = 0.0002 / 0.0001, phase p = 0.001, 13–14 of 14, both variants) meets the paper's own weighting rule ("both ≤ 0.005 across variants"); what keeps it out is the closure rule and the variance-change exposure of the global fit. The paper gives both reasons; it should say that the weighting rule is met, so that the reader sees the exclusion is by rule, not by evidence.

### 13. The central claim

Supported, in the form the Conclusions give it: on autocorrelated BOLD the Gaussian-MMI sts contrast is dominated by the lag-1 autocorrelation contrast; here (a_x, a_y, q) predict its sign and 114 % of its magnitude, per subject at 0.99, and the remainder is within the interval of a finite-sample null. Weaker than the title and the Methods present it: the identity is a family statement whose excess has the wrong sign on the data; the "prediction" keeps four of the six correlations of the 4 × 4 matrix and substitutes two, so its agreement with the observed level is partly guaranteed and the informative quantity is the residual, which is not near zero; the null that halves the residual has no recorded rule and a specification-dependent value; the test meant to separate the residual's shared variance with CCS-sts from noise could not have separated them; and the demonstration criterion the authors set for themselves was not met. None of this touches the analytic account, which is correct and new at the atom level. It touches the word "demonstration" and the sentences listed under items 1b–1d and 1g, which should be brought down to the level of the Conclusions.
