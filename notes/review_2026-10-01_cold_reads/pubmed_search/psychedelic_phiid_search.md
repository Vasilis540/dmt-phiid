# Web searches for applications of ΦID to psychedelic data (1–2 October 2026)

C's M3 asked how the fall of r₁ under DMT relates to the psychedelic literature and whether ΦID had been applied to
psychedelic data. The Methods' statement that web searches on 1–2 October 2026 and the PubMed search for applications of ΦID to
psychedelic data of any modality found none rests on the searches below, run from the planning session's web-search tool (a general web
search, not a bibliographic database; its results are the pages it ranked, as listed here) on 1 October 2026 (three
searches, queries not recorded) and again on 2 October 2026 (the three queries below, recorded verbatim). The systematic
check is the PubMed search without fMRI terms that V.S. ran, recorded in the last section.

## Query 1 (2 October 2026)

`"integrated information decomposition" psychedelic (LSD OR psilocybin OR DMT OR ketamine) synergy redundancy`

Results (titles as returned): a Frontiers in Pharmacology article (2018); "Hallucinogens in Mental Health: Preclinical
and Clinical Studies on LSD, Psilocybin, MDMA, and Ketamine" (PMC7880300); "Decreased directed functional connectivity
in the psychedelic state" (NeuroImage, 2020); "Synergistic, multi-level understanding of psychedelics: three systematic
reviews and meta-analyses" (Translational Psychiatry, 2024; "synergistic" in its ordinary sense); "Increased spontaneous

ΦID); "Towards an extended taxonomy of information dynamics via Integrated Information Decomposition" (Mediano et al.,

## What the text says

The Discussion ("The fall of r₁ under DMT") says that no published application of ΦID to psychedelic data was found and
names the nearest work read in full, Liardi et al. (2025), a PID (MMI, compared with CCS and the dependency-constraints
PID, raw and under the NuMIT normalisation) on MEG under LSD, ketamine and psilocybin, which names ΦID as a further
generalisation (its pp. 3, 9–11 and 14); Varley et al. (2024) is a PID on cultures and is not cited. The statement is about what these searches found, not a claim that no such study
exists; the PubMed search below is the check.

## The PubMed search of 2 October 2026 (run by V.S.)

PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), run by V.S. on 2 October 2026 with the string below, as the planning session
gave it to him; the export of all its results, "Save" as CSV, is `pubmed_psychedelic_search.csv` beside this file. The
string combines the decomposition terms (integrated information decomposition, phiid, information decomposition,
partial information decomposition, or synergy and redundancy together) with psychedelic terms (psychedelic*, LSD,
lysergic acid diethylamide, psilocybin, DMT, dimethyltryptamine, ayahuasca, mescaline, ketamine), each in PubMed's
Title/Abstract field. That field takes in a record's author keywords as well as its title and abstract: so NLM's
training module on the search tags says (https://www.nlm.nih.gov/oet/ed/pubmed/mesh/mod04/01-100.html, whose sentence a
fetch tool returned on 2 October 2026: the tag "will search within a citation's title, collection title, abstract,
other abstract, and author keywords"), and the second record below shows it, since the title and abstract of Varley et
al. (2024) name a drug and, of the decomposition terms, "synergy" alone, and "partial information decomposition" stands
in the article's keyword line (the audit of the citations, C30, from the article's first page). PubMed's own reading of
the string was not kept. A study is returned only if these fields name both a decomposition term and a drug: Liardi et
al. (2025), read in full for the Discussion, names partial information decomposition and "human neuroimaging data" in
its abstract but not the drugs, prints no keywords, and is not returned.

```
("integrated information decomposition"[Title/Abstract] OR phiid[Title/Abstract] OR "information decomposition"[Title/Abstract] OR "partial information decomposition"[Title/Abstract] OR (synergy[Title/Abstract] AND redundancy[Title/Abstract])) AND (psychedelic*[Title/Abstract] OR LSD[Title/Abstract] OR "lysergic acid diethylamide"[Title/Abstract] OR psilocybin[Title/Abstract] OR DMT[Title/Abstract] OR dimethyltryptamine[Title/Abstract] OR ayahuasca[Title/Abstract] OR mescaline[Title/Abstract] OR ketamine[Title/Abstract])
```

Neither of the two applies ΦID to psychedelic data. The Methods' statement of the literature search names this search
beside the web searches, with its date and count; the Discussion's statement stands.

Ketamine is among the string's drug terms, and the Discussion names it with LSD and psilocybin for Liardi et al.
(2025), whose ketamine recordings come from a study of subanaesthetic doses (their reference 43, by its title). One
study of S20 Table applies ΦID to fMRI under ketamine at an anaesthetic dose: Luppi et al. (2026), whose macaques were
scanned awake and under anaesthesia "with sevoflurane, propofol or ketamine" (its PDF pp. 3–4; S20 Table, row 5). The
macaques of a second study of the table, Gatica et al. (2024; row 4), scanned under isoflurane, received injections of
ketamine (10 mg/kg, intramuscularly) and of five other agents, and their scans began about two hours after anaesthesia,
to avoid the peak of ketamine's effect (its PDF p. 12). The paper does not count data under anaesthesia as psychedelic
data, which S3 Text §10 says, and the string returns neither study: the abstract of Luppi et al. (2026) names no drug
and none of the string's decomposition terms, and the title, abstract and keywords of Gatica et al. (2024) name no
drug.
