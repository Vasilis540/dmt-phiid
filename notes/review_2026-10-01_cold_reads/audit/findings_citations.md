### C01 — blocking
Where: `manuscript/draft_v2.md`, Results 7, line 173 (replacement R703); the cited results are stated in `manuscript/si/S3_Text.md` §3, line 124.
Text: "The pair of transfer entropies, a lagged measure on the same 4 × 4 matrix […] in population it is invariant under invertible filtering, haemodynamic convolution included, though not under severe downsampling"
Problem: The invariance that Barnett & Seth (2011) prove, and that Seth et al. (2013) restate and apply to haemodynamic convolution, is that of Granger causality regressed on the whole past, with the model order allowed to be infinite; both say that filtering makes the order infinite. The quantity this sentence names is the lag-1 pair on the 4 × 4 matrix, I(x_t; y_{t+1} | y_t) and I(y_t; x_{t+1} | x_t) (S3 Text §3). Neither paper states invariance for it, and it is not invariant. On the family the change is small (0.0015 nats or less in the cases below, where sts is of the order of one nat), so the sentence's contrast with sts is not at stake there; what fails is the statement of invariance and its citation. The words "haemodynamic convolution included" also pass over Seth et al.'s proviso that the convolutions are covered only as invertible filters, which excludes onset delays. S3 Text §3 gives the cited result correctly ("the Granger-causality form of transfer entropy") but does not say that it holds for the regression on the whole past, in a paragraph whose subject is the lag-1 pair. The claim record's two items state the papers' results; "Stated in Results 7" is where they are misapplied.
Evidence: Barnett & Seth, p. 405 (PDF p. 2): "We allow the model order p to be infinite."; p. 408 (PDF p. 5): "even if the unfiltered VAR (2) has finite model order, the filtered VAR (26) will in general have infinite model order". Seth et al., p. 545 (PDF p. 6): "Barnett and Seth (2011) showed that GC is invariant under a broad class of stable, invertible multivariate digital filters"; "the model order for the filter-transformed regressions will generally be higher (in theory infinite) than that of the original regressions"; "provided they are invertible filters—the theoretical invariance applies"; "in which case the filter is not invertible and invariance does not necessarily apply"; p. 548 (PDF p. 9): "GC is invariant to hemodynamic convolution (excluding confounding onset delays)". A population check without data (`te_filter_check.py`, sent with this file): the filter 1 + 0.6L + 0.3L², invertible, applied to both series. For two AR(1) processes with coefficients 0.90 and 0.70, innovation correlation 0.3 and no lagged coupling, members of the paper's family, the lag-1 transfer entropies are 0 and 0 before the filter and 0.0015 and 0.0004 nats after it; conditioned on 10 lags of the past they are 0.0000 again, which is the papers' theorem. Equivalently, after the filter the pair's cross-lag correlations are no longer its r₁(y) q and r₁(x) q (0.2091 against 0.2224; 0.2580 against 0.2430). With coefficients 0.85 and 0.80 (innovation correlation 0.25) the filtered lag-1 values are 0.00006 and 0.00004; on the symmetric family they stay 0. For a VAR(1) coupled in one direction they are 0.0620 and 0 before and 0.0635 and 0.0079 after, against 0.0620 and 0 on the whole past before and after. One of the last two sessions reproduced the first case by its own derivation and by simulation.

### C02 — blocking
Where: `manuscript/draft_v2.md`, Results 7, line 173 (replacement R703).
Text: "Null-model normalisation of atoms (Liardi et al., 2025, for PID on magnetoencephalography) would take from sts the 2S of two independent autocorrelated processes and leave −C, which depends on r₁ at a hundredth of sts's rate"
Problem: The sentence reads as a statement of what the cited normalisation would do, and that is not the normalisation of Liardi et al. Theirs compares each atom with the atoms of an ensemble of random systems that have the same total mutual information, and reports the atom's quantile in that ensemble. Nothing is subtracted, and its null systems are not two independent processes with the pair's autocorrelations: for time series they are autoregressive systems with random coefficients, or with the system's own coefficients, rescaled to the same total mutual information. The operation the sentence describes is the revision's own (its record entry calls it "the surrogate normalisation"); its arithmetic (sts − 2S = −C; 0.056 against 6.07) is right for that operation. The grade takes the sentence as a statement about the cited method; read as the paper's own subtraction with a citation that does not belong to it, it would be a should fix. The sentence is new in this revision, the committed sentence did not describe a subtraction, and the claim record's item on Liardi et al. does not cover it.
Evidence: Liardi et al., PDF p. 7: "the core idea of our method is to compare the PID atoms of the system of interest against those of an ensemble of all possible systems with the same TMI"; "Sample a null model qi (S, T) that has the same TMI as p(S, T) but is otherwise random, and compute its PID"; "The relative amount of synergistic, unique, or redundant information of p can be quantified by taking the quantile of the PID atoms of p w.r.t. the null models"; PDF p. 20: "rescale A by a suitable g⋆ such that null system conveys the desired TMI"; "we propose fixing the coefficient matrix to its empirical estimate". The record's entry on the revision, list A, m14: "what the surrogate normalisation would take from sts (2S) and leave (−C, which depends on r₁ at ∂C/∂r₁ = 0.056 against 6.07)". The committed sentence (`git show HEAD:manuscript/draft_v2.md`, line 156): "on the family the dependence it addresses is r₁'s route into sts, since TDMI = 2S depends on r₁ alone". S3 Text §10 (line 552, unchanged) describes the method as it is: "null-model normalisation of PID atoms, naming ΦID as a possible further generalisation".
