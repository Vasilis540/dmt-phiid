# Referee report — "The Gaussian-MMI synergy atom of integrated information decomposition is mostly self-prediction" (typeset commit 24919ea; main PDF 27 pp., SI 133 pp.)

Reviewed as for PLOS Computational Biology, with the data authors' perspective in mind. Page/line references are to the main PDF unless marked "SI p.". I verified every number I quote against the SI and, where possible, by recomputation (the identities of Results 1, ∂sts/∂r₁ = 6.07 and ∂sts/∂q = −0.19 at (0.85, 0.25), the 32.8 at (0.97, 0.25), the ideal-band-pass r₁ values 0.78/0.82/0.97, cos(2π·0.08·2) = 0.54, the t intervals of Results 3, −7.0 %, 2,569,560 pair-windows, 3,220 series, the study counts of the Introduction against S20). I found no numerical discrepancy between the main text and the SI. The problems are of framing, of what is said about the dataset and about other people's papers, and of readability.

## MAJOR

**M1. The subject-code / pseudonymisation text must be removed from the paper and resolved privately with the data authors before any further circulation.**
p. 17 l. 363–365 (Ethics statement): "This is a secondary analysis of data released by the data authors (Singleton et al., 2025), pseudonymised: the released ratings table carries subject codes, which this analysis does not use (S1 Text)." SI p. 3 l. 118–124 (S1 Text): "The released ratings table ... carries subject codes — an S, a two-digit subject number and two letters — which this analysis does not use ... the codes remain in the repository's git history ... Whether the two letters identify anyone is a question for the data authors [TK ...; if they do, the history is rewritten before the preprint]."
An ethics statement that announces, in print, that a public data release of the co-authors carries codes which might be initials, and that the author's own public repository (cited at p. 20 l. 503) still carries them in its history, is exactly what the data authors would not want published. It also contradicts p. 20 l. 493 ("cloned into external/DMT_NCT/ and not redistributed"): if the ratings table's codes are in the git history, part of the release was redistributed. Action: settle with C.T./S.P.S. now whether the letters identify anyone; if they might, rewrite the public repository's history immediately (independently of the paper); delete the description of the code format and the git-history sentence from S1 Text; replace the Ethics clause by a standard statement (publicly released, de-identified derivatives; no new data; no participant contacted).

**M2. No human other than the first author has checked an analysis that an AI system wrote, ran in part, reviewed and audited — and the co-authors are being asked to take responsibility for it.**
p. 19 l. 449–453: "The analysis code, the verification scripts, the second implementation of the estimator, the design of the post hoc computations, the adversarial reviews and citation audits, and the drafts of this text were produced with an AI system ... The system proposed analyses, interpretations and wording"; l. 461–463: "The reviews and audits were carried out by separate sessions of the same AI system; no person other than V.S. has yet checked the analysis. The authors are responsible for the content." (Repeated SI p. 53.)
The disclosure is exemplary in completeness, but as it stands the data authors would be signing for a 133-page analysis verified only by the tool that produced it. Before submission, at least one co-author (or an independent person) should reproduce the primary contrast (Table 2), Table 1, the closed-form identities and the regional correlation from the public repository, and the AI section should say so. The CRediT line for S.P.S. — "validation (confirmation of the mean-filled parcel ...)" (p. 21 l. 551) — is far too thin for a co-author on a paper of this kind.

**M3. For a psychedelic readership (and for the data authors) the fall of r₁ under DMT is framed as nuisance ("artefact") and never connected to the signal-diversity/entropy literature, including the data authors' own EEG results — although the paper's own cross-modal check supports a neural reading.**
p. 16 l. 338–339: "Why r₁ fell (neural, haemodynamic, cardiac, respiratory or motion effects) is not identified; the account needs only that it did." p. 12 l. 222: "A longer lag reduces everything, the artefact included" (also Fig 6 caption, p. 14). p. 12 l. 226–228: "the regional autocorrelation function moves under DMT towards a faster decay ... (the in-band spectral centroid rises by +0.0023 Hz, p = 0.022)". SI p. 5 l. 205–210 (S1 Text) and S6 Table: "the prediction recorded before computation, that TDMI anti-correlates with EEG Lempel–Ziv complexity within the DMT run, held on both variants: per-subject Spearman ρ across 28 bins −0.2416 ... one-sided p = 0.0020 ... nothing on the placebo run".
A psychedelic researcher will accept "not identified" as honest but will not accept the silence: a faster autocorrelation decay and higher spectral centroid of BOLD under DMT is the fMRI face of the well-replicated increase in signal diversity/complexity under psychedelics (Timmermann et al. 2023's EEG LZ result is in the paper's own reference list and dataset), and the paper has a within-run EEG–fMRI coherence result that it hides in S6 Table. The consequence of the current framing is that "artefact" will be read as "the DMT effect is spurious", when the paper's evidence is equally compatible with the r₁ change being the neural effect of DMT that MMI mislabels as synergy. Required text (no new analysis): (i) a Discussion paragraph placing the r₁/centroid change in the psychedelic literature (entropy/LZ/spectral-flattening results; verify and cite the primary fMRI and EEG papers); (ii) the EEG LZ check in the main text; (iii) DMT's cardiovascular/respiratory effects named as candidate non-neural contributors with a citation; (iv) "artefact" reserved for the estimator's r₁-dependence, never for the r₁ change itself. Also check and cite any prior ΦID application to psychedelic data (there is, to my knowledge, at least one on LSD M/EEG with C.T. among the authors — verify; the fMRI-only scope of the literature search, p. 19 l. 445–446, does not excuse omitting it in the Introduction or Discussion of a DMT paper).

**M4. The COBIDAS checklist and S20 Table make unverified negative claims about what the data authors' and their colleagues' papers report.**
SI p. 40–44 (S4 Text): D3 "how participants were recruited is not described"; A1 "head restraint and mock scanning are not described"; A3 "FOV, matrix, acceleration and phase-encoding direction are not reported by the source"; A4 "their acquisition parameters are not reported"; P1 "neither gives versions"; P2 "distortion correction is not described"; p. 43 "Items not reported in this paper, collected ... the structural parameters are not reported by the source". SI p. 51 (S5 Text) states the audit basis: "every entry except the software was read in full (main article; supplementary materials only where named)". Timmermann et al. (2023) is a PNAS paper with an SI Appendix; the claim-check file in the repository cites only the 12-page main PDF for these items. Either verify each item against the SI Appendices of Timmermann et al. (2023) and Singleton et al. (2025) or replace the negative statements by "see Timmermann et al. (2023) and its SI Appendix". A co-author will not sign a checklist listing reporting gaps in their own paper that nobody checked. The same applies to S20 Table row 5 (SI p. 129–130): "HRF deconvolution not mentioned anywhere in the PDF (the Supplementary Methods it defers to are not included)" — S.P.S. is an author of Luppi et al. (2026) and can answer this; the scope reading should not rest on unread supplementary methods.

**M5. Claims of error or inconsistency in the ΦID group's papers and code that the argument does not need, which are sensitive given the declared competing interest (p. 22 l. 557–560).**
(a) p. 2 l. 53–55: "they call that synergy the persistent synergy but write it as the whole-minus-max synergy of their Eq. 5, in ΦID terms str + stx + sty + sts" — this alleges a mislabelling in Luppi et al. (2024); if it is right it matters (on the family the aggregate is S, not 2S − C), but it should be stated neutrally ("the ranking synergy of Luppi et al. 2024 is defined by their Eq. 5, which is str + stx + sty + sts") and, ideally, confirmed with those authors.
(b) Editorial nitpicks in S20 Table that have nothing to do with applicability: row 2 (SI p. 127) "22 included ... — an internal inconsistency of the paper, noted, not resolved here"; row 3 (SI p. 128) "N = 15 in the Introduction and Results (pp. 4, 8) but 'n=16 for analysis' in Methods (p. 15) — noted"; row 1 (SI p. 127) "no 'DK-66' in the paper"; row 4 "'60 sliding windows of 500 s' in the Fig. 2 legend, p. 4 — noted"; row 6 "'tCompCor' is only an fMRIPrep output listed on p. 3"; row 8 "(unit not printed)"; row 11 (SI p. 132) "The audit's F7 records that the SI's description of Ince's original preprint is not what that preprint says". Delete all of these.
(c) Substantive and public: p. 17 l. 389–390 "phyid applies a different fifth condition (S3 Text)" and SI p. 10 l. 447–450 "phyid implements a different mask: it keeps c at the samples where the four single-source, single-target local mutual informations and c itself share a sign, so its fifth sign condition is that of the double co-information rather than of the full mutual information." This says the group's released code does not implement its published CCS definition. Raise it with the phyid maintainers before submission (an issue or correspondence) and report their answer; if the paper has misread Definition 1 it is a public error about their code, and if it is right the group should be credited and the code fixed upstream.

**M6. Results 4 and Table 3 cannot be followed by the journal's readership, and they end in a non-result.**
p. 12 l. 212–216: "What a change in lagged structure does to the residual depends on how the change is made: on AR(1) pairs it rises, falls or moves with the sign of q according to the construction (Table 3, which defines each; S18 Table). The residual's rise is therefore compatible with a pure autocorrelation change that the generators do not represent, with a coupling change at fixed lag-0 structure, and with a weakening of lagged structure aligned with q." p. 12 l. 216–220 (Aother); Table 3 (p. 13) with a 25-line caption and "/"-separated paired entries. A neuroimaging reader meets "residual", "AR(1)-substituted estimate", "band-passed generator", "finite-sample null", "aligned statistic Aother", "δsym" and "δ·sign(q)" within one page and is then told nothing can be attributed. Keep in the main text the residual DiD (+0.0115 [+0.0005, +0.0226]), the three expectations (+0.0027, +0.0049, +0.0054) with their p values, the per-subject slope and the one-sentence conclusion; move the constructions, Aother and Table 3 to S3 Text. This is presentation, but it decides whether the paper's own limit statement is understood.

## MINOR

**m1.** p. 17 l. 368 "fourteen subjects (six of twenty excluded for head movement by Singleton et al., 2025)" and SI p. 4 l. 133–135 "Region 20 (0-based; LH_DorsAttn_Post_6), constant across the DMT run of subject 8 and confirmed by the data authors as a mean-filled replacement" — both are statements the co-authors must confirm as theirs (the second is also claimed as a CRediT contribution, p. 21 l. 551). Note that subject 8, the subject with the mean-filled DMT-run parcel, is also the high-leverage subject of Fig 3a (p. 7 l. 157 "the one whose r₁ fell most (−0.064)"; without it the mean cross-half correlation falls from 0.694 to 0.486, SI p. 16 l. 784). The two facts are stated a hundred pages apart and never connected; report subject 8's framewise displacement and whether its DMT run is otherwise unusual.

**m2.** SI p. 4 l. 136–137 "one non-finite TR was dropped" — say where (the superseded draft in the repository identifies it as TR 839 of one placebo run); dropping a TR inside a window would break the lag-1 pairing, so state that it falls at the run's end.

**m3.** Scrubbing/censoring is never mentioned (p. 17 Dataset; SI p. 41 P6 "The remaining denoising steps are the data authors'"). If the released series were censored and interpolated, autocorrelation is altered in a motion-dependent way, which bears directly on the paper's input quantity. Ask the data authors and state it.

**m4.** p. 20 l. 493–495 "the release's repository carries no licence of its own at that commit (its three licence files belong to bundled third-party plotting functions), and the data are used with the written agreement of the data collectors and the derivative authors." Check the Zenodo record's licence field (Zenodo records carry one) and cite it; let the data authors decide whether the GitHub remark stays. The "written agreement" must exist in writing from C.T. and S.P.S. The Acknowledgments [TK] and "[TK further co-authors nominated by C.T.]" (p. 1 l. 6–7) should also cover the senior authors of the two source papers.

**m5.** Author summary p. 2 l. 38 "Under DMT the autocorrelation of the signals fell" — it fell relative to placebo (DiD −0.0146, p = 0.0106, phase-randomised p = 0.073, p. 7 l. 144–146), with the placebo run's rise carrying 0.34 of the DiD (l. 148–150). Say "relative to placebo".

**m6.** Discussion p. 15–16 l. 313–316: "That study's phase-randomised surrogates, which on the family give the regional r₁ gradient up to noise, are the table's one test of the exposure: the surrogate gradient's correlations with the six macroscale maps ... were small (|ρ| ≤ 0.26; one significant)". If Luppi et al. (2022) built the gradient as a difference of ranks (S20 row 1: "redundancy-to-synergy rank gradient"), then in a q = 0 surrogate rtr is finite-sample noise (SI p. 128: "sts = 2 min(Sx, Sy) and rtr = 0"), its rank is noise, and the surrogate gradient's correlation with any map is attenuated by roughly 1/√2 relative to an sts-only gradient. The "test" is therefore weaker than stated; say so, after checking how that gradient was constructed.

**m7.** Abstract p. 1 l. 11–12 "underlies most fMRI synergy reports" and Discussion p. 15 l. 303–305 "nine empirical studies ... whose main texts were read in full" rest on a search described at p. 19 l. 442–444 as "web searches run from a session of the AI system (the queries were not recorded)". "Most" is not supported by an unrecorded, non-systematic search; write "the nine studies we found" and say in the Discussion that the search was not systematic.

**m8.** Table 1 (p. 4) and Fig 2 (p. 6) use "AR(1)-substituted" and p. 4 l. 125–127 reads "The data's atom table has the family's pattern (Table 1): the excess sts − (xtx + yty) is negative in the data ... and in the AR(1)-substituted atoms" before the estimate is defined at p. 8 l. 187–190. Add a one-line definition to the Notation paragraph (p. 3 l. 89–95) or move Table 1/Fig 2 after Results 4.

**m9.** Fig 5c (p. 11) is illegible at its scale: the "± 1 replicate SD (±0.0014)" error bars and the dashed step cannot be seen. Enlarge the panel or drop it and keep the numbers in the text. Fig 1b's colour-bar label is cramped.

**m10.** p. 1 l. 23–24 and p. 7 l. 156 headline the "disattenuated r 0.95 [0.62, 0.99]"; SI p. 16 l. 764 notes "366 of 10,000 draws excluded". State the exclusion rule and its effect in the main text, or make the cross-half correlation (0.694 [0.258, 0.895]) the headline at N = 14.

**m11.** p. 2 l. 78–79 "the predicted increase was motivated by the synergistic-workspace account, in which loss of consciousness goes with reduced ΦR within the workspace (Luppi et al., 2024)". S1 Text (SI p. 4 l. 160–161) adds "the hypothesis extrapolates from that account rather than being its prediction"; put that clause in the main text so Luppi et al. (2024) are not read as having predicted an sts increase under DMT.

**m12.** The history of the contrast is told five times: Abstract l. 19–22, Introduction l. 80–82, Results 2 l. 136–140, Limitations l. 352–357, Methods l. 465–481. Once in full (Methods) and once briefly (Results 2) is enough; and at p. 7 l. 133 "The primary contrast of this paper is the pre-specified DiD" the word "pre-specified" should carry "(statistic fixed after the global fit; Methods)" or be dropped here, since the paper itself says what it means.

**m13.** Data and code availability (p. 20–21 l. 483–546) reads as a run log ("475 min wall-clock, rounded down to the minute", "the earlier attempts are recorded in S5 Text", lid closures in S5 Text p. 48). Cut to repository, DOI, licence, contents and how to reproduce; keep the audit in S5 Text. Line 483 "Manuscript for co-author review; not for citation or distribution" must go at submission.

**m14.** p. 14 l. 256–258 "on Human Connectome Project (HCP) fMRI a mutual-information test's false-positive rate rose from 42 % to over 88 % after AR(p) prewhitening (Cliff et al., 2021)" — a striking claim readers will check; give the figure or table of Cliff et al. it comes from.

**m15.** p. 15 l. 300–302 "higher r₁ gives higher sts with rtr moving much less (∂rtr/∂r₁ = 0.06 on the family)" is stated as a general property of spatial maps, but in these data "rtr follows regional r₁ at r = 0.638, with a slope of 0.50 nats per unit, about nine times the family's 0.056" (p. 8 l. 179–180). Hedge the Discussion sentence with the data's own number (the sts − rtr map still correlates with r₁ at 0.79, SI p. 14 l. 640–641, so the point survives).

**m16.** The within-window regression (p. 4 l. 119–121: "the slopes, +5.13 per unit of r₁ and −0.53 per unit of |q|, make the per-SD ratio 1.4") and r² = 0.462 for r₁ alone (SI p. 14 l. 652) mean that for between-pair variation within a window, lag-0 correlation and asymmetry matter almost as much as r₁. The author summary's "the synergy value is mostly ... its autocorrelation" (p. 2 l. 35–37) should be bounded to what is shown: the level (sts ≈ xtx + yty + rtr on the family), the regional map (r² = 0.745) and the between-condition contrast.

**m17.** Session order was counterbalanced (SI p. 3 l. 125–126) and the placebo run drifts (p. 7 l. 148–151); if order is recoverable from the release or from C.T., a check of the DiD by order would address the time-in-scanner confound. Suggestion only.

**m18.** The ratings result of S2 Table (group-mean sts against intensity ρ = −0.98, void under its controls) and the tier-2 verdict are mentioned only in S1 Text; a sentence in Results 2 would let the data authors see what their ratings gave.

**m19.** Abstract is about 320 words; check the journal's 300-word limit.

## What is right and should be kept
The closed form and its identities check out by hand; the sixteen-atom table is internally consistent (the fourteen non-sts, non-rtr atoms sum to zero, as they must). The text is careful to say that ΦR is not affected on the symmetric family (p. 16 l. 319–320), that the applicability statements are "what the map predicts, not evidence about any study" (p. 15 l. 309), and that Luppi et al. (2022)'s surrogate argues against the exposure for their macroscale associations — all of which the data authors' colleagues will read as fair. The history of the contrast is reported without spin; if anything it is over-reported. The recommendation (report r₁ beside sts; report each contrast beside its AR(1)-substituted estimate; use autocorrelation-preserving surrogates that keep q) is sound and costs nothing.

## Overall assessment
As it stands the data authors should not put their names on it: the Ethics statement and S1 Text publish a potentially identifying feature of their own release and the author's public repository still carries it (M1); S4 Text lists reporting gaps in their own paper that were checked against the main article only (M4); S20 Table and the Introduction carry gratuitous fault-finding in their colleagues' papers and a public claim that the group's code departs from its published definition (M5); the DMT effect their own EEG work explains as increased signal diversity is framed as an "artefact" without a word of that literature (M3); and no human has checked an AI-produced analysis they would be signing (M2). All of this is fixable without new analysis, and the numerical core is meticulous and candid. The single greatest weakness for this readership is that the paper cannot say whether anything beyond autocorrelation changed under DMT, or whether any published ΦID result is affected, and says so itself — so what it establishes is a property of the MMI estimator that Varley (2024) and the ΦID authors' own worked example already imply, delivered with a forensic apparatus (Results 4, Table 3, a 133-page SI, a 64-line availability statement) that will stop most readers before they reach the one-paragraph recommendation that is the paper's real contribution.
