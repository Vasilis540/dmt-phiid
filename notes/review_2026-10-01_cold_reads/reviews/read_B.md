REFEREE REPORT — "The Gaussian-MMI synergy atom of integrated information decomposition is mostly self-prediction" (main PDF 27 pp., SI 133 pp.; typeset from commit 24919ea)

Scope of what I did: read the main text in full, then S1–S5 Text, S8–S19 Tables and the relevant rows of S17/S19. I re-derived the closed form with my own Möbius inversion of the ΦID lattice under MMI (all sixteen atoms, TDMI = 2S, ∂sts/∂r₁ = 6.0705, ∂sts/∂q = −0.1892, the q = 0 unequal-coefficient identity, the excess sign change at |ax − ay| ≈ 0.008, and the six Fig 1c values 1.2588/1.2315/1.2155/1.2569/1.3023/1.4111 from a VAR(1) Lyapunov solve) — all reproduce exactly. From the per-subject DiD vectors in notes/review_results/inference_rows_{diag,raw}.pkl I reproduced the exact sign-flip p (0.0038), the residual's p against zero (0.043) and against +0.0027/+0.0049/+0.0054 (0.108/0.220/0.252), both OLS slopes with their 12-df t intervals (+4.26 [3.41, 5.12]; −0.75 [−1.13, −0.37]), both Fieller intervals ([4.45, 10.5]; [−1.60, −0.08]) and the Fisher-z intervals. The numbers are right. The problems below are about what is not reported and how comparisons are framed.

======================================================================
MAJOR
======================================================================

MAJOR 1. The primary contrast is reported without the pre-injection baseline gap, the post-injection gap, or a baseline-adjusted estimate; its size is estimator-dependent and the paper applies a baseline-gap argument to ΦR that it does not apply to sts.
Anchors: p. 7, Table 2, "Pre-injection DMT / placebo mean 1.1554 / 1.1378" and "DiD −0.0809 [−0.1317, −0.0310], p = 0.0038; phase p = 0.0020; 13/14 negative"; p. 7, ll. 148–151, "Both runs contribute: the placebo run's r₁ and sts rose across the session and carry 0.34 and 0.40 of the two DiDs, so the DiD estimates the drug's effect only if that drift would have occurred on the DMT run too"; SI p. 6 (S2 Text), where the deconvolved ΦR increase is dismissed because of "a pre-injection baseline gap in the direction that creates it (deconvolved −0.0124, p = 0.014; raw −0.0042, p = 0.28)".
From results/atoms_win60_115regions-all_ts_gsr_window.npy (ts_gsr, W = 60, the primary cell) I computed: baseline gap (DMT pre − placebo pre) = +0.0176 nats, positive in 10 of 14, sign-flip p = 0.23 — in the direction that inflates the decrease, exactly the pattern used to discount ΦR (where the raw gap had p = 0.28). Because DiD = Δpost − gap by construction, the per-subject DiD correlates with the baseline gap at r = −0.89 (r² = 0.79): four-fifths of the between-subject variance of the primary statistic is pre-injection between-run difference, not response. The post-injection gap alone (DMT post − placebo post) is −0.063, negative in 14 of 14 (p = 0.00012), SD 0.049 against the DiD's SD 0.089; an ANCOVA of Δpost on the baseline gap gives an adjusted DMT effect of −0.054 (SE 0.012; t interval [−0.081, −0.028]). So the "DMT contrast" is −0.054 (baseline-adjusted), −0.063 (post gap) or −0.081 (DiD, the largest and the one in the Abstract), and the only subject with a positive DiD (subject 14, +0.095) is the one with the most negative baseline gap (−0.112), while its post gap is negative like everyone else's. None of this threatens the existence of the effect, but the paper must (a) report the baseline gap with its test, the post-injection gap and a baseline-adjusted estimate beside the DiD; (b) state that the per-subject DiD is mostly baseline-gap variance; and (c) apply the baseline-gap scrutiny it used in S2 Text symmetrically.

MAJOR 2. The per-subject "shared reliable variance" of the sts and r₁ contrasts is largely covariation of between-run pre-injection state, and the Abstract and Author summary present it as covariation of drug-induced changes.
Anchors: p. 1, ll. 23–24, "across subjects the two changes share most of their reliable variance (disattenuated r 0.95 [0.62, 0.99])"; p. 2, ll. 39–40, "across volunteers the two falls went together"; p. 7, ll. 152–156; the caveat exists only in SI p. 16 (S3 Text §5): "it is not evidence that the subjects' underlying drug effects on sts and on r₁ covary".
With the vectors above, the sts baseline gap correlates with the per-subject r₁ DiD at r = −0.84 and the post gap at r = +0.83: the 0.953 of Fig 3a is substantially the two runs' pre-injection difference propagating through both DiDs (the estimator tracks r₁ in every window, which is the account, but it is not "two falls"). The split-half design does not remove this, because the baseline gap is reproducible across odd and even windows and therefore counts as "reliable". Also, "most of their reliable variance" is not supported at the interval's lower limit (0.62² = 0.38). The SI caveat belongs in the Abstract/Results, "the two changes" should be "the two DMT-minus-placebo differences", and subject 14 (sts rose) and subject 5 (r₁ rose) contradict "across volunteers the two falls went together".

MAJOR 3. The verdict that "the calibration is not validated" rests on a comparison that is not like-for-like and on two subjects.
Anchors: p. 8, ll. 204–205 and p. 12, ll. 206–211: "Across subjects the data's residual DiD scales with the r₁ DiD at −0.75 per unit (OLS slope, t interval [−1.13, −0.37]; Fig 3c), an interval that excludes the band-passed rate, contains the AR(1) generator's and ends at the null's; at the group level the ratio of means … has a Fieller interval of [−1.60, −0.08], which contains all three" and "Since the generators apply one change to every subject and the band-passed rate lies outside the data's slope interval, the calibration is not validated for the residual on this dataset"; p. 9, Fig 3 caption, "each rate is taken within one simulation and drawn through the origin"; p. 16, ll. 290–292 (Discussion) repeats "which excludes the band-passed generator's rate".
(i) The generators' rates (−0.18, −0.39, −0.37) are ratios of group means under a homogeneous post-only change; the data's −0.75 is a between-subject regression whose x-variable (r₁ DiD, split-half reliability 0.72) carries sampling error that is, by construction, correlated with the y-variable's error (the residual's response to a sampled r₁ deviation is roughly the within-window slope of observed sts, +5.13, minus the substituted estimate's ≈ +6.07, i.e. about −0.9 per unit), and whose true between-subject variation is mostly baseline-gap variation (MAJOR 1–2). The paper invokes the shared-error caveat for the sts–r₁ correlation (ll. 153–154) but not here. The like-for-like comparison is the per-subject OLS slope computed inside generator replicates with heterogeneous Δa matched to the data's spread; the paper reports only the Fieller interval of the ratio of means, which "contains all three". (ii) Leave-one-out, which the paper gives for the cross-half correlation (SI p. 16–17) but not for this slope: without subject 8 the slope is −0.78 [−1.36, −0.21]; without 14, −0.70 [−1.18, −0.22]; without both, −0.67 [−1.56, +0.23], r = −0.46 — the interval then contains all three generator rates and zero. These are the two subjects with the extreme r₁ changes and the extreme baseline gaps. Either compute the matched slope in the generators and report the leave-one/two-out, or withdraw "excludes the band-passed rate" and "not validated" from Results 4 and the Discussion.

MINOR
======================================================================

MINOR 1. "Not distinguished" is a low-power non-rejection and no sensitivity is given. p. 8, ll. 202–203: "The data's residual DiD is not distinguished from these three (exact p = 0.108, 0.218 … and 0.251)"; SI p. 40 (S4 Text, D2): "No power analysis was possible or performed." With the residual DiD's SE of 0.0055 (t interval [+0.0005, +0.0226]), the minimal detectable difference from a point expectation at 80 % power is ≈ 0.016 nats, nearly twice the 0.009 excess over the band-passed expectation; the point estimate is 2–4× every expectation. A sensitivity statement is always possible for a secondary analysis and should accompany every "not distinguished"/"cannot be attributed" sentence (Abstract l. 26; Discussion ll. 288–290). The test also treats each expectation as a known constant, which is fine for Monte-Carlo error (SD/√50) but not for the ±50 % spread between generators that the three values themselves show; say so.

MINOR 2. Disattenuated correlation. p. 7, l. 156, "disattenuated, 0.95 [0.62, 0.99] (subject bootstrap)"; SI p. 16, "366 of 10,000 draws excluded"; ts_demean "+0.99 [+0.90, +1.07]". The exclusion rule is not stated; excluding draws (presumably ratio > 1 or undefined ceiling) truncates the upper tail, and the ts_demean interval exceeding 1 shows the estimand is unbounded. State the rule and the estimator's behaviour, and temper "most" (MAJOR 2).

MINOR 3. Regional analysis. (a) p. 8, ll. 167–168: "regional MMI-sts (global fit; S3 Text) correlates with regional r₁" while Fig 4 caption (p. 10) uses "the region's lag-1 autocorrelation r₁ (W = 60, windows 1–4)": a global-fit atom (level 1.31) is regressed on a windowed r₁ (operating point of the W = 60 estimator); say this is because only the global-fit regional atoms were saved and that the slope (3.09) is therefore not the family's derivative at either point. (b) p. 8, ll. 177–178, "Partialling does not remove all network structure: the residual map's eight-class F lies in the upper 0.1 % of its spin distribution (p = 0.0009)", read against Fig 4c "(unpartialled map: F = 16.93, spin p = 0.0564)": the two spin p-values are not comparable, because the spin null preserves each map's own spatial autocorrelation and partialling a smooth covariate changes it; a residual map with less variance but less spatial smoothness will look "more structured" under its own spin null. The sentence should not be read as "more structure after partialling". (c) The eight-class F keeps the 16 subcortical values fixed in every rotation while the subcortex is the most extreme class (−0.0235) and the lowest-r₁ leverage group of the 115-region regression; state what the spin test of the F is actually testing (cortical network means only).

MINOR 4. Prewhitening is framed one-sidedly and left incomplete. p. 1, l. 28, "prewhitening cannot fully remove the dependence from band-passed data"; p. 14, ll. 253–255, "AR(p) residuals … keep r₁ = 0.26 … MMI-sts on them is 0.22, its contrast −0.026 [−0.064, +0.011] (p = 0.14)". AR(5) whitening removed 81 % of the level and 68 % of the contrast and its per-subject correlation with the raw sts DiD is 0.47 (SI p. 33); "cannot fully remove" is literally true but underplays what the data show. BIC chose p = 5 at the cap for 3,218 of 3,220 series, so the order was never selected; and SI p. 34, "No atoms were computed at p = 10 or 20, as the entry specified", leaves untested the one series that is actually white (p = 20, r₁ = 0.0002). Compute the atoms there. Also state the obvious remedy the paper cannot test with released derivatives: not band-pass filtering (or whitening before filtering).

MINOR 5. Motion. p. 7, Table 2: FD residualisation moves the DiD from −0.0809 to −0.0649 (−20 %) and the r₁ DiD from −0.0146 to −0.0123 (SI p. 15); the FD DiD is +0.0143 in the direction that lowers r₁. The regression is one slope per subject × run on 14 points, which also removes any drug effect coincident with the FD rise, so the residualised value is a lower bound of sorts; the paper reports the numbers but never discusses that a fifth of the contrast is linearly tied to motion. Add a sentence.

MINOR 6. Sidedness. p. 18, ll. 407–408, "an exact sign-flip permutation over the 14 subjects (16,384 assignments, two-sided)"; SI p. 4, "a directional test"; the record (analysis_record.md, 13 Sep entry) fixes "two-sided p" and the directional-failure rule after the 115-region fit had shown the decrease. State explicitly in Methods that the two-sided reading was itself fixed with the sign known; "the significant decrease refutes that hypothesis" (p. 7, l. 137) should say that the pre-registered increase failed regardless of the two-sided p.

MINOR 7. Drug order. SI p. 3, "Drug order was counterbalanced … half the participants receiving placebo first"; order is used nowhere. With 40 % of the DiD carried by a placebo-run drift (p. 7, l. 149), a session-order effect would bias the DiD; report the DiD by order group or state that order is not in the release and ask the data authors (two are co-authors).

MINOR 8. The exploratory disclaimer is applied inconsistently. p. 18, ll. 418–420, "a p value quoted for an exploratory quantity describes the sample and is not a claim", yet claims are drawn from exploratory p's: p. 8, l. 177 (network structure, p = 0.0009); p. 12, ll. 227–228, "(the in-band spectral centroid rises by +0.0023 Hz, p = 0.022)"; Author summary l. 38, "Under DMT the autocorrelation of the signals fell" (a post hoc quantity, p = 0.0106, phase p = 0.073, 16 % smaller after FD residualisation). Either qualify these sentences or drop the blanket disclaimer.

MINOR 9. OLS and Fieller assumptions are not stated (p. 18, ll. 412–413, "slopes across subjects t intervals on 12 degrees of freedom"); the Fieller interval [4.45, 10.54] has g = 0.61 (the denominator is only 2.8 SE from zero), which is why it is asymmetric — say so; and no leave-one-out is given for either slope although subjects 8 and 14 dominate the x-range (MAJOR 3).

MINOR 10. Reproducibility gap: p. 14, ll. 261–262, the deconvolution result "(−0.0782, p = 0.0013)" rests on files that "carry git=nogit" and "The deconvolved series and the sandbox are not in the repository" (SI p. 6). A quoted result should be regenerable; add the sandbox or the deconvolved derivatives, or label the result as not regenerable in the main text.

MINOR 11. Table 1 vs text: p. 4, ll. 126–127, "every atom moved under DMT as its AR(1)-substituted value did (Fig 2)" is vacuous for rty, xtr and xty, whose substituted DiDs print as ∓0.0000; restrict the sentence to the atoms with non-zero substituted DiDs.

======================================================================
Overall assessment
======================================================================
The analytic core is correct and matters: on two AR(1) processes with correlated innovations and no lagged interaction, MMI-ΦID assigns essentially the whole TDMI to "synergy" (sts = 2S − C, rtr = C), so the atom is a monotone function of lag-1 autocorrelation and every fMRI contrast or map computed with it is exposed to r₁ differences; I reproduced every identity and the derivatives independently. The empirical section is competently and, by the standards of the field, unusually honestly reported — the sign-flip inference, inverted intervals and exact p-values are right, the history of the primary contrast is disclosed in the Abstract, and the provenance trail is complete. What is not at the level of a top journal is the inferential layer built on fourteen per-subject difference-in-differences: the primary statistic's between-subject variance is four-fifths pre-injection between-run difference, which inflates the headline effect relative to a baseline-adjusted estimate (−0.081 vs −0.054), turns the celebrated per-subject collinearity into largely a between-run state correlation, and makes the residual's per-subject slope — the one place the paper tries to say something beyond "the estimator tracks r₁" — hinge on two subjects and on a comparison with the generators that is not like-for-like, while the group-level test against the calibration is too underpowered to distinguish a residual 2–4 times its expectation. The single greatest weakness is therefore that the data cannot test the account at N = 14 with this design, and the paper's comparative statements about the residual and the shared variance (Abstract ll. 23–26, Results 4, Discussion) are stronger than the per-subject evidence supports; with the post-injection gap, a baseline-adjusted estimate, the leave-one-out and a matched generator slope added and the Abstract's per-subject sentence qualified, the paper would be statistically sound and honestly reported.
