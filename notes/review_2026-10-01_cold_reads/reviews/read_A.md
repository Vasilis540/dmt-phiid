REFEREE REPORT — "The Gaussian-MMI synergy atom of integrated information decomposition is mostly self-prediction: a closed form on the AR(1) family and a within-subject DMT fMRI application" (typeset from commit 24919ea; main PDF 27 pp., SI 133 pp.)

WHAT I CHECKED INDEPENDENTLY (own implementation of the MMI product-lattice Möbius inversion, not the repository's code; scratch files under scratchpad/coldA/)
- All sixteen atoms of Results 1 (p.3 L101–102) on the symmetric family, the identities sts − (xtx+yty) = rtr, rtr + sts = TDMI = 2S, str+stx+sty+sts = S, ΦR = rtr; 2,000 random (a,q): max deviation 1.6e-14.
- ∂sts/∂a = 6.0705 and ∂sts/∂q = −0.1892 at (0.85, 0.25); ∂rtr/∂a = 0.056; the stated per-step and per-SD figures (0.061, 0.019, 4.7, 1.4) follow.
- Unequal coefficients: at q = 0, sts = 2 min(Sx,Sy), rtr = 0, xtx = Sx, yty = Sy, str = rts = min(Sx,Sy) (exact); at mean a = 0.85, q = 0.25, str = min(xtx,yty), rts ≥ str, sts ≤ xtx+yty+rtr with equality only at d = 0, the excess turns negative between d = 0.0075 and 0.0080, and sts = 1.2285, 1.1993, 1.1711, 1.1176, 0.9988 at d = 0.01–0.10 (S3 Text §3, SI p.12).
- Fig 1c: with a = r₁ − cq and cross-lag r₁q + c(1−q²), sts = 1.2588, 1.2315, 1.2155, 1.2569, 1.3023, 1.4111 at c = 0, +0.02, +0.05, +0.10, −0.02, −0.05; minimum at c ≈ 0.05; the (c,q) → (−c,−q) invariance; the departure ≈ 0.94c; the (0.85,0) and (0.6,0.25) curves; the plotted range stays positive definite (the (0.85,0.25) matrix fails only below c ≈ −0.12, and the figure stops at −0.10). The shared-slow-component worked example (λ = 0.25, as = 0.95, an = 0.80 ⇒ a = 0.8375, q = 0.25, cross-lag 0.2375, d = 0.028125, equal to the coupled family at 0.83, c = 0.03) checks.
- The band-limit bound r₁ ≥ cos(2π·0.08·2) = 0.537 and the flat-band value 0.817 (p.14 L250–251); the effective sample size ≈ 18 (p.17 L378–380); ideal band-pass r₁ = 0.970 at TR 0.72 s and ∂sts/∂r₁ = 32.8 there (p.15 L311).
- Windowed-estimator simulation on AR(1) pairs at W = 60: pair r₁ 0.786 at a = 0.85, level ≈ 0.72, residual (observed − AR(1)-substituted) ≈ −0.065, residual DiD ≈ +0.005 for Δa = −0.015 (paper: 0.786, 0.715, +0.0049 ± 0.0017). The within-window regression of windowed sts on (pair r₁, |q|, |ax−ay|) on pure AR(1) pairs gives a |q| slope 2.6 times that of the substituted estimate at the same measured values, which supports the sentence at p.4 L119–121.
- Not verified: the CCS and binarised computations (S3 §2, §11, S18), anything about the content of cited papers, and every number computed on the data.

MAJOR

M1. The headline comparison of the residual with the "pure autocorrelation" expectations rests on a calibration the paper itself finds invalid for this dataset, and the abstract does not say so. Abstract, p.1 L24–26: "sts falls by 0.0115 more than observed, against 0.003–0.005 in simulated pure autocorrelation changes (p = 0.11–0.25); the difference cannot be attributed on these data." Discussion, p.15 L289–290: "is not distinguished from the 0.003–0.005 of a simulated pure autocorrelation change". But Results 4, p.8 L204–205 and p.12 L206–211: "the data's residual DiD scales with the r₁ DiD at −0.75 per unit (OLS slope, t interval [−1.13, −0.37]; Fig 3c), an interval that excludes the band-passed rate" and "Since the generators apply one change to every subject and the band-passed rate lies outside the data's slope interval, the calibration is not validated for the residual on this dataset." Three problems: (a) the exact sign-flip p values test fourteen subject DiDs against a single constant (one generator even "from a single simulation, without a replicate SD", p.19 L433), ignoring both the generators' replicate uncertainty and the subjects' spread in Δr₁ (−0.064 to +0.024), although the residual's expectation under the paper's own account is proportional to Δr₁; the per-subject slope comparison is the right test, and it rejects the generator the paper says is in the data's regime; (b) the residual DiD is concentrated in the early post-injection windows (p.8 L195–196: "larger in the early post-injection windows (+0.0179) than in the late (+0.0064)"), where the drug response ramps and decays inside 60-TR windows, yet every generator is stationary (p.19 L430–433; SI p.27 "The null is stationary by construction"); a within-window non-stationarity control (injection ramp in r₁ and variance inside the window, or a semi-synthetic version in which a known change is written into the real placebo series) is the missing control that would make "pure autocorrelation change" mean the change these data underwent; (c) the abstract and Discussion should state the per-subject scaling result (twice the generators' rate, band-passed rate excluded) beside, or instead of, the group-level p values. The conclusion "not identified" is honest; the numbers chosen to summarise it are not the ones that carry the information.

M2. The manuscript is not in a reviewable final state. p.1 L6: "Christopher Timmermann² [TK], S. P. Singleton³ [TK], [TK further co-authors nominated by C.T.]"; p.17 L362–363 "[TK: the REC reference number…]"; p.19 L462: "no person other than V.S. has yet checked the analysis."; p.20 L483: "Manuscript for co-author review; not for citation or distribution."; p.20 L514 "[TK: the DOI of the archived version…]". Before submission the listed co-authors must have checked the analysis, and every TK must be resolved; the journal will not accept a paper in which the data authors have not confirmed the ethics basis for secondary use (p.17 L362–363).

MINOR

m1. The mechanism of the closed form is never stated, and the main text gives no derivation. p.3 L100–102 and p.18 L395–396 ("its block determinants and the Möbius inversion of Mediano et al. (2021) give the atoms of Results 1, checked to 4 × 10⁻¹⁵ (S3 Text)") assert the result; S3 §3 (SI p.11) gives the cumulative values in two sentences. Move those two sentences into Methods and add the one sentence that explains the result: at the joint-target node, I_∩({1}{2}→{12}) = min(I(x;x′y′), I(y;x′y′)) = min(Sx,Sy) even at q = 0, where x and y are independent — MMI counts equal amounts as redundant information — so the inversion must put S − C at rts and str, balance it with the four negative mirror atoms, and sts receives 2S − C; with unequal coefficients the min is what makes sts "follow the less self-predictive member". This connects the paper to the known MMI caveat (which the paper cites Barrett 2015 for only in the Limitations, p.16 L340–343) and makes clear that the property is specific to the joint-target MMI choice, which is exactly why CCS behaves differently (Results 6).

m2. The exchange rate for lagged coupling — the one practitioners need — is absent from the main text. p.4 L122–124 gives only "Lagged interaction moves sts non-monotonically … coupling c of the same sign as q lowers the atom called synergy". State the rate from S3 §3 (SI p.12): "sts changes with c at fixed (r₁, q) with slope −1.74 nats per unit c", i.e. 0.01 of r₁ is worth about 0.035 of symmetric coupling at the operating point; and state the pair-averaged population response, which is second order because "the first-order response of sts to c is odd in q … so the first-order term averages out" (SI p.30 L1543–1546): +0.009 nats for c = +0.02 at fixed (r₁, q) (Table 3, p.13, population column). That is the strongest form of the paper's claim — the whole-brain mean atom is nearly insensitive to small symmetric coupling at this operating point — and it is only in the SI.

m3. "Non-monotonically" is overgeneralised. p.1 L18: "while lagged coupling moves it non-monotonically"; p.15 L281–282 "lagged interaction, the thing the atom is meant to capture, moves it non-monotonically". Verified: the non-monotone response is at (0.85, 0.25) for c of the same sign as q; for the opposite sign it is monotone, and at (0.6, 0.25) it is monotone over the whole plotted range (Fig 1c, blue). Write "can move it non-monotonically" or "at the operating point".

m4. Prewhitening is presented one-sidedly. p.14 L249: "Prewhitening cannot fully remove the dependence from band-passed data."; p.16 L329–330: "Do not expect prewhitening to remove the dependence from band-passed data". The paper's own numbers (p.14 L253–255) show AR(p) prewhitening took the level from 1.155 to 0.22 and the contrast from −0.081 to −0.026 (p = 0.14); at r₁ = 0.26 the family's ∂sts/∂r₁ is 2a/(1−a²) ≈ 0.56, a tenth of 6.07, and the remaining contrast still tracks the whitened r₁ (SI p.33: "its DiD −0.0544"; "r(MMI sts DiD, whitened autocorrelation DiD) = +0.495"). The accurate statement is that prewhitening reduced the exposure by an order of magnitude, that what remains is again an r₁ contrast, and that the costs are the ones listed; the recommendation should say that, not discourage the one remedy that worked best in these data. Separately, p.14 L256–258 "a mutual-information test's false-positive rate rose from 42 % to over 88 % after AR(p) prewhitening (Cliff et al., 2021)": I cannot check the source; the figure is counter-intuitive enough that the authors should confirm the exact condition and numbers it refers to.

m5. CCS: the negative synergy atom is reported without comment. p.12 L231: "Under CCS whole-brain sts is −0.048 nats (MMI 1.155)"; on the family CCS-sts is −0.030 to −0.036 (SI p.112–113). The recommendation "check CCS contrasts against r₁ too" (p.16 L330) should be accompanied by the statement that CCS-ΦID's synergy atom is negative on the family and in the data and is not offered as a replacement. Also, the one lead for an independent dataset — CCS-sts rose under DMT (+0.0197, p = 0.0002, 13/14 at the global fit, p.12 L240–241) and its per-subject DiD correlates with the residual DiD at r = 0.80 (partial 0.83) with an undetermined split-half test (SI p.28) — is only in the SI; one sentence in the Discussion stating it as a hypothesis for a pre-specified test elsewhere would serve readers without claiming anything.

m6. The scope statement about discrete estimators is in the SI only. p.16 L339–340: "Two redundancy functions were examined on the data, both Gaussian." S3 §11 (SI p.38) shows binarised plug-in MMI-sts "rises with r₁ at every step" with "∂(MMI-sts)/∂r₁ = +1.815 and ∂(MMI-sts)/∂q = −0.150", per-SD ratio 1.76, and that for the binarised CCS emergence capacity of Luppi et al. (2023) the rates are comparable ("∂/∂r₁ = +0.124 and ∂/∂q = +0.142"). Summarise this in the main text (Results 6 or Limitations); the title's "Gaussian-MMI" otherwise reads as if the discrete estimator were exempt.

m7. Fairness to Luppi et al. (2022): the main text leaves implicit what the SI states. p.15–16 L313–316: "That study's phase-randomised surrogates … are the table's one test of the exposure: the surrogate gradient's correlations with the six macroscale maps of their Table 1 were small (|ρ| ≤ 0.26; one significant) against 0.22–0.54 for the observed gradient". S3 §10 (SI p.37) says plainly this is "evidence against the exposure for those associations, and the only such evidence in the table", and S20 row 1 (SI p.127) "That argues against the exposure for those macroscale associations on that dataset". Say it in the main text.

m8. "Scope map" names something that has no scope. Fig 1 (p.5) is titled "The scope map"; p.15 L305 "For every study within the map's scope"; but S3 §4 (SI p.13): "The family therefore has no region in which q contributes comparably, and the fact that all of a subject's pairs fall in the r₁-dominated region carries no information about the pairs." The map locates the operating point; the "scope" is the literature table's criteria (TR, estimator, deconvolution). Rename the figure and define scope once.

m9. Regional slope explanation is loose and mixes estimators. p.8 L171–173: "a region's r₁ is one member of each of its pairs, and a pair's sts follows its less autocorrelated member". On the family sts ≈ 2 min(Sx,Sy) − C, so the one-member derivative is ≈ 2∂S/∂a (≈ 6.1) for the less autocorrelated member and ≈ 0 for the more; the regional slope therefore depends on where a region sits relative to its partners, and "about half" is a statement about that distribution, not a property of the family. Also the regional sts is the global-fit value while regional r₁ is windowed (Fig 4 caption, p.10); report the same-estimator version (the per-subject slope 2.645, SI p.15) beside it.

m10. A lagged-interaction companion is missing. On the family and at every AR(1)-substituted matrix the Gaussian transfer entropies I(x;y′|y) and I(y;x′|x) are exactly zero (I checked for unequal coefficients), and they are computable from the same window's 4 × 4 matrix (Granger causality for Gaussians: Barnett, Barrett & Seth 2009). Reporting their DiD beside sts would give readers the standard lagged-interaction measure that does not inherit self-information, and would complement the directed/symmetric decomposition of S3 §6. Related: Table 1 (p.4) shows the TDMI residual DiD is +0.0092 against +0.0115 for sts, i.e. most of the sts residual DiD is a residual in the total lagged information; worth one sentence. For the filtering/deconvolution discussion in Results 7, Barnett & Seth (2011, J Neurosci Methods, Granger causality under filtering) and Seth, Chorley & Barnett (2013, NeuroImage, invariance to haemodynamic convolution but not downsampling) are the directly relevant prior results on lagged measures of BOLD; and for "emergence capacity" (S3 §11, S20) the defining paper is Rosas et al. (2020, PLOS Comput Biol, "Reconciling emergences"), which is not cited.

m11. p.4 L119–121: "the |q| slope being 2.8 times the population rate, consistent with a finite-sample bias that grows with |q|." My simulation on pure AR(1) pairs at W = 60 reproduces a ratio of 2.6 between the windowed sts and the substituted estimate, so the sentence is right, but the paper offers no check; one line citing a simulation would turn "consistent with" into a shown fact.

m12. Literature search is not reproducible as described. p.19 L442–444: "identified on 14 September 2026 by web searches run from a session of the AI system (the queries were not recorded)". The journal will want databases, strings and dates; at minimum state the limitation in the main text and supplement with a conventional database search.

m13. Provenance detail overwhelms the main text. "Data and code availability" (p.20–21 L482–546) runs two pages of commit hashes, wall-clock minutes and rounding anecdotes ("475 min wall-clock, rounded down to the minute"; the two CCS agreement-share arrays). Condense to the repository link, archived DOI and one paragraph; the rest belongs in S5 Text.

m14. p.14 L269–271: "Null-model normalisation of atoms is a direction the ΦID authors have named …; on the family the dependence it addresses is r₁'s route into sts, since TDMI = 2S depends on r₁ alone." The second clause is obscure (sts/TDMI = 1 − C/2S still depends on r₁ for q ≠ 0). Say what normalisation is meant and what it would and would not remove.

WORDING

w1. p.12 L222 "A longer lag reduces everything, the artefact included" and Fig 6 caption (p.14): "artefact" is used without definition and presupposes the conclusion; the paper elsewhere says carefully that the atom measures what it measures ("bears on MMI-sts, not on synergy in the intended sense", p.7 L138). Define or avoid.

w2. p.4 L125–127: "every atom moved under DMT as its AR(1)-substituted value did (Fig 2)". For the six cross-prediction atoms the substituted DiDs are 0.0000–0.0003 (Table 1), so "moved as" is not meaningful there; restrict the sentence to the ten atoms with non-trivial substituted DiDs.

w3. p.1 L19–22 (abstract) and p.7 L133–139: "the primary contrast … was planned to test an increase; its statistic was fixed after global fits on the same data had shown a decrease." The disclosure is exemplary, but the words "pre-specified" and "primary contrast" with "p = 0.004" in the abstract still read as a planned test; Limitations (p.16 L337–338) already calls it "a proof of concept" — the abstract should use that register.

w4. p.1 L17: "4.7 to 1 per standard deviation across pairs" — the ratio compares r₁ with lag-0 correlation q, neither of which is lagged interaction; a reader may take it as "autocorrelation outweighs interaction". One qualifying clause ("neither input is lagged coupling; see Fig 1c") would prevent that.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT
The mathematics is correct: I reproduced every closed-form statement, identity, derivative, unequal-coefficient claim and coupled-family value from an independent implementation, and the finite-sample behaviour of the windowed estimator that the residual construction relies on. The closed form itself is elementary once the MMI cumulative values are written down, and its substance — that MMI counts equal amounts as redundant, so two independent autocorrelated processes receive sts = 2S — is the known MMI weakness that Varley (2024) already stated at the aggregate level; the paper's real contributions are the atom-level form, the exchange rates (r₁ against q, asymmetry and coupling), the lagged-coupling and shared-component analyses, the exposure-per-spectral-difference result, and a careful demonstration on real BOLD data that the level, the drug contrast and the regional map of MMI-sts are reproduced by each pair's (ax, ay, q), with an honest, if over-long, account of what the remainder cannot tell. The literature treatment is fair and conditional throughout. The paper is a sound and useful contribution that the journal could accept after the minor points, with M1 addressed (the abstract and Discussion re-centred on the per-subject scaling result and a non-stationary or semi-synthetic control added) and M2 resolved. Its single greatest weakness is that the apparatus built to quantify the remainder — the residual, its three generators, Table 3, S9–S10, S18 — ends in "not identified": the paper can show that autocorrelation carries the whole contrast and more, but it cannot bound how much of an MMI-sts contrast in real data is not autocorrelation, which is the question every practitioner will bring to it, and the length devoted to that undetermined question obscures the clear and valuable result.
