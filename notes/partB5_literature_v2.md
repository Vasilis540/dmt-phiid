Pre-specified in `notes/partB_prespec_2026-09-14.md` (B5): a table, no critique of any paper. Scope as written there: fMRI work reporting Gaussian-MMI ΦID synergy atoms, or synergy-minus-redundancy derived from them, on BOLD time series without HRF deconvolution at TR ≈ 1–3 s; for each, the quantity reported, the preprocessing, the parcellation, whether autocorrelation was addressed, and whether the reported effect is one the scope map (B1) would predict from an autocorrelation change. Studies that deconvolve are listed too and marked, because the repository's own deconvolution result (review item 3) bears on whether the map still applies to them. ## Table B. Inputs for the last column (from this repository's computations and the sources read)

1. Atom signature of an r₁ change versus a q change on the bivariate AR(1) family (closed form, B1 machinery; symmetric a_x = a_y). At (r₁, q) = (0.85, 0.25): ∂/∂r₁ — sts +6.07, xtx + yty +6.02, TDMI +6.13, rtr +0.06, rts + str +6.02, mirror atoms −12.0; ∂/∂q — sts −0.19, rtr +0.19, xtx + yty −0.38, TDMI 0.00, mirrors +0.76. At (0.85, 0.5): ∂sts/∂r₁ +5.87, ∂rtr/∂r₁ +0.26; ∂sts/∂q −0.44, ∂rtr/∂q +0.44. Exact properties on this family (checked to 4e-15 over 2,000 seeded draws of (a, q)): TDMI = −ln(1 − a²) independent of q; rtr + sts = TDMI, hence ∂rtr/∂q = −∂sts/∂q; ΦR ≡ rtr = −½ ln(1 − a² q²) (the MMI double redundancy is the cross-lag MI, the smallest of the four single MIs). So: an r₁ change moves sts, the self-prediction atoms and TDMI together and leaves rtr and ΦR nearly unchanged; a q change moves sts and rtr in opposite directions by equal amounts, leaves TDMI unchanged, and moves ΦR. For unequal a_x ≠ a_y, ΦR − rtr is not zero (2,000 draws a_x, a_y ~ U(0, 0.95), q ~ U(−0.9, 0.9), seed 20261120: −1.36 to +4.59, |ΦR − rtr| > 0.01 in 57 %), so the ΦR identity is the symmetric case only. Script `notes/partB5_family_checks.py`, log `notes/review_results/partB/family_checks.log`. At q = 0 with unequal coefficients sts = 2 min(S_x, S_y) and rtr = 0 exactly (closed form, `rev_phiid_fast.atoms_from_corr(ar1_corr(a_x, a_y, 0))`, re-checked on 20 Sep 2026 at (0.83, 0.87), (0.85, 0.85), (0.80, 0.90) and (0.95, 0.60): equality to machine precision; the value used for row 1's surrogate reading; logged by `partB19_exchange_rates.py`).

2. Operating point implied by the preprocessing: lag-1 autocorrelation of ideal band-passed white noise, r₁ = [sin(2π f_hi TR) − sin(2π f_lo TR)] / [2π (f_hi − f_lo) TR]: 0.008–0.09 Hz at TR 2 s → 0.78; at TR 1.838 s → 0.81; at TR 0.72 s → 0.97; 0.0025–0.05 Hz at TR 2 s → 0.93; at TR 2.4 s → 0.90; at TR 2.6 s → 0.89; at TR 3 s → 0.85; at TR 1.25 s → 0.97; 0.01–0.1 Hz at TR 2 s → 0.73, at TR 1.0 s → 0.93, at TR 1.2 s → 0.90; this repository's data (0.01–0.08 Hz, TR 2 s) → 0.82, against a measured 0.866. Real BOLD has its power at the low end of the band, so the measured value sits above the ideal one (deconvolution moves it below: 0.790 here). Every row but row 8 now states its band and TR; for row 8 the band is not stated. At |q| = 0.25 the ratio |∂sts/∂r₁| / |∂sts/∂q| is 25 (r₁ 0.78), 32 (0.85), 60 (0.93), 131 (0.97); at |q| = 0.6 it is 8, 10, 18, 38 (`family_checks.log`) — r₁-dominated throughout in the per-unit sense of the map; the review of 20 Sep 2026 notes that per unit of the data's own variation the ratio is 2–5 (`notes/review_2026-09-20/review_verification_2026-09-20.md`, item 3), which the manuscript's round-14 revision states in data units.

3. Direction of the autocorrelation change in the conditions studied, from the sources read: Huang et al. 2018, *J Neurosci* 38:2304 (TR 2 s; propofol sedation n = 23, deep anaesthesia n = 12, DoC n = 21) — "a graded global increase of AC1 from wakefulness to light and deep sedation, followed by a decrease during transition and return to baseline in recovery"; "all of these parameters decreased in deep anesthesia and in patients with DOC" (both quotations confirmed against the PDF on 20 Sep 2026). Luppi et al. 2025 bioRxiv 2025.03.22.644729 (hctsa on human sevoflurane n = 15 TR 1.838 s, macaque, marmoset, mouse) — anaesthesia "reduces the value of features related to linear and nonlinear temporal autocorrelation", "faster decay of autocorrelation under anaesthesia". For AD, schizophrenia, ASD, ADHD and TUS no source was read here; the intrinsic-timescale literature on these conditions exists but was not consulted, so those cells say "not determined here".

4. HRF deconvolution and the map (this repository, review item 3, rsHRF / Wu et al. 2013): the three deconvolving Luppi studies (rows 1–3) all name the Wu et al. 2013 method (rsHRF v2.2 in rows 1 and 3); Tarchi et al. 2026 (row 9) names "standard SPM methods". On this repository's data deconvolution takes the run-level r₁ from 0.866 to 0.790 (ts_gsr), removes ~30 % of the sts level, leaves sts ≈ xtx + yty (0.924 vs 0.968 at the global fit), leaves the sts contrast unchanged (−0.0782 vs −0.0809 at W = 60) and makes the autocorrelation contrast larger (−0.0220 vs −0.0146). On these data, therefore, deconvolved series remain in the r₁-dominated region and the map's reading applies to them as to the raw series; whether that holds for the deconvolved series of rows 1–3 and 9 is not determined, and Luppi et al. 2022 report "negligible effects" of the step at TR 0.72 s.
