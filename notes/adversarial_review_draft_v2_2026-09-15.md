# Adversarial review of `manuscript/draft_v2.md` (15 Sep 2026)

Scope: `draft_v2.md`, `supplementary.md`, `prespecification_summary.md` and `figures/` read as one document; every claim checked against `results/*.csv`, `manuscript/analysis_record.md`, the `notes/` Part B notes and `notes/review_results/` (tables, logs, inference rows, per-subject `.pkl` DiDs, the diagnostic `.npz`), the git history (public repository at `6700f87`, plus the three incremental bundles), and the literature reachable from this session. `draft.md` was read only to check draft_v2's account of it. Two computations of my own are appended (per-subject correlations from the committed `.pkl` files; a null simulation of the B4 residual, `notes/review_v2_residual_null.py`, seeded, ≈ 4 min). Nothing in the folder was rewritten.

**Verdict first.** The numbers are clean: every value I could trace in draft_v2, Tables 1–5 and the supplement matches its source file, with one wrong sentence in the S2 pointer (section 2). The closed form is right (I re-derived it and checked it numerically against the lattice inversion). The paper's weaknesses are in what it makes of the numbers, in three places: (i) the central methods claim is presented as established by the DMT demonstration, but the pre-specified demonstration criterion (a residual DiD near zero) failed, the paper's own diagnostic finds a significant autocorrelation-independent remainder in the direction of the original hypothesis, and the paper attributes that remainder to model misfit on correlational evidence that cannot carry the attribution (my null simulation accounts for about half of it); (ii) the CCS comparison, on which the "MMI-specific" claim rests, verifies phyid's CCS against Ince's single-target rule but not against the published ΦID double-redundancy definition, which is retrievable (arXiv 2109.13186, Appendix, Definition 1) and differs from the code in the fifth sign condition, so every CCS number in the paper is about phyid's variant; (iii) the "scope map" result that "no q-dominated region exists" and "100 % of real pairs are r₁-dominated" is a property of a family constructed without lagged interaction and is presented as an empirical finding. The analytic identity itself is new at the atom level, but its coarse form (MMI synergy of two independent autocorrelated processes equals one process's self-information) is in Varley 2024, Eq. 12, in closed form, and the Introduction undersells that. The paper is also silent on a per-subject fact it could have computed from its own files: the diagnostic residual and the CCS-sts change correlate at r = 0.73 (ts_gsr) and 0.84 (ts_demean) across subjects, and the relation survives partialling out the autocorrelation change. Either both are estimation noise shared across the same windows or both are the same signal; the paper needs to find out before it can call the residual "model misfit" and the CCS change "not evidence of anything".

## 1. Claims not supported by the numbers in `results/` and `notes/review_results/`

Ordered by how much of the paper each one takes down.

**1.1 "A post-hoc supplement … locates the whole residual in the diagnostic's single model assumption, corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) = a_y q" (Results 4).** The supplement's evidence is `residual_source.log`: the cross-lag substitution alone reproduces the level residual (−0.0489), the deviations have "mean signed +0.00014", and "Joint regression of the cell residual on (pair a, pair |q|, cross-lag deviation SD), standardised: coefficients −0.743, −0.437, −0.262; partial correlations −0.629, −0.601, −0.296; R² 0.744". R² = 0.74 is not "the whole"; and a regression of the residual on a and |q| cannot distinguish "finite-sample over-prediction that shrinks as a and |q| fall" from "a real component that scales with the size of the drug effect", because both hypotheses predict the same correlations across cells and across subjects. The test that can distinguish them — the expected residual under a null in which the population cross-lag correlation is exactly a_y q — was not run. I ran it (Appendix B): with the data's own ACF shape (lags 1–6 matched to 0.868, 0.539, 0.172, −0.085, −0.174, −0.145), per-pair heterogeneity in a (SD 0.03) and q, and the window-level operating points of `residual_source.log` (pair a 0.8632 → 0.8532 on DMT, 0.8603 → 0.8657 on placebo; |q| 0.2842 → 0.2662 and 0.2839 → 0.2824), the null gives a residual level of −0.035 (−3.0 %) against the observed −0.0489 (−4.3 %), and a residual DiD of +0.0054 against the observed +0.0115 [+0.0021, +0.0211]. The null reproduces the DMT-run change (+0.0041 vs +0.0077) and not the placebo-run change (−0.0013 vs −0.0038). Scaling with window length tells the same story: null −8.5 % / −3.1 % / −0.34 % at W = 30 / 60 / 840 against the data's −9.7 % / −4.3 % / −1.1 %; the run-level residual is three times what sampling scatter produces, i.e. there is a systematic, N-independent departure from the cross-lag substitution of roughly −0.01 nats even though the deviations have zero mean sign. So: the direction and about half of the residual DiD are what the model assumption produces; the other half is not located anywhere. The Abstract and Discussion say "unresolved"; Results 4 says "locates the whole residual". Only the first is supported.

**1.2 "per subject it is uncorrelated with the autocorrelation contrast in every cell (r = −0.439, −0.253, −0.390, −0.207; all p ≥ 0.117)" (Results 3), and the derived claim "CCS-sts does not carry the autocorrelation contrast".** At N = 14, r = −0.44 has a 95 % interval of about [−0.78, +0.12]; "p ≥ 0.117" is a failure to reject, not "uncorrelated", and all four cells carry the same negative sign. The per-pair evidence (`ccs_tables.md`: CCS sts vs pair r₁ r = −0.011 and +0.003) is the strong part of the argument and should carry it; the per-subject line as written over-reads a null at N = 14, in the same paragraph that treats r = 0.953 as identity.

**1.3 "The sts contrast and the autocorrelation contrast are the same contrast twice" (Results 3).** r = 0.953 leaves 9 % of the per-subject variance unexplained, the paper's own diagnostic finds that remainder significant (Table 3: +0.0115, p = 0.042, phase p = 0.019; early set +0.0179, p = 0.0012), and the two contrasts behave differently under the paper's own temporal null (`inference_rows_raw.csv`: sts phase p = 0.0020 at 3.77 null SD; autocorrelation phase p = 0.0729 at 1.76 SD, primary cell). The paper reports the 0.073 and never reconciles it with "the same contrast twice".

**1.4 "Real pairs sit in the steep part: for subject 1 … 100 % of the 6,555 pairs fall in the r₁-dominated region in all 20 cells" (Results 2), and "the pairs are r-dominated at every τ (100, 100, 97.7, 99.5 %)" (Results 5).** On the family the r₁-dominated region is the entire plane except the line r₁ = 0 (`scope_map_tables.md`: "q-dominated r1 range: none" for every q; `lag_tables.md`: 0 of 191 × 191 cells). A statement that 100 % of measured pairs fall in a region that covers every cell of the grid is true by construction and carries no information about the pairs; presenting it as an overlay result ("where real pairs sit") is rhetoric. What the overlay does establish — the pairs' operating point (r₁ ≈ 0.85, |q| ≈ 0.25) and the derivative ratio there (32) — is the real content and should be stated as such.

**1.5 "we established what the code computes … An independent Gaussian implementation of Ince's construction agreed with phyid to 2.1 × 10⁻¹³" (Methods) together with "The published definition of the double-redundancy step (Mediano et al., 2025) could not be retrieved during this work" (Methods) and "its double-redundancy step has no published text we could check" (Discussion).** The published definition exists and is retrievable: arXiv 2109.13186 (v1, 28 Sep 2021, the preprint of the PNAS paper), Appendix, Definition 1: "the double-redundancy based on common change in surprisal is defined as I_∂,CCS^{{1}{2}→{1}{2}} := Σ_{i∈S} c(x^(i); y^(i))", with "the set S ⊆ {1, …, M} as the subset of samples for which all marginal pointwise mutual informations, as well as the pointwise full mutual information i(x; y), have the same sign", c being the pointwise co-information summed over the product lattice "excluding the lowest node". phyid's code (`calculate.py`: `rtr_input_list = [I_xta, I_xtb, I_yta, I_ytb, double_coinfo]`; `measures.double_redundacy_ccs` returns `mi_lst[-1]` where all listed signs agree) keeps D at the samples where the four single-source, single-target MIs and D itself share a sign. The published rule uses the sign of the full pointwise TDMI i(x; y) as the fifth condition; the code uses the sign of D. These are different masks, and the paper's Methods describe the code's mask ("five local signs (the four single-source, single-target mutual informations and D itself)") without noticing the discrepancy. Consequences: (a) the CCS atoms in Tables 1 and 4, the "MMI-specific" verdict and the B2 outcome are all about phyid's variant of ΦID-CCS, not the published one; (b) "verified its implementation independently" (Abstract) verified that the code does what the code does. Whether the PNAS 2025 text retains the arXiv definition I could not check (PNAS returned 403 here too); the arXiv appendix alone is enough to make the paper's "no published text" wrong and to require a rerun with the published mask, which is a one-line change.

**1.6 "sts (+1.1554 nats) is within 8 % of xtx + yty" (Results 1).** Within 8 %, but on the wrong side. The family gives sts − (xtx + yty) = rtr > 0 (Methods: "Hence sts − (xtx + yty) = C = rtr, exact at q = 0 and growing with |q|"); Table 1 gives 1.1554 − (0.6273 + 0.6125) = −0.084 against rtr = +0.039, and the global fit gives 1.3085 − 1.3616 = −0.053 (`lag_tables.md`, τ = 1). The data violate the sign of the identity the paper is built on, by 0.12 nats at W = 60; the diagnostic's −4.3 % over-prediction is the same fact seen from the other side. The paper never says so.

**1.7 "the intensity ratings … enter no result reported here" (Methods, Dataset).** Figure 1 plots them ("The dotted line (right axis) is the group-mean DMT intensity rating per 60-s bin", `figures/captions.md`), and Supplementary Table S2 is built on them. If "here" means the main text, the sentence must say so and Figure 1 must lose the overlay.

**1.8 "The DMT dataset demonstrates this" (Abstract, Conclusions).** The demonstration criterion was fixed in `notes/partB_prespec_2026-09-14.md`: "on these data the residual DiD is expected near zero, which would be the demonstration that the observed synergy change is accounted for by autocorrelation change." It was not met (Results 4: "Neither recorded outcome obtained as worded"). What the data show is that the autocorrelation change predicts the sign and 114 % of the magnitude, with a significant remainder of the opposite sign. That is a strong, specific finding, and it is not "demonstrates".

**1.9 "The property is MMI-specific under the rule recorded before the CCS computation" (Discussion).** One alternative redundancy function was examined, and it is phyid's variant (1.5). "Not shared by phyid's CCS" is what the data support. I_min (Williams & Beer), I_BROJA, the dependency-based Gaussian PID (Kay & Ince) and the information-geometric Gaussian PID were not examined, and any of them could share or not share the property.

**1.10 "DMT changed r₁ and barely moved q on the map's scale" (Discussion).** True on `ts_gsr` (pair |q| DiD −0.0164 → 0.003 nats). On `ts_demean` the paper's own Results 2 reports the signed mean r rising by +0.0526 [+0.0073, +0.0976] (28 % of its baseline, `global_fc_did_ts_demean.csv`); through ∂sts/∂q ≈ −0.2 to −0.4 at |q| ≈ 0.3 that is of the order of −0.01 nats, 5–10 % of the ts_demean DiD, in the same direction as the sts decrease. Not negligible, not stated, and the |q| DiD on ts_demean is not reported at all.

**1.11 "What its synergy atom (sts) measures on strongly autocorrelated BOLD has not been established analytically" (Abstract) and "Varley (2024) showed on discrete toy systems and one human fMRI subject …" (Introduction).** See section 4: Varley's Eq. 12 is a general closed-form statement that the MMI synergy of two independent autocorrelated processes equals one process's self-information. The paper's atom-level identity is finer, but the Abstract's sentence and the "discrete toy systems" characterisation are not accurate.

## 4. Novelty

**What the paper claims is new** (Introduction): a closed-form account of sts over (r₁, q); where real pairs sit; a comparison with a redundancy function that lacks the property; a diagnostic.

**What is already documented.** Varley 2024 (arXiv 2407.16601) defines the MMI synergy of a bivariate process as I_Syn(X) = I(X_t; X_{t+1}) − max_i I(X^i_t; X_{t+1}) (his Eq. 3, the forward-PID synergy with the joint future as target, which in ΦID terms is str + stx + sty + sts) and shows, for a "disintegrated" twin X⊥ with the same first-order autocorrelations and X¹ ⊥ X², that I_Syn(X⊥) = I(X²_t; X²_{t+1}) (Eq. 12): the synergy of two independent autocorrelated processes is the self-information of one of them. The derivation is general (it uses only additivity of MI under independence and the MMI maximum), not discrete-only; the toy systems are discrete but Eq. 12 is not. He also shows Eq. 13 (the change on disintegration depends on I(X¹_t; X²_t), i.e. on q), an HCP check on 1,000 pairs (Δsynergy vs pairwise MI r = 0.97), names Luppi 2022/2023/2024, Gatica 2024 and Mediano 2022 as users of MMI-ΦID, and concludes that MMI "should probably not continue to be used in its current form". On the paper's own family at q = 0, Varley's identity reads str + stx + sty + sts = S = xtx, which the paper's atoms satisfy (2S − C + (S − C) − 2(S − C) = S). So the coarse statement — MMI synergy of autocorrelated Gaussian pairs is self-prediction — is prior art in closed form, and the Introduction's "on discrete toy systems and one human fMRI subject" undersells it. The Abstract's "has not been established analytically" is wrong for the aggregate synergy and right only for the sts atom.

Also relevant, not cited for this point: Barrett 2015 (the paper's own reference for MMI) already exhibits positive MMI-PID net synergy in dynamical Gaussian examples where the target's own lagged past is one source (Example 1, Eq. 73: WMS(X_t; X_{t−1}, Y_{t−1}) = ½ log(1 + α⁴) > 0 with Y white noise) and with two uncorrelated sources (Example 3, Eq. 111), i.e. MMI synergy from lagged self-terms is known since 2015 in the single-target setting; and Faes, Sparacino et al. 2025 (Partial Information Rate Decomposition, PRL / arXiv 2502.04550) state the general problem — PID applied to autocorrelated time series under an implicit i.i.d. assumption misreads the lagged structure — and propose rate-based decomposition as the remedy, citing Luppi 2022/2024 as examples of the practice. Neither says anything about the sts atom.

**What the paper adds beyond Varley**, as far as I can find: the sixteen-atom closed form on the AR(1) family, in particular sts = xtx + yty + rtr with the four negative atoms balancing the block; the derivatives and their ratio as a function of (r₁, q); the statement of the operating point of real pairs; the lag dependence; the per-pair diagnostic; and the demonstration on a within-subject drug contrast. None of these is in Varley 2024, Mediano et al. 2021 (whose worked example is a coupled AR pair, a = 0.4, plotting ΦWMS and ΦR against noise correlation), Barrett 2015, or the PIRD papers.

**What I searched.** Full texts (via the fetch tool): Varley 2024 (arXiv HTML); Mediano et al. 2021 (arXiv PDF, v1); Barrett 2015 (arXiv PDF); Faes et al. 2025 PIRD (arXiv HTML); Luppi et al. 2024 (eLife reviewed preprint). Web searches: ["minimum mutual information" + ΦID + autocorrelation + synergy], [ΦID "two independent" autoregressive "self-transfer"/"self-prediction" closed form], [PID Gaussian autoregressive synergy self-prediction MMI critique], [partial information rate decomposition Faes Sparacino], [Luppi 2022 supplementary autocorrelation control], [MMI negative atoms ΦID autocorrelated 2025]. Not readable from here: Mediano et al. 2025 PNAS (403), Varley 2023 PLOS One (rate-limited; it may contain a further statement on MMI in ΦID and should be read), PMC (captcha), the Luppi 2022 supplement. Not searched: Google Scholar full-text, GitHub issues on the ΦID toolboxes. The identity "sts = xtx + yty + rtr" as such I did not find anywhere.

## 5. Positive results in the folder that the paper does not make

**5.1 CCS-sts rises under DMT.** `inference_rows_ccs.csv`, primary set: ts_gsr global +0.0210 [+0.0154, +0.0270], sign-flip p = 0.0001, phase p = 0.0010, positive in 14/14, FD-residualised +0.0164 (p = 0.0001), both trend corrections +0.0242 (p = 0.0001), early +0.0199 and late +0.0219; ts_demean global +0.0321, 14/14, p = 0.0001, survives everything; ts_demean W60 +0.0134 [+0.0056, +0.0221], p = 0.0052, phase p = 0.0030, 11/14; ts_gsr W60 +0.0036 [−0.0001, +0.0072], p = 0.084, phase p = 0.016. This is, per subject, the most consistent effect in the folder — more consistent than the headline MMI contrast (12–13/14) — and its sign is the direction the study pre-specified for synergy. The paper says "positive in every cell (exploratory)", "no interpretation is attached", and "Whether its small positive DMT change means anything is a question these data do not answer".

**Correct treatment or evasion?** Both, in parts. What is defensible: `partB_prespec` bound the authors in advance ("Nothing in Part B is to be interpreted as a route back to a DMT-specific claim") and the closure entry bound them further ("No further specification will be run in search of a positive DMT result"); CCS-sts under phyid's construction is −D averaged over sign-disagreeing samples, a quantity without an established reading; and the global-fit cells, where the 14/14 lives, are local atoms under one run-level covariance and are exposed to the within-run variance change the review documented (window/run variance 1.28 pre → 0.89 post on DMT, `review_computations` section 6), which moves every local MI. What is evasive: (a) the reason recorded for discounting the sign ("the earlier CCS result was withdrawn on exactly that ground") was an implementation doubt that B2 then resolved, so the discount is inherited from a ground that no longer holds — while a new ground (section 1.5: the mask differs from the published definition) does hold and is not given; (b) the paper never says the CCS change is in the pre-specified direction of the original hypothesis; (c) it calls a 14/14, p = 0.0001, control-surviving effect "small"; (d) it reports no CCS scope map, so "does not carry the autocorrelation contrast" rests on per-pair correlations rather than on an analytic or simulated null; (e) it does not report that the per-subject CCS-sts DiD and the diagnostic residual DiD are correlated (Appendix A: r = +0.729, p = 0.003, ts_gsr W60; +0.838, p < 0.001, ts_demean W60; partial correlations controlling for the autocorrelation DiD +0.69 and +0.82) — the two quantities the paper declines to interpret are, per subject, largely one quantity; (f) "a question these data do not answer" is not true; the data answer parts of it (the global-vs-windowed disagreement, the early = late flatness that does not follow the drug's decay, the variance non-stationarity), and those parts are the reasons for caution that should be given in place of silence. The right treatment is an explicit exploratory paragraph: direction, consistency, controls survived, the three reasons it cannot be read (mask ≠ published definition; run-level fit exposed to variance change; no CCS null), and the rule that forbids a DMT claim — not "no interpretation is attached".

**5.2 The residual itself** (Table 3), early set +0.0179 [+0.0099, +0.0260], p = 0.0012, phase p = 0.003, positive in 10/14 on ts_gsr and 12/14 on ts_demean, and its shared variance with CCS-sts (above). The paper has a significant, pre-specified-test remainder in the direction of an sts increase after the autocorrelation change is removed, and reports it as "unresolved". Section 6.

**5.3 The edge-level statement.** `ccs_tables.md`: within a single window, pair-level MMI sts correlates with pair r₁ at +0.742 and +0.697 (subject 1). About half of the between-pair variance of sts in a window (r² = 0.55 and 0.49) is lag-1 autocorrelation. That is the strongest and simplest form of the paper's claim, it applies directly to every edge-level and regional synergy map in the literature, and it appears nowhere in the main text.

**5.4 The regional test of the spatial-map claim** could be run on the paper's own data (regional sts from `regional_atoms_bins_*` against a regional r₁ map, placebo run, 115 regions) and would turn the Discussion's conditional ("a regional autocorrelation gradient would produce…") into a result. It was not run.

The paper is right not to make the deconvolved-ΦR claim (closure entry) and right not to make a workspace claim from S4.

## 6. The residual diagnostic

**The attribution as written is hand-waving; the direction is right and about half the magnitude is accounted for.** Three of the four steps in `residual_source.log` are sound: the residual comes from the cross-lag entries (substitution decomposition), the deviations have no mean sign, and the residual is larger where a and |q| are larger. The fourth step — therefore "the positive residual DiD is that over-prediction shrinking as a and |q| fall under DMT" — does not follow from correlations, because (i) a genuine drug-related component that scales with drug effect would correlate with the a and |q| changes exactly as an artefact does (the per-subject r(residual DiD, autocorrelation DiD) = −0.78 is equally consistent with both), and (ii) the pre-specified discriminator ("uncorrelated with r₁") was itself unable to discriminate for the same reason. The quantitative test is a null simulation; the paper did not run one; mine (Appendix B, `notes/review_v2_residual_null.py`) gives a null residual DiD of +0.0054 against +0.0115 observed, a null level of −0.035 against −0.049, and a run-level null of −0.34 % against −1.1 %. The observed DiD's interval [+0.0021, +0.0211] contains the null value, so the honest statement is "compatible with finite-sample scatter, which accounts for roughly half the point estimate; the remainder is unexplained", not "locates the whole residual". Two further facts the paper omits: the residual's per-subject variance is shared with CCS-sts beyond what the autocorrelation change explains (Appendix A, partial r +0.69 / +0.82; R² of the residual DiD rises from 0.61 to 0.79 when CCS-sts is added to the autocorrelation DiD on ts_gsr), and the residual is time-locked to the drug (early p = 0.0012, late p = 0.32) — which, like everything else here, fits both readings, since the r₁ fall is also early-concentrated (−0.0213 early, −0.0093 late). The shared variance with CCS-sts needs to be reported and resolved (shared window noise vs shared signal — a split-window or odd/even-TR check would do it). One technicality: the phase-randomised null for the residual scrambles the observed local series against a fixed per-window prediction, so it is not a null of the residual's construction; say so or drop the phase p for that row.

## 7. Applicability and Supplement S3

**The distinction is drawn correctly in principle and applied timidly.** Both uses (between-state contrast; spatial map) follow from sts(r₁, q) with ∂sts/∂r₁ ≫ ∂sts/∂q, and the paper states both correctly. Three omissions. First, "between-state" should include between-group (AD, SZ, ASD, ADHD are group contrasts, exposed to any group difference in r₁ from physiology, motion, age or scanner) and within-session drift: the paper's own placebo run carries 34–40 % of its DiD through an r₁ drift (Results 3), so "whenever the manipulation changes r₁" understates the exposure to anything that changes r₁. Second, the edge-level case (5.3) is the most direct and is missing. Third, the spatial-map claim is testable on the paper's own data and was not tested (5.4).

## 9. The three criticisms most likely from a hostile reviewer at a Q1 journal

**9.1 "This is Varley 2024 with a derivative map."** The reviewer will cite Eq. 12, the r = 0.97 with pairwise MI, and Varley's list of affected papers, and add that the ΦID authors themselves moved to CCS and null-model normalisation (Liardi et al. 2024/2025, NuMIT — cited in draft.md, absent from draft_v2) precisely because of MMI's known pathologies. What the paper needs: an Introduction that states Varley's result in its general form and says exactly what is added (the atom-level identity, rtr as the excess, the map, the diagnostic, the between-state demonstration); a paragraph on null-model normalisation as the standard remedy and why the diagnostic is different from it (it is per-pair and analytic); and engagement with the PNAS 2025 text, which the paper cites as [TK].

**9.2 "Your family excludes the thing synergy is supposed to measure, and your own data show the remainder."** The AR(1) family has no lagged interaction by construction (the Markov property sets every cross-prediction atom to zero), so "no q-dominated region" and "100 % of pairs r₁-dominated" are properties of the construction (1.4); the data violate the identity's sign (1.6); the pre-specified demonstration failed (1.8); the residual is significant, time-locked to the drug, in the direction of the original hypothesis, shares variance with CCS-sts (5.1–5.2, 6), and CCS-sts rises in 14/14 at the global fit. The reviewer will say the paper found an autocorrelation-independent synergy increase and buried it under "exploratory". What the paper needs: a VAR(1) map with cross-coupling c (the record's own asymmetric family) showing what true lagged interaction does to sts and to the diagnostic; the null simulation of the residual; the residual–CCS relationship resolved; a CCS run with the published mask; and either an interpretation of the positive remainder or a stated rule for why none is offered.

**9.3 "The estimator, the design and the reproducibility chain do not support the numbers you lean on."** Fifty-nine samples per window of a process with integrated autocorrelation time ≈ 2.8 (≈ 20 effective samples per 4 × 4 fit); manufacture of ±16 % of the DiD at W = 60 from covariance change alone; 40 % of the primary DiD from a placebo drift; the autocorrelation contrast failing the temporal null on the primary cell (p = 0.073) while "the same contrast" passes at p = 0.002; N = 14; a CCS implementation that does not match the published definition; and every Part B number produced by scripts in `notes/` that `run_all.sh` does not run (it still reads "git submodule", still omits step 14, and `README.md` still calls `draft.md` "the paper"). What the paper needs: the global fit as primary for the between-state contrast (Results 6 makes that case and then does not adopt it), a run-level diagnostic where the manufacture is ≤ 2 %, an effective-sample statement, and a `run_all.sh` that regenerates draft_v2's tables.

A fourth the paper has pre-empted but will still hear: the conflict of interest (a critique of the estimator of the group the author is applying to, produced "as evidence of competence" per the public record's first paragraph), the AI authorship of the review and the analyses, and the same-day pre-specification of everything in Part B. The COI and AI-use statements are candid; the History should be equally candid about 3.6 and section 8.

## 13. Results stated as null or unsupported where the numbers justify more

1. **CCS-sts** (5.1): "positive in every cell (exploratory)" understates 14/14, p = 0.0001, phase p = 0.001, FD- and trend-surviving, on both variants at the global fit; the paper can and should say that, with the reasons it is not read.
