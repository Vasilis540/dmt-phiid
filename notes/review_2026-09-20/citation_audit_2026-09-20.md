5.3 Prior closed-form Gaussian decompositions (for the novelty framing).
- Faes, Marinazzo & Stramaglia (2017), Entropy 19(8), 408, p. 1: "we derive the exact expressions of the information transfer, as well as redundant and synergistic transfer, for coupled Gaussian processes observed at multiple temporal scales ... analytically obtained for different time scales from the parameters of the VAR model"; the decomposed quantity is the joint transfer entropy to one target conditioned on the target's past (Eq. 2, p. 3); MMI redundancy (p. 5, Eq. 7: "Rik→j = min{Ti→j, Tk→j}"); p. 5: "if the observed processes have a joint Gaussian distribution, all previously-proposed PID formulations reduce to the MMI PID [36]" (ref. 36 = Barrett 2015). - Kay & Ince (2018), Entropy 20(4), 240, p. 2–3: "It was shown [12] that for a univariate target, if red, unq0 and unq1 depend only on the predictor-target marginal (X0, Y), (X1, Y) distributions, then there is a unique non-negative PID for which the redundancy is given by the minimum mutual information (MMI)"; p. 3: "Several proposed discrete PID measures fall into this class [1,3–5,21], so for Gaussian systems these approaches are all equivalent and equal to the MMI PID."; static Gaussian variables throughout; p. 12: "The Immi PID gives much larger values for the redundancy and synergy components than does the Idep PID."
- Barrett (2015), p. 5: the key result holds "with X univariate and Y and Z of arbitrary dimensions".
Use: novelty sentence — closed-form Gaussian decompositions exist for transfer (Faes et al., 2017) and for static systems (Barrett, 2015; Kay & Ince, 2018), and Varley (2024) gave the aggregate identity; the atom-level closed form on the lag-0/lag-1 structure, the derivative map, the operating point of real pairs and the per-pair diagnostic are what this paper adds. Also a scope sentence for "MMI-specific is not a licensed phrase": on Gaussian systems every PID whose redundancy depends only on the source–target marginals reduces to MMI when the target is univariate (Barrett, 2015; Kay & Ince, 2018), which covers the lattice nodes with a single future or single past variable but not the joint-target nodes or the double redundancy, which are ΦID's own extensions (Mediano et al., 2021, Appendix, Definitions 1–2).
