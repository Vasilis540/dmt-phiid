## Major

1. Asymmetric family explains the "wrong-sign excess" and the rts < xtx pattern — **confirmed**. Recomputed: (a_x, a_y, q) = (0.83, 0.87, 0.25) gives sts 1.1439, xtx 0.5596, yty 0.6829, excess −0.0986, rts = str = 0.5597, mirrors −0.5596 (= −rts); the symmetric case (0.85, 0.85, 0.25) gives excess +0.0231 = rtr. Over pairs with a ~ N(0.85, 0.03) per region and q ~ N(0, 0.3), the mean excess is −0.068 (the data's Table 1: −0.084), rts = str 0.559 below xtx = yty 0.612 (data 0.567 vs 0.627, 0.613), mirrors −0.559 (data −0.535). With a ~ N(0.85, 0.0125) the excess is −0.006, so the size of the effect depends on the within-pair scatter of window-level a's (sampling scatter of a window's lag-1 autocorrelation is about (1 − a²)/√60 ≈ 0.036, so 0.03 is the realistic value). `diag_tables.md` line 5 already gives the asymmetric prediction 1.1865 against the symmetric 1.3212 (observed 1.1377). The mechanism is MMI's minimum over unequal candidates, and the "ACF decaying faster than any AR(1)" attribution is wrong because, as Methods says, τ = 1 atoms depend on lag-0 and lag-1 correlations only. Disposition: NEW-B14 (family-predicted sixteen atoms per pair from measured (a_x, a_y, q), Table 1 column) and R14 rewrite of Results 1 and the two Discussion/Limitations sentences that call the data's fit "loose"; R13 records the finding.
2. Directed (antisymmetric) cross-lag deviation lowers MMI-sts and δ_run averages it away — **confirmed** in direction; magnitude re-estimated. At (0.85, 0.85, 0.25): deviations (+0.05, −0.05) give sts − sts_AR(1) = −0.0126 (reviewer's value); antisymmetric scatter of SD 0.02 / 0.04 / 0.08 gives mean −0.0038 / −0.0080 / −0.0177 (the reviewer wrote −0.015 for SD 0.04; the order is right); independent scatter of both directions gives a positive mean (+0.011 at SD 0.04), so the sign of the finite-sample residual depends on the correlation between the two directions' deviations, which the current null (one symmetric filter per pair) fixes at +1. The data's run-level residual (−0.0137) is of the size an antisymmetric SD of 0.06–0.07 would give. Disposition: NEW-B15 (per-pair antisymmetric component, its run-level RMS and DiD, the closed-form response, and a null configuration with lead–lag asymmetry); Discussion sentence on hemodynamic latency heterogeneity (R14), cited to the literature only if a source is in hand (none of the 36 PDFs is; do not cite from memory).

Disposition: R13 quote R² = 0.796 beside r² = 0.55; R14 replace the 32-fold statement by exchange rates in data units everywhere (abstract, Results 2, Discussion), drop the "r₁-dominated region" label and Figure 2c; NEW-B19 within-window regression of pair sts on (r₁, |q|, |a_x − a_y|).
