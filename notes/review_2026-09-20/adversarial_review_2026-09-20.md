Nothing below is fatal to the closed form or to the DMT demonstration, both of which reproduce (I recomputed the atoms, the derivatives, the determinant identity, the ratio minima and the VAR(1)-equivalence claim; every number I traced to `notes/review_results/` and `results/` matched). Ordered by severity.

## Major

**1. Results 1, "The data depart from the family's predictions in two respects … both are consistent with the regional autocorrelation function decaying faster than any AR(1)" — both "departures" are what the family itself gives once a_x ≠ a_y, and the attribution to the ACF beyond lag 1 contradicts the paper's own Methods.** With unequal coefficients the MMI minima stop being tied and (numerically, using `rev_phiid_fast.atoms_from_corr(ar1_corr(a_x, a_y, q))`) sts ≈ 2·min(S_x, S_y) − C while xtx = S_x − C, yty = S_y − C, rts = str = min(S_x,S_y) − C and the four mirrors = −rts. At (0.83, 0.87, 0.25) the excess sts − (xtx + yty) is −0.099, not +rtr; averaging pairs with SD(a) = 0.03 and SD(q) = 0.3 gives excess −0.065, rts = str = 0.547 below xtx = yty = 0.598, mirrors −0.547 — exactly the data's pattern (0.567 vs 0.627/0.613, mirrors −0.535). The paper already holds the number that proves it: `diag_tables.md` gives the symmetric-a prediction 1.3212 against the asymmetric 1.1865, i.e. asymmetry moves sts by 0.13 nats, 2.7× the residual (−0.049). And Methods states correctly that "the τ = 1 atoms depend only on lag-0 and lag-1 correlations", so the ACF beyond lag 1 cannot produce the excess at all except through n_eff. The same mechanism (MMI's minimum over noisy, unequal per-pair candidates) explains why sts/TDMI is 0.78 windowed vs 0.98 on the family (Results 3, 6), which the paper attributes to unnamed "bias falling unequally on the atoms". Why it matters: the "wrong-sign excess" is presented in Results 1, Discussion and Limitations as evidence that the closed form is "only approximate" and the data "follow the family only loosely"; in fact the (asymmetric) family predicts the pattern, and the true residual is a quarter of what the text implies. Fix: state the asymmetric case (one sentence and a numerical row), add a "family-predicted" column to Table 1 computed for all sixteen atoms from each pair's measured (a_x, a_y, q) with the diagnostic's pipeline, and rewrite Results 1 so the only "departures" are residuals from that prediction; delete the ACF-decay attribution; name the min-over-candidates mechanism where sts/TDMI is discussed.

**2. Results 4, "d is the measured lag-1 cross-correlation minus the substitution's a_y q (both directions averaged)" and "what produces the rest of the run-level residual … is not located" — directed (asymmetric) cross-lag structure lowers MMI-sts and the paper's statistic averages it away.** Because MMI takes minima, any asymmetry between corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) and corr(y_t, x_{t+1}) lowers sts even when their mean deviation is zero. At (0.85, 0.25): deviations (+0.05, −0.05) give a residual of −0.0126; a zero-mean asymmetric scatter of SD 0.04 across pairs gives −0.015, SD 0.08 gives −0.026 — the size of the "unlocated" run-level residual (−0.0137). δ_run, which averages the two directions, is blind to this by construction, and the finite-sample null has no directed structure (one filter per pair, symmetric cross-lag), so its −0.34 % run-level value cannot be a reference for it. In BOLD, directed lead–lag between regions arises from regional hemodynamic latency differences of 0.5–2 s (at TR 2 s that is a sizeable fraction of a lag), a vascular source; DMT is vasoactive. Fix: compute per pair the directed component (d_xy − d_yx), its run-level magnitude, its DiD, and the residual's response to it (the same closed form); add hemodynamic-latency heterogeneity to the mechanisms in Discussion; either add a null configuration with within-pair lead–lag asymmetry or state that the null excludes it.

**3. Abstract, "|∂sts/∂r₁| exceeds |∂sts/∂q| 32-fold at BOLD pairs' operating point"; Results 2 and Figure 2c ("no ratio = 1 boundary exists") — the derivative ratio compares nats per unit of two inputs whose natural ranges differ by an order of magnitude.** Regional r₁ spans 0.81–0.87 (SD 0.0125); pair |q| spans 0–0.6 (SD ≈ 0.2). Per SD of empirical variation the ratio is about 2–4, not 32, and the paper's own numbers say so: pair sts vs pair r₁ gives r² = 0.55 within a window, while the prediction from (a_x, a_y, q) gives R² = 0.80 (`diag_tables.md`, not quoted in the text), so q and asymmetry carry a quarter of between-pair variance. The framing licences "dominates" for the DMT contrast (Δr₁ = −0.015 → −0.09 nats against Δ|q| = −0.016 → +0.003) and for region-averaged maps (where q averages out), not per unit of q in general. Fix: replace the 32-fold statement everywhere with exchange rates in data units ("0.01 of r₁ moves sts by 0.06 nats at r₁ = 0.85; 0.1 of |q| by 0.02; 0.01 of VAR(1) cross-coupling by 0.02"), report the multiple regression of pair sts on (r₁, |q|, |a_x − a_y|) within a window, and drop the "r₁-dominated region" language and Figure 2c's ratio map (finding 36).

**4. Methods "The family with lagged coupling", "slope −1.77 nats per unit c and curvature +40 nats per unit c²"; Abstract last sentence — dominance over lagged interaction is not shown, and the paper never states the striking fact its own table contains: MMI-sts is non-monotone in coupling and falls when positive coupling is added.** `coupling_map_tables.md` at (0.85, 0.25): sts = 1.92, 1.41, 1.30, 1.259, 1.2315, 1.2155, 1.257 for c = −0.10 … +0.10 — U-shaped with its minimum near c ≈ +0.05. Per unit, ∂sts/∂c ≈ −1.8 against ∂sts/∂r₁ ≈ 6, a ratio of 3.4, not 32. The abstract's conclusion ("dominated by lag-1 autocorrelation change wherever r₁ differs and lagged interaction does not change comparably") is therefore a conditional whose antecedent is not quantified and whose "comparably" hides a factor of 3. Why it matters: a synergy atom that decreases with positive lagged coupling is a stronger indictment of the estimator than its r₁ dependence, and it means the diagnostic residual is not a signed measure of interaction (finding 2). Fix: state the non-monotonicity plainly in Results and Discussion; replace the conditional with the exchange-rate sentence of finding 3; add a Figure 2 panel of sts against c at fixed (r₁, q).

**5. Missing analysis (Results 6 / Methods "Bias simulations"): no ground-truth simulation through the actual estimator in which Δr₁ and Δc are varied independently.** The coupled family is population-only; the bias simulations vary neither jointly; the matched null at data-like autocorrelation yields differences "not distinguishable from zero". A referee will ask for the canonical test: VAR(1) pairs at the data's a, q and heterogeneity, W = 60 and 840, N = 14-like, with (i) Δa = −0.015, Δc = 0; (ii) Δa = 0, Δc = ±0.01–0.03; (iii) both; run the full pipeline and report the sts DiD, the predicted DiD, the residual DiD and δ's DiD. Without it the diagnostic has no calibration: a user cannot know what a residual of 0.01 nats means. Fix: add it as a Results section and a figure; it is the one analysis that would let the title's verb stand.

**6. Missing analysis (Recommendations): prewhitening and the ΦID authors' own remedy are absent.** The standard fMRI treatment for autocorrelation is AR(p) prewhitening; the ΦID group's stated treatment is null-model normalisation (Liardi et al., 2025). Neither is run, and deconvolution (a different operation) is relegated to S2. Fix: recompute the sixteen atoms and the DMT contrast (a) after univariate AR(p) prewhitening of each region, (b) on the innovations of the fitted diagonal VAR(1) per pair, (c) against an autocorrelation-matched surrogate null per region (AR bootstrap or IAAFT); report whether the sts contrast survives. Under (a) the family predicts sts → 0 and whatever remains is cross-lag structure; that is the direct test of "autocorrelation-independent component" the residual cannot give.

**7. Author summary, "with the estimator almost all of these studies use, the synergy value mostly reflects … how smooth each region's signal is"; Abstract last sentence; Discussion "A distinction for applicability" — the generalisation to the published literature outruns the evidence.** The papers the argument targets (Luppi 2022, 2023, 2024) use HRF-deconvolved data, mostly at TR 0.72 s, and rank-based synergy–redundancy gradients; the evidence here is TR 2 s, non-deconvolved, N = 14, one drug, and the paper's own deconvolution appendix takes r₁ from 0.87 to 0.79 and "does not transfer to TR 0.72 s". The applicability table also carries entries "not read" (Tarchi et al., listed from its title; Luppi 2026 "Methods inaccessible"). Fix: either add an HCP-like demonstration (TR 0.72 s, deconvolved: regional sts vs regional r₁ and the rank gradient after partialling r₁) or restrict title, abstract and summary to "at TR ≈ 2 s without deconvolution", delete the per-study conditional sentences for studies that could not be read, and remove unread references.

**8. Methods "Multiplicity", "Inferential weight is placed on … results whose sign-flip and phase-randomised p are both ≤ 0.005 across variants"; Results 2 "the r₁ change is the mechanism's input, not its evidence"; Results 3 "so that the exclusion is seen to be by rule and not by evidence" — a post hoc threshold that controls nothing is applied asymmetrically.** The r₁ DiD (p = 0.0106, phase p = 0.073) fails the rule yet the abstract states it as a finding ("as did mean r₁"); the CCS global-fit increase passes it and is dismissed by a second post hoc rule (the closure entry); the residual DiD fails it and receives 2,500 words. A reviewer will read this as judgement dressed as procedure. Fix: delete the rule. Designate the MMI-sts DiD as the sole confirmatory test; label every other quantity exploratory and report it as an effect size with CI and no threshold language; carry the mechanism's evidential weight on the predicted DiD (−0.092 [−0.144, −0.041], p = 0.0037), which is what actually shows the change is a function of the pairs' measured autocorrelations.

**9. ## Minor

**17. Methods "Closed-form atoms", "at q = 0, str + stx + sty + sts = … = S".** The aggregate equals S identically for all q on the family ((S−C) − 2(S−C) + (2S−C) = S); the restriction weakens a stronger statement. Fix: drop "at q = 0".

**18. Same section, "sts − (xtx + yty) = C = rtr, exact at q = 0 and growing with |q|".** The identity is exact for all q; it is the excess that vanishes at q = 0. Fix: "exact for all (a, q); the excess rtr vanishes at q = 0 and grows with |q|".

**19. Methods coupled family, "A = Γ₁Γ₀⁻¹".** Γ₁'s orientation is undefined (it must be E[x_{t+1}x_tᵀ]); stability of A follows from the discrete Lyapunov theorem (Γ₀ − AΓ₀Aᵀ ≻ 0 with Γ₀ ≻ 0 ⇒ ρ(A) < 1) and should be said; and the equivalence is a population statement — a window's sample matrix does not have equal diagonal blocks (mean |q_past − q_future| = 0.017, `residual_source.log`). I verified the claim on 1,751 random PD stationary matrices. Fix: one clause each.

**20. Methods "The DMT contrast", "phase-randomised … (1,000 surrogates; Prichard & Theiler, 1994)".** The surrogate is of a derived, nonlinear local series (whole-brain local sts; window-standardised products), not of the BOLD, so the multivariate P&T citation is inapt and, for the windowed estimator, the concatenated local series mixes fourteen separate fits. The sts/r₁ discrepancy (phase p 0.002 vs 0.073), described as something the surrogate "does not by itself explain", is a property of the two derived series' spectra, not evidence. Fix: phase-randomise the regional BOLD (multivariate, preserving cross-spectra) and recompute atoms, or confine the phase null to a stationarity check and remove it from headline statements.

**21. Methods inference, "subject-bootstrap 95 % CI (10,000 draws)" and the "survives" rule.** Percentile bootstrap intervals at N = 14 under-cover, and "survives" is defined by them. Fix: BCa or a sign-flip-inverted interval; or define "survives" by the sign-flip p.

**Strongest claim the evidence supports (abstract, ≤ 60 words):** On a bivariate AR(1) family the Gaussian-MMI synergy atom equals the two self-prediction atoms plus double redundancy, so it inherits self-information's steep dependence on lag-1 autocorrelation. In DMT fMRI (N = 14, TR 2 s), a prediction from each pair's measured autocorrelations and lag-0 correlation reproduced the whole-brain sts decrease (−0.09 vs −0.08 nats), and regional sts tracked regional r₁ (r = 0.86); CCS-sts showed neither.
