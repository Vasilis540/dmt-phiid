B15 The directed cross-lag component. Per pair and window: d_xy = corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) − a_y q, d_yx = corr(y_t, x_{t+1}) − a_x q; δ_sym = (d_xy + d_yx)/2 (the existing d) and δ_anti = (d_xy − d_yx)/2. ## 6. What the final paper will and will not claim (the target for round 14)

Claims: (1) on the bivariate AR(1) family the Gaussian-MMI synergy atom is the two self-prediction atoms plus the double redundancy, with the four mirror atoms negative — exact, closed form — so it inherits self-information's dependence on lag-1 autocorrelation, at the exchange rates stated in data units; with unequal coefficients the excess turns negative because MMI takes minima, which is the pattern the data show; (2) with lagged coupling the atom is non-monotone and falls for small positive coupling at the data's operating point; (3) on DMT fMRI (N = 14, TR 2 s, no deconvolution) the prediction from each pair's measured autocorrelations and lag-0 correlation reproduces the whole-brain sts decrease (−0.092 predicted, −0.081 observed) and the regional sts map follows regional r₁ (r = 0.86), while CCS-sts is near zero and does not follow it; (4) the residual diagnostic, calibrated on ground truth (B17), reads a residual against its finite-sample expectation; on these data the residual is within that expectation; (5) the directed cross-lag component (B15) and prewhitening (B16) as reported. Not claimed: that any published result is an artefact; that the property is MMI-specific beyond the PIDs that reduce to MMI on Gaussian systems with a univariate target; anything about TR 0.72 s or deconvolved data beyond S2 Text's own numbers.
