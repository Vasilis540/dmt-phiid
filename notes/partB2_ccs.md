## Step 1. What phyid's CCS is, and what it could be verified against

1. The reference. phyid pins the MATLAB toolbox `pmediano/PhiID` at commit a633cc1 as its `matlab` submodule; that checkout is present in this environment. Its `PhiIDFull.m` hard-codes `RedFun = @RedundancyMMI` and `DoubleRedFun = @DoubleRedundancyMMI`, its `private/` folder holds `DoubleRedundancyMMI.m` and `DoubleRedundancyMMIDiscrete.m` and nothing else of the kind, the string "ccs" occurs 0 times in the toolbox, and its README says the library computes ΦID "using Barrett's Minimum Mutual Info (MMI) redundancy function". phyid's `redundancy_ccs` and `double_redundacy_ccs` (docstrings "To be implemented.") therefore have no counterpart in the reference implementation, and the comparison the plan asked for first cannot be made. The published definition (Mediano et al., arXiv:2109.13186 / PNAS 2025) could not be retrieved in this session (arXiv returned only the abstract; PMC and PNAS refused the fetch), so its wording is not quoted here.

2. What the code does (established algebraically and checked numerically, `ccs_verify.log`). phyid's CCS double redundancy is the "double co-information" D = −I_xta − I_xtb − I_yta − I_ytb + I_xtab + I_ytab + I_xyta + I_xytb − I_xytab + R_xyta + R_xytb − R_xytab + R_abtx + R_abty − R_abtxy, kept at the samples where sign(I_xta) = sign(I_xtb) = sign(I_yta) = sign(I_ytb) = sign(D) and set to 0 elsewhere. On the ΦID lattice D ≡ rtr − sts identically (max |D − (rtr − sts)| = 3e-15 over 1,000 random consistent atom vectors): D is the four-variable analogue of the PID identity co-information = redundancy − synergy on which Ince's CCS rests (redundancy = co-information where the signs agree; synergy takes the remainder). Consequence, exact in phyid's output on real data (max deviation 2e-13): at every sample where the five signs agree, sts = 0 and rtr = D; at every other sample, rtr = 0 and sts = −D. The last element of `mi_lst` is therefore not an arbitrary element — it is D — and the construction is Ince's pointwise-exclusive one lifted to the double lattice. Across the real data 28–31 % of samples are sign-agreeing (per-pair range 2–66 %), so the CCS sts of a pair is −D averaged over the other ~70 % of its samples.

3. Verification against the definition, independently of phyid's code. (a) Gaussian: an independent implementation (closed-form Gaussian local entropies, local MIs, Ince's four-way sign rule for the six single-target redundancies, the five-way rule for D) agrees with `phyid.calc_PhiID(redundancy="CCS")` on 200 random (subject, run, window, pair) draws of the real data: max |difference| 2.1e-13 over all 16 atoms and all samples; the six single-target CCS redundancies agree with Ince's definition to 2.1e-13. (b) Binary hand case: 56 samples over the 16 cells of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}) with a designed table; every local MI computed as log₂ of a ratio of table probabilities: local MIs agree with phyid to 7e-16 bits, the six redundancies to 2e-15, the double redundancy to 1e-15, all 16 atoms to 3e-15; the exclusivity holds by hand (max |sts| at agreeing samples 2e-16). Conclusion of step 1: phyid's CCS implements Ince's single-target CCS exactly and extends it to the double lattice with the D-mask; that extension is the only element without a published text checked here. The plan's fallback (implement Gaussian CCS from Ince directly) was carried out and is the implementation used below (`rev_phiid_fast.PairPhiID.atoms_ccs`, identical to phyid to 1e-14 on window means, TR-local series and global bin means).

Structure. Under CCS the four MMI mirror atoms disappear (xts, yts, stx, sty: +0.05 to +0.07 instead of −0.49 to −0.64) and rts/str go to ≈ 0 (instead of +0.53 to +0.64); rtr roughly doubles (0.09–0.10 vs 0.03–0.04); xtx and yty are almost unchanged (ts_gsr W60: 0.6245/0.6105 CCS vs 0.6273/0.6125 MMI) and their DiDs are almost identical to MMI's (−0.0515/−0.0394 vs −0.0523/−0.0396); TDMI is identical by construction. The CCS sts level is negative and 3 % of the MMI level in magnitude; sts ≈ xtx + yty does not hold (−0.04 vs +1.24).

Tracking of r₁. Group-mean window series (28 condition-windows): CCS sts vs mean r₁ r = −0.43 (ts_gsr), −0.62 (ts_demean), against +0.98 / +0.94 for MMI sts; per pair within a window (subject 1, DMT window 6 and placebo window 2): CCS sts vs pair r₁ r = −0.01 / +0.00 (ts_gsr), −0.15 / −0.01 (ts_demean), against +0.74 / +0.70 and +0.52 / +0.63 for MMI sts; CCS sts vs |q| ≤ 0.28. Per subject: the CCS sts DiD is not correlated with the autocorrelation contrast in any cell (r between −0.44 and −0.21, all p ≥ 0.12); the CCS xtx + yty DiD is (r = 0.91–0.96), as the MMI sts DiD was (0.95).

Outcome against the recorded prediction. The criterion for "broad" (CCS sts tracks r₁ with a per-subject correlation above 0.8 and has the sts ≈ self-prediction structure) is not met on any cell: the CCS sts is small (|level| ≤ 0.04 nats), negative, uncorrelated with r₁ per subject, per window and per pair, and does not resemble xtx + yty. By the rule fixed in advance this is the "MMI-specific, claim narrow" branch. The CCS sts DiD is positive in every cell (ts_gsr W60 +0.0036, p = 0.084; ts_gsr global +0.0210, p = 0.0001, 14/14 positive, phase p = 0.0010, survives FD residualisation; ts_demean +0.0134, p = 0.0052, and +0.0321, p = 0.0001), which is the sign flip the earlier withdrawal concerned; as recorded, it is not by itself evidence of anything, and under the construction above it is the change in the average of −D over the sign-disagreeing samples. What does track r₁ under CCS is the pair of self-prediction atoms xtx and yty, whose levels and DiDs coincide with their MMI values.
