# B1. Scope map — sts of a bivariate AR(1) pair as a function of (r₁, q) (14 Sep 2026)

Pre-specified in `notes/partB_prespec_2026-09-14.md` (B1; no prediction). Script `notes/partB1_scope_map.py`; tables `notes/review_results/partB/scope_map_tables.md`; overlay `scope_map_overlay.csv`; figure `scope_map.png`; the full grid and overlay points are regenerated by the script in a few seconds (`scope_map_grid.npz`, `scope_map_overlay_points.npz`, not committed). No manuscript text.

Model. Two unit-variance AR(1) processes with common coefficient a = r₁ and innovation correlation q, so the lag-1 4-vector has correlation matrix S₄ = [[1, q, a, aq], [q, 1, aq, a], [a, aq, 1, q], [aq, a, q, 1]]; the 16 Gaussian-MMI atoms are closed-form functions of (r₁, q) through S₄ (`rev_phiid_fast.atoms_from_corr`, phyid's definitions; `ar1_corr` reproduces the review's F3 table: sts(0.87, 0.2) = 1.3989, sts(0.84, 0.6) = 1.0763). Grid r₁ ∈ [0, 0.95], q ∈ [−0.6, 0.6], step 0.01 (96 × 121); derivatives by central differences (h = 0.001); "flat" = both |∂sts/∂r₁| and |∂sts/∂q| below 0.001 nats per unit.

## What the map shows

1. sts is an even function of q, decreasing in |q| and steeply increasing in r₁. At q = 0: sts = 0.041 (r₁ 0.2), 0.174 (0.4), 0.446 (0.6), 1.022 (0.8), 1.282 (0.85), 1.661 (0.9), 2.328 (0.95). Across q from 0 to ±0.6 at fixed r₁ = 0.85, sts falls from 1.282 to 1.131.

2. |∂sts/∂r₁| exceeds |∂sts/∂q| at every grid point except the flat line r₁ = 0. There is no q-dominated region and therefore no boundary inside the requested grid; on the extended grid |q| ≤ 0.95 there is none either (0 of 18,336 cells). The ratio |∂sts/∂r₁| / |∂sts/∂q| is smallest at large |q| and small r₁ (r₁ = 0.2, q = ±0.6: 0.344 / 0.024 = 14) and grows with r₁: at r₁ = 0.85 it is 6.13 / 0 (q = 0), 6.07 / 0.19 = 32 (|q| = 0.25), 5.97 / 0.33 = 18 (|q| = 0.4), 5.71 / 0.59 = 10 (|q| = 0.6). ∂sts/∂q vanishes on q = 0 by symmetry.

3. sts ≈ xtx + yty as a function of the parameters: the identity is exact on q = 0 at every r₁ (sts − (xtx + yty) = 0 to 1e-4) and the difference grows with |q|, independently of r₁ in relative terms at small |q|: sts − (xtx + yty) is +1.0–2.0 % of sts at |q| = 0.2, +3.5–8.5 % at |q| = 0.4, +9–22 % at |q| = 0.6 (larger shares at smaller r₁). So sts ≈ xtx + yty is a small-|q| statement, and sts exceeds the self-prediction sum by an amount that grows with the pair's cross-correlation.

4. Where the real pairs sit (subject 1, W = 60; every one of the 20 variant × run × window cells): 100 % of the 6,555 pairs fall in the r₁-dominated region. Pair r₁ medians 0.840–0.867 (ts_gsr) and 0.816–0.860 (ts_demean), 5–95 % ranges within 0.775–0.910; pair q medians −0.023 to −0.008 (ts_gsr, after GSR) and +0.13 to +0.39 (ts_demean), 5–95 % ranges −0.63 to +0.86; median |q| 0.22–0.31 (ts_gsr), 0.23–0.41 (ts_demean); 4–13 % (ts_gsr) and 7–30 % (ts_demean) of pairs have |q| > 0.6 and were classified on the extended grid. The pairs' observed window sts (pair means 1.03–1.12 ts_gsr, 0.95–1.09 ts_demean) sit 0.10–0.24 nats below the model's analytic sts at the same (r₁, q) (1.20–1.32 and 1.06–1.30): the finite-window estimate of the review's item 5 (bias −0.15 to −0.20 at W = 60), which is the subject of B4.

5. First-order projection at the data's operating point: at (r₁, |q|) = (0.848, 0.24), ∂sts/∂r₁ = 5.99 nats per unit, so the observed autocorrelation DiD of −0.0146 (ts_gsr, W = 60) maps to −0.087 nats (exact model difference for r₁ 0.848 → 0.8334 at fixed q: −0.084 at q = 0, −0.083 at q = 0.24, −0.082 at q = 0.4), against the observed sts DiD of −0.081; for ts_demean −0.0216 maps to −0.129 against −0.103. The same map says a change of 0.01 in |q| moves sts by 0.002 at that point.
