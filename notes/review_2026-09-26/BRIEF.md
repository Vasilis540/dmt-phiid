## What to report

Report only these four kinds of problem.

**T: text errors.** Typos and spelling; grammar that is wrong (a missing or doubled word, broken agreement, a sentence
that does not parse); wrong or inconsistent symbols (r₁ written as r1, a wrong subscript, a hyphen for a minus sign in a
number, unbalanced brackets); inconsistent spelling of the same term across files; Markdown that will not render as
intended (a table row with the wrong number of cells, broken emphasis).

**N: numbers.** A number that does not match its source file; a rounding error at the printed precision; a sign error;
an interval that does not contain its point estimate, or whose endpoints are swapped; a count or percentage that does
not match its parts; a total that does not add up. Spend your number effort on the numbers of the SI checked against the
files they cite, on every quantity that appears in more than one place (main text, SI, tables, captions, figures: the
same value, sign and precision everywhere, or a stated reason for differing), and on any number that looks wrong on
reading.

**X: cross-references.** A reference that points to something that does not exist or does not say what is claimed: a
Fig, Table, S Table or S Text reference with its section (§) numbers; an equation reference; "record, '<entry title>'"
(the title must match a heading of `analysis_record.md`); a citation (author and year) with no reference-list entry or
the wrong year; a reference-list entry never cited. Also: figures and tables first cited in ascending order, each
caption after the paragraph of its first citation, every S item cited exists and is in the Supporting-information
captions, and every S item listed there exists in the SI files.

**C: contradictions.** The same quantity with two different values in two places; a count stated inconsistently
(subjects, runs, windows, regions, pairs, predictions, sign-flip assignments, table rows); a method or setting
described two different ways (window length, filter band, seed, lag, estimator); two statements that cannot both be
true.

If you are unsure, report the item under **U: uncertain**, with your reasoning, not as an error.

## Not in scope: do not report

- Interpretation, framing, emphasis or strength of claims; whether a conclusion follows from the evidence.
- New analyses, extra citations, reorganisation, length, or style preferences where the text is correct and clear. A
  sentence that is correct and parses is out of scope even if you would have written it differently.

These deliberate choices are not errors:

- **Citation form**: author–year for the preprint; the numbered form is made at submission.
- **[TK] placeholders**: they await the co-authors or the data owners (affiliations, co-authors, the ethics reference
  number and the basis of secondary use, funding statements, contributions, competing interests, acknowledgments, and
  S1 Text's question on the subject codes' letters).
- **Pre-submission leftovers**: the line "Manuscript for co-author review; not for citation or distribution." stays
  until the co-author review. Funding, Competing interests, Data and code availability and Author contributions stay in
  the file and move to the submission system at submission.
- **SI packaging**: five S Text files and one file holding S1–S20 Table, split into separate files at submission.
- **Nichols et al. 2017**: cited only in S4 Text, with its reference entry there.
- **Caption labels**: "Fig N." and "Table N."; in-text references are "Fig N" and "Table N".
- **Labels and paths**: the main text carries no computation label (B1–B24) and no file path, except in Data and code
  availability; the SI may carry both. A computation label or file path in the main text outside that section *is* an
  error: report it.
- **Process labels**: no manuscript file carries a process label (round, stage, bundle, session, writer or planner);
  dated descriptions replace them (S5 Text §5). Such a label in the files you read *is* an error: report it.
- **Notation**: regional r₁ (a region's lag-1 autocorrelation in a window), whole-brain r₁ (its mean over regions) and
  pair r₁ (the mean of a pair's two lag-1 correlations). "pair-level a" is withdrawn: any occurrence *is* an error.

**Coverage** section: what you read in full, what you checked against which files, which cross-reference classes
exhaustively and which by sample, and anything you could not check and why.
