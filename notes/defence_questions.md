**1. Your pre-registered hypothesis was that synergy goes up under DMT. It went down, and now the paper is about the estimator. Isn't this a failed study repackaged — and why was the hypothesis wrong?**

The pre-specified test refuted the hypothesis, and the paper reports it as a refutation under the rule written for that case: whole-brain MMI-sts fell, DiD −0.0809 nats, p = 0.0038, 13 of 14 subjects. The reason it was wrong is the finding: the sixteen-atom table showed the fall was carried by the self-prediction atoms, and the lag-1 autocorrelation change reproduced it per subject at r = 0.953 — so the quantity the hypothesis was stated on does not measure what the workspace account needs, on this estimator, at all. The paper keeps both facts in view: the refutation stands as a refutation of the pre-specified test, and the reframing explains why that test, and others like it, cannot test the account with Gaussian-MMI sts at τ = 1. — *Introduction paragraph 3; Results 3; Methods, "History of the study"; Discussion, "What the finding is and is not".*

**2. What does your pre-specification actually establish? The hypothesis was committed seventeen minutes after a real-data result with the opposite sign was on disk, and the refutation rule was written after the decrease was known.**

It establishes ordering, not blindness, and the paper says so in those words. The hypothesis itself predates every result: it is the record's opening section, written before any script existed — an ordering that rests on document order within the record, not on a commit, since the hypothesis and the fit entered git together — and the 20-region fit in the initial commit was a pipeline check under a setting the script's own docstring marks as never to be reported from — it was inspected, it showed a decrease, and the hypothesis was kept as the workspace account's prediction rather than revised to match it. What is protected: the windowed primary test's estimator, window sets, nulls and motion rule were fixed before the windowed result existed, and every later rule carries a date and commit. What is not: the direction was known from the global fit (per-subject DiDs correlating at 0.95 with the windowed ones) before the windowed rules were fixed, the directional-failure rule entered the repository in the same commit as the 115-region decrease, the Part B plans were written after the review computations had characterised the phenomenon, and the weighting rule of Methods was written at write-up (commit 9318997) after every result it governs existed — all stated in History and Limitations. — *Methods, "History of the study"; Methods, "Multiplicity"; Discussion, "Limitations".*

**3. Everything you couldn't explain points in your original direction: the residual DiD is positive, largest in the drug windows, and CCS-sts rises in 13–14 of 14 subjects. Aren't you burying a real DMT effect? What is the residual, and why isn't it resolved?**

The residual is observed sts minus the sts predicted from each pair's own measured autocorrelations and correlation, with the cross-lag correlations replaced by the AR(1) values; its DiD is +0.0115 nats (p = 0.042), positive in every cell, and neither of the two outcomes the plan named — near zero, or significant and uncorrelated with r₁ — obtained, because it correlates with the r₁ change at −0.78. A stationary finite-sample null, a review computation, produces +0.0037 to +0.0077 of it depending on its free choices, so a third to two-thirds is what the cross-lag assumption does with twenty effective samples; the rest, and a run-level residual of −1.1 % on ts_gsr that is absent on ts_demean, are not accounted for by that null. For the run-level part, the sign(q)-weighted run-level cross-lag deviation on ts_gsr is +0.0034, positive in 14 of 14 (44 % of the run-level residual on the family's scale), and an exact budget of it against a finite-sample null solved to the data — rules recorded before the run — puts finite sampling at a minor part (8–14 %), the within-window term at most (83–84 %), pooling at a minor part (15 %, larger on the DMT run by a tenth of that run's signature) and the window means at a minor part; so the departure sits inside 2-minute windows, where pooling cannot act, and what acts there — common slow drive or lagged coupling, which are one account at τ = 1, faster non-stationarity, or a within-window finite-sample effect absent from the null — the paper does not identify (the signed mean first computed, +0.00009, could not test any of this, because the pairs with q < 0 cancel those with q > 0; the W = 60 and null values of 16 September were withdrawn by a correction note, Supplementary Table S9). Per subject it is largely the same quantity as the CCS-sts change (r = 0.80), and the split-half test meant to tell shared signal from shared window noise could not decide. So the paper does not say there is no effect; it says the remainder is not located, that it arose after four specification changes, and that a pre-specified test on an independent dataset is what would settle it. — *Methods, "The residual diagnostic"; Results 4; Table 4; Discussion, "What the finding is and is not".*

**4. Your whole account rests on "DMT lowers r₁" — and that contrast has sign-flip p = 0.0106 and phase-randomised p = 0.0729. It fails your own weighting rule.**

By the letter of the rule it does, and the paper now says so in Results 2, in the sentence after the r₁ DiD. The account does not rest on that group-level p: it rests on the closed form (∂sts/∂r₁ = 6.07 against ∂sts/∂q = −0.19 at the operating point), on the per-subject collinearity (0.953; 0.846–0.973 with any two subjects dropped), and on the per-pair prediction that reproduces the DiD at r = 0.989 and 114 % of its size. The r₁ change is the input, not the evidence for the mechanism — if r₁ had not moved, sts would not have moved by the map, and the per-subject relation shows they moved together. The paper does flag, as unexplained, that the phase null treats the two contrasts differently (3.8 against 1.8 null SD); and the weighting rule itself is a write-up convention, dated 9318997, not a pre-specified rule. — *Results 2; Results 3, second paragraph; Results 4; Methods, "Multiplicity".*

**5. CCS-sts goes up under DMT in every cell, in 13 and 14 of 14 subjects at the global fit at p = 0.0002 and 0.0001 — it meets your own weighting rule and it is the direction you predicted. Why do you refuse to call it a finding, and why does it go up?**

It is excluded by rule, and the paper says so precisely so that the exclusion is seen to be by rule and not by evidence. Two reasons and one rule: the global-fit cells where the consistency lives are local atoms under a single run-level covariance and are exposed to the DMT run's within-run variance change (window-to-run variance ratio 1.28 before injection, 0.89 after); there is no null model or scope map for CCS-sts, which is −c on the sign-disagreeing samples and has no established reading; and the record's closure entry forbids a DMT-specific claim from any specification chosen after the primary result — this one arose after four. As to why it rises, the paper does not claim to know: per subject its change is largely the diagnostic residual's change (r = 0.80, partial r = 0.83 controlling for the autocorrelation DiD), and whether that shared component is signal or common window noise is undetermined. — *Results 3, CCS paragraph; Table 3; Results 4, last paragraph; Discussion, "Redundancy functions".*

**7. What does this paper say about Luppi et al. (2022), and what doesn't it?**

It says that a synergy-minus-redundancy gradient across regions is the spatial-map case exactly: on the family, higher r₁ raises sts while leaving rtr almost unmoved, so a regional autocorrelation gradient would produce a gradient of the same spatial form, and at the r₁ ≈ 0.97 implied by the HCP TR of 0.72 s and a 0.008–0.09 Hz band, ∂sts/∂r₁ is 32.8 against ∂rtr/∂r₁ of 0.06, which would make it the most exposed dataset in the table. It does not say the gradient is an artefact: the band-pass and TR are not stated in the text we could read, none of the studies reports regional r₁, the primary analysis there is HRF-deconvolved and our deconvolution evidence is from TR 2 s data and does not transfer, and the study falls outside the table's pre-fixed TR range. Every readable study receives the same conditional sentence and none is singled out. The regional test on our own data, run with its prediction recorded first, shows the exposure is real here: on the placebo run before injection, regional sts follows regional r₁ at +0.863 across 115 regions (spin p < 0.0001 on the cortical parcels; r² = 0.745) and the synergy-minus-redundancy map at +0.792 — which says that a regional r₁ gradient and a regional sts gradient coincide on this dataset, not which is the cause, and nothing about that study, whose regional r₁ is not reported. — *Discussion, "A distinction for applicability"; Results 2; Methods, "Literature search"; Supplement S3; Limitations.*

**9. Your AR(1) family does not fit the data: the excess has the wrong sign, atoms the family says are equal differ, and the prediction over-shoots the DiD by 14–29 %. How is this an "analytic account"?**

It is an account of the estimator's structure, exact on the family and approximate on the data, and the paper says that in Results 1 and again in Limitations. The τ = 1 atoms depend only on lag-0 and lag-1 correlations, so the family's statements about (r₁, q) hold for the estimator whatever the process; the AR(1) assumption enters the diagnostic at one place, the cross-lag substitution, and the paper traces the level residual entirely to that step (−0.0489 of −0.0489). Both departures are reported in Results 1 — the wrong-signed excess (−0.084 against +0.039) and the family's equality xtx = yty = rts = str, which the data break by about a tenth (0.6273 and 0.6125 against 0.5669 and 0.5667) — with two candidate causes for the excess, positive lagged coupling and finite-sample scatter, that the data do not separate. — *Results 1; Methods, "Closed-form atoms" and "The residual diagnostic"; Results 4; Limitations.*

**10. Varley (2024) already showed that MMI synergy of two independent autocorrelated processes is one process's self-information. What do you add?**

Varley's result is about the aggregate forward synergy, str + stx + sty + sts, and about disintegration: on cutting every link the MMI synergy equals one process's self-information (Eq. 12), and the change depends on the instantaneous mutual information (Eq. 13), with r = 0.97 against pairwise mutual information on 1,000 pairs. This paper says which atoms carry it — sts = 2S − C, with rts and str at S − C and four mirror atoms at −(S − C) — gives sts as a function of (r₁, q) with the derivative ratio and the operating point of real pairs, adds the coupled family, the CCS comparison, the lag dependence, and a per-pair residual diagnostic with a finite-sample null, and demonstrates on within-subject data that a real between-state contrast is reproduced, subject by subject, by the r₁ change. Varley is cited as prior art throughout. — *Introduction paragraph 2; Methods, "Closed-form atoms".*

**11. Explain why sts under MMI carries self-prediction.**

On the AR(1) family each region's present predicts its own next value with information S = −½ ln(1 − a²), and predicts the other region's next value with only C = −½ ln(1 − a²q²), which is much smaller. MMI defines redundancy as the minimum, so every one-source-one-target redundancy and rtr are set to C, and the lattice inversion has nowhere to put the large S except into sts, rts and str, balanced by the four mirror atoms at −(S − C): sts = 2S − C = xtx + yty + rtr. The six cross-prediction atoms are zero because the other region carries nothing about a region's future beyond its own past, so the pair's entire TDMI is self-prediction, of which MMI assigns all but C to sts; Varley's identity is the q = 0 sum of these atoms. The data show it: sts = 1.1554 against xtx + yty = 1.2398, rts and str about half of sts, four mirrors at about −0.535. — *Methods, "Closed-form atoms of a bivariate AR(1) pair"; Results 1; Table 1.*

**12. What does the scope map show, and what doesn't it?**

It shows sts, the excess and the derivative ratio over (r₁, q): |∂sts/∂r₁| exceeds |∂sts/∂q| at every grid point except r₁ = 0, so there is no q-dominated region anywhere, and the informative content of the overlay is the operating point of real pairs and the ratio there — 32 at (0.85, 0.25), 10 at (0.85, 0.6), never below 1.9 even at |q| = 0.95. It does not show what lagged interaction does (the family has none; the coupled family shows interaction moves sts at fixed (r₁, q)), it says nothing about the brain, and the fact that all of a subject's pairs fall in the "r₁-dominated region" carries no information, which the paper says in those words. What the data add to the map, in the revision after the companion, is that the dependence it draws holds across regions as well as across pairs: regional sts follows regional r₁ at +0.863 on the placebo baseline. — *Methods, "The scope map"; Results 2; Figure 2.*

**13. Your split-half test had a pass threshold that could not be reached. How did that happen, and what does the test tell us?**

The rule — cross-half mean at least half the within-half mean, both cross-half correlations positive — was written without reference to the split-half reliabilities of the two quantities; at the reliabilities observed (residual DiD 0.49, CCS-sts DiD 0.30 on ts_gsr) the largest cross-half correlation two perfectly correlated reliable components could show is √(0.494 × 0.302) = 0.39, below the threshold of 0.41, so the "not window noise" branch was unreachable, and the paper records that as a flaw in the rule. The observed cross-half correlation, 0.32, sits at the ceiling and disattenuates to 0.82, which is consistent with a shared reliable component but not distinguishable at N = 14 from a partial one or from none; the verdict is undetermined. What the test does establish is that most of the within-half correlation (0.815 against a ceiling of 0.39) is estimation noise common to the same windows, and that the reliable part of each quantity is small. — *Methods, "The residual diagnostic"; Results 4; Table 5.*

**14. What is the paper's single weakest point?**

The remainder: a pre-specified diagnostic whose outcome fell outside both of its recorded branches, leaving a positive residual DiD in the direction of the original hypothesis, of which a stationary null with free choices accounts for a third to two-thirds, sharing most of its per-subject variance with an unread CCS-sts increase, and with the one test meant to classify it unable to. The paper cannot say what the remainder is; for the run-level part, the cross-lag departure behind it (sign(q)-weighted deviation +0.0034, 44 % of that residual on the family's scale) is by an exact budget mostly inside 2-minute windows (83–84 %), with finite sampling and pooling a minor part each, but what acts inside the windows the paper cannot say — common slow drive and lagged coupling are one account at τ = 1 — and the reading of this part changed three times as the statistic was corrected: a signed mean that could not test pooling (caught by a fifth review), then a W = 60 value whose sign was taken from each window and a null read at the wrong q̂ density (withdrawn by a correction note), then the budget. That weakness is compounded by the history: the account was derived after the observation, and the rules that govern the remainder were written on the day they were run. The paper's defence is that it says all of this itself and claims nothing from the remainder in either direction. — *Results 4; Discussion, "What the finding is and is not"; Limitations.*

**15. The placebo run rises and carries 0.40 of your DiD. Is your "effect" a placebo drift?**

Partly, and that is part of the phenomenon rather than an artefact of it: on the placebo run sts rose by +0.0324 (p = 0.0425) while on DMT it fell by −0.0485 (p = 0.0267), and the autocorrelation contrast has the same structure, its placebo-run r₁ drift carrying 0.34 of its DiD. The paper treats a within-session r₁ drift as one of the exposures it warns about, and the two post-hoc trend corrections leave the sts contrast in place (−0.0890, p = 0.0021; −0.0995, p = 0.0007). The residual DiD rests on the same two-directions structure, with a third of it on the placebo run, and the paper says so. — *Table 2; Results 3; Results 4; Discussion, "A distinction for applicability".*

**16. Under the phase-randomised null the sts DiD sits at 3.8 SD and the autocorrelation DiD at 1.8 SD. If they are the same thing, why does the null treat them so differently?**

The paper does not know, and says so: it lists this as one of the two facts that keep the two contrasts from being identical, and describes it as "a difference the phase-randomised surrogate of a bounded, window-standardised statistic does not by itself explain". The two statistics are built differently — sts from a 4 × 4 fit, the autocorrelation from a window-standardised product bounded in [−1, 1] — and the surrogate preserves the spectrum but not that construction. It is an open point, and the sign-flip test, which treats both alike, gives p = 0.0038 and p = 0.0106. — *Results 3, second paragraph; Methods, "The DMT contrast".*

**17. Four hundred and seventy-four inference rows and no multiplicity correction.**

Correct, and stated: no correction is applied, the files hold 474 rows, and the paper quotes only the rows it names. Inferential weight is placed on the one pre-specified primary contrast and on results whose sign-flip and phase-randomised p are both at or below 0.005 across both variants; anything between 0.01 and 0.06 — the residual DiD at 0.042, CCS-sts at the primary cell at 0.056 — is reported as such and not treated as established. That weighting rule is a write-up convention, first committed at 9318997 after every result it governs existed, and the paper says so; the positive claims are the analytic ones, and the data claims are one contrast and one collinearity. — *Methods, "Multiplicity"; Limitations.*

**18. A 4 × 4 Gaussian fit on twenty effective samples — your matched null shows the windowed estimator manufactures up to 16 % of your DiD from covariance changes alone. Why is it your primary estimator, and why recommend the global fit but not use it?**

W = 60 is primary because it was pre-specified, after bias simulations showed it keeps the true sign and absorbs 15–45 % of a true difference against 52–77 % at W = 30, and because the global fit gives the same contrast (−0.0801 against −0.0809). The manufacture figures are reported as they are: −9 %, +16 %, +7 % and −8 % of the real DiD at W = 60 in the matched pairs, up to +33 % at W = 30, at most 2 % at run length in three of the four matched pairs. The global fit is recommended on those manufacture grounds for contrasts between separately fitted runs or groups, and the paper immediately qualifies it: a pre/post contrast within one run under a single fit is exposed to within-run variance change, and the global fit on ts_gsr is the one cell where sts fell less than proportionally to TDMI. — *Methods, "Bias simulations"; Results 6; Results 3, proportionality.*

**19. DMT makes people move. Is the sts decrease just motion?**

Framewise displacement itself did not change (FD DiD +0.0143, p = 0.2452), and the sts contrast survives residualisation on window-mean FD within each subject and run: −0.0649 [−0.0966, −0.0289], p = 0.0048, 13 of 14. The autocorrelation contrast survives the same control (−0.0123, p = 0.0135), as do the CCS-sts and residual contrasts. Motion is the one confound the pre-specified rule addressed, and the rule's definition of survival (same sign, bootstrap CI excluding zero) was fixed in advance. — *Table 2; Methods, "The DMT contrast"; Results 2; Results 4.*

**20. The intensity ratings came from a different session. Wasn't the original design broken from the start?**

The ratings were collected in a second, later session of the same day, and the paper says so and uses them in no main-text result. The original intensity-tracking claim was void under its own pre-specified controls in every cell regardless — the sign was negative in every cell against the predicted positive direction and the claim was void under its controls in every cell — and it is reported in Supplement S1, Table S2, with the void verdict. The step contrast, which is what the paper rests on, does not use the ratings. — *Methods, "Dataset"; Supplement S1; Table S2.*

**21. Isn't the fix simply to use CCS, or another redundancy function?**

CCS is not offered as the corrected estimator, only as the one whose sts does not carry the autocorrelation contrast the way MMI-sts does, and its costs are stated: it takes negative values, its whole-brain value depends on which fifth sign the mask uses (−0.048 under the published definition against −0.039 under `phyid`'s, which the paper found implements a different mask), it has no null model or scope map, and its DMT increase has no established reading. Other redundancy functions — I_min, I_BROJA, the dependency-based and information-geometric ones — were not examined, so "MMI-specific" is not a phrase the paper licenses. The recommendation is to report r₁ beside sts and run the diagnostic, whichever redundancy function is used. — *Discussion, "Redundancy functions"; Methods, "The two redundancy functions"; Recommendations.*

**22. Wouldn't a longer lag, or HRF deconvolution, remove the artefact?**

A longer lag weakens it by weakening everything: as r_τ falls (0.85, 0.51, 0.14, −0.20 at τ = 1, 2, 3, 5) the sts atom collapses from 1.1554 to 0.0262 nats while the autocorrelation change grows to τ = 3, the sts contrast tracks the r_τ contrast wherever the latter is significant and is null where it is not, and at no lag do the pairs reach a point where q contributes comparably, because no such point exists on the family. Deconvolution, tried post hoc, took r₁ from 0.87 to 0.79 and left the sts contrast and its collinearity with the autocorrelation contrast in place; it is not a remedy on these data, and its effect at TR 0.72 s is unknown. — *Results 5; Table 6; Figure 5; Recommendations; Supplement S2.*

**23. You give three different predictions of the sts change from autocorrelation: −0.087 from the map, −0.0924 from the diagnostic, −0.069 from the matched-null family. Which is the number, and why do they differ?**

Results 6 now reconciles them, and the Abstract says which it means: the 114 % is the diagnostic's −0.0924. They answer three different questions: the map projection multiplies the mean r₁ change by the slope at one point; the diagnostic predicts every pair from its own (a_x, a_y, q) and averages, which is why it is the one used for the residual; and the matched-null figure is the change in the *true* sts from the observed change in the whole autocorrelation function at fixed q, of which the W = 60 estimator returns −0.056. All three have the observed sign; the largest, the diagnostic's, over-shoots by the residual the paper reports, and the smallest is the true-sts change before the estimator's own shrinkage, so the differences are the residual and the manufacture figures seen from different sides. — *Results 2; Results 4 and Table 4; Results 6.*

**24. ΦR, the workspace quantity, did not change on the raw data but rose after deconvolution. Doesn't the workspace account survive untouched — or even gain support?**

ΦR did not change at the primary cell (+0.0007, p = 0.8971, phase p = 0.8372) and no cell had a primary-set sign-flip p below 0.05; the three cells with phase p below 0.05 disagree in sign across variants. The deconvolved increase was closed by a dated entry for four reasons the paper lists: a pre-injection baseline gap in the direction that creates it, a placebo-run decline across the session, estimator disagreement, and a 56–64 % placebo share. The paper makes no claim about ΦR in either direction; on the family ΦR = rtr falls with a fall in either r₁ or |q|, so the r₁-dominance result for sts does not carry over to it, which is stated as a limit on the paper's reach, not as support for the account. — *Results 3, last paragraph; Table 3; Supplement S2; Discussion, "A distinction for applicability".*

**25. Your pipeline has never been run end to end, the subject order across files was checked on one subject, and it is one dataset with N = 14. How reproducible and general is this?**

Each script was run individually as recorded, every results table carries the git SHA that produced it, the closed-form implementation reproduces `phyid` to 10⁻¹⁴, and the end-to-end run is marked [TK] before submission; the subject-order check is stated as one subject with the record entry cited.
