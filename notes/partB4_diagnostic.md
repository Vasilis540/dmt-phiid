# B4. The diagnostic — sts predicted per pair from measured (a_x, a_y, q); residual = observed − predicted (14 Sep 2026)

Pre-specified in `notes/partB_prespec_2026-09-14.md` (B4), prediction fixed before any number below existed. Script `notes/partB4_diagnostic.py`; supplement `notes/partB4_residual_source.py` (written after the diagnostic's numbers were seen, to locate the residual's source; labelled as such). Outputs in `notes/review_results/partB/` (`diag_tables.md`, `diag_run.log`, `diag_series_<variant>_W<W>.npz` with observed / predicted / residual per subject × run × window and the per-window R² and cross-lag checks; `residual_source.log`) and `notes/review_results/inference_rows_diag.csv` (+ `.pkl`). W = 30 was added as a second windowed estimator beyond the plan (W = 60 and global) and is labelled as an addition. No manuscript text.

## What was computed

For every pair, subject, run and window, the measured 4 × 4 correlation matrix of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}) gives the observed sts (closed form, identical to phyid). The prediction keeps the pair's measured lag-1 autocorrelations a_x, a_y and lag-0 correlation q (mean of the past- and future-block values) and uses the diagonal VAR(1) matrix S₄ = [[1, q, a_x, a_y q], [q, 1, a_x q, a_y], [a_x, a_x q, 1, q], [a_y q, a_y, q, 1]]; predicted sts = closed-form sts of S₄. Residual = observed − predicted, per pair, averaged over the 6,555 pairs per window. The engine is run on the observed, predicted and residual whole-brain series (phase-randomised null on the TR-local observed sts minus the window's predicted value). Per-pair R² of predicted vs observed within a window; AR(1) cross-lag check: measured corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) against a_y q (and corr(y_t, x_{t+1}) against a_x q).

## What it shows against the recorded prediction

1. Level. The prediction from (a_x, a_y, q) alone reproduces the observed sts to within 4.3 % (ts_gsr W60; 3.5 % ts_demean), over-predicting: residual −0.049 nats on 1.138. Across the 392 subject × run × window cells the residual's SD is 0.016 against 0.079 for the observed sts. Per pair within a window the squared correlation between predicted and observed sts across the 6,555 pairs is 0.80 (W60); pair-level r(observed, predicted) 0.885–0.914 in the four subject-1 windows listed in `diag_tables.md`. The symmetric prediction with a_x = a_y = (a_x + a_y)/2 (the B1 map's family) over-predicts by 16 % (1.321), so the unequal-a form matters at the level. At W30 the over-prediction is larger (−9.7 %, R² 0.62); at the run level (global fit) the residual is −1.1 % (ts_gsr) and +0.1 % (ts_demean).

2. The contrast. The predicted DiD has the observed sign in every cell and exceeds the observed DiD in magnitude by 14 % (ts_gsr W60: −0.0924 vs −0.0809), 27 % (W30), 18 % and 29 % (ts_demean W60, W30). Per subject, predicted and observed DiDs correlate at 0.98–0.99, and the predicted DiD correlates with the autocorrelation contrast at 0.965–0.976 (the observed DiD: 0.953–0.976). The residual DiD is positive in every cell — +0.0115 [+0.0021, +0.0211] at ts_gsr W60, sign-flip p = 0.042, phase p = 0.019, negative in 4/14, FD-residualised +0.0100 [+0.0008, +0.0185] (p = 0.058, survives by the CI rule); +0.0182 (p = 0.004) at ts_demean W60; +0.0182 and +0.0259 at W30 — and is 14–29 % of the observed DiD in magnitude. Under the trend corrections: ts_gsr W60 +0.0106 (p = 0.054) and +0.0134 (p = 0.0089). The residual window series is small relative to the observed one: group means −0.034 to −0.059 (DMT; range 0.025 against 0.132 for the observed DMT series) and −0.041 to −0.057 (placebo) at ts_gsr W60, the least negative DMT values falling in windows 6–8 (−0.034, −0.039, −0.042), where the observed sts is lowest; the DiD comes from DMT −0.0530 (pre) → −0.0453 (post) against placebo −0.0479 → −0.0517. By set: early +0.0179 [+0.0099, +0.0260] (p = 0.0012, phase p = 0.0030), late +0.0064 [−0.0058, +0.0183] (p = 0.32, does not survive FD residualisation); the late set does not survive at ts_gsr W30 or ts_demean W60 either.

3. Outcome against the recorded prediction. The prediction was a residual DiD near zero; the alternative to be reported as exploratory was a residual DiD significant *and uncorrelated with r₁*. Neither outcome obtained as worded. The residual DiD is not near zero by the record's conventions: it is 14–29 % of the observed DiD, opposite in sign (the AR(1) prediction over-shoots the observed decrease), with the CI excluding zero and both nulls below 0.05 in every windowed cell. It is not uncorrelated with r₁ either: per subject it correlates with the autocorrelation contrast at −0.78 (ts_gsr W60), −0.82 (W30), −0.60 (ts_demean W60, W30) and with the observed DiD at −0.63 / −0.73 / −0.51 / −0.57 — subjects whose r₁ falls more have a larger positive residual DiD, i.e. a larger over-prediction of the decrease. The pre-specified signature of an autocorrelation-independent component (significant and uncorrelated with r₁) is therefore not met. No further reading is attached to the residual.

4. Where the residual comes from (supplement, `residual_source.log`; ts_gsr W60, all cells). At τ = 1 the prediction differs from the measured matrix in two kinds of entry only (checked: the diagonal is 1 and a_x, a_y are kept verbatim): the two cross-lag entries, corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) → a_y q and corr(y_t, x_{t+1}) → a_x q, and the two lag-0 entries → their mean q. Applying the substitutions separately: the cross-lag substitution alone gives 1.1866 (residual −0.0489, the whole of it; max cell difference from the full prediction 0.0006); the lag-0 substitution alone gives 1.1372 (residual +0.0005). The measured cross-lag correlations deviate from a_y q with mean +0.00014 (no offset; per-cell range −0.0004 to +0.0014) and SD 0.113 across pairs within a window (mean absolute 0.091; 1/√60 = 0.129 for reference); the over-prediction is therefore produced by the pair-level scatter of the cross-lag correlations about the AR(1) value, not by a systematic departure from it, and it is 1.1 % at the run level where the scatter is averaged over the run's 839 lagged samples (58–59 per window). Across the 392 cells the residual is more negative where the pairs' a and |q| are higher (r = −0.74 with mean pair a, −0.71 with mean |q|); its raw correlation with the cross-lag scatter SD is +0.43, but the scatter SD is itself lower where a is higher (r = −0.79), and in a joint regression of the residual on standardised (a, |q|, scatter SD) the coefficients are −0.74, −0.44, −0.26 (partial correlations −0.63, −0.60, −0.30; R² 0.74): more scatter, at given a and |q|, gives a more negative residual. Per subject the residual DiD correlates with the pair-a DiD at −0.78 and with the pair-|q| DiD at −0.74, and with the scatter-SD DiD at +0.28. Under DMT both a and |q| fall (pair a: DMT 0.863 → 0.853, placebo 0.860 → 0.866, DiD −0.0155, 12/14 negative; pair |q|: DMT 0.284 → 0.266, placebo 0.284 → 0.282, DiD −0.0164, 10/14 negative) while the scatter SD rises slightly (DMT 0.111 → 0.115, placebo 0.113 → 0.110; DiD +0.0068, 5/14 negative — a change whose own effect on the residual would be negative, not positive). The positive residual DiD is thus the over-prediction shrinking as a and |q| fall under DMT.

5. Dependence on the AR(1) assumption, as required. The τ = 1 closed-form atoms depend on the lag-0 and lag-1 correlations only, so the shape of the autocorrelation function beyond lag 1 (the review's item 5: pooled placebo ACF 0.868, 0.539, 0.172, −0.085, −0.174, −0.145 at lags 1–6 against AR(1) 0.868, 0.753, 0.654, …) does not enter this diagnostic at all; the AR(1) assumption enters only through the cross-lag entries a_y q and a_x q (point 4), and the cross-lag check gives a mean absolute deviation of 0.091 / 0.093 (ts_gsr / ts_demean, W60) and 0.128 / 0.130 (W30), with pair-level correlation between measured and model cross-lag correlations of 0.933 (min over windows 0.867) at ts_gsr W60, 0.912 (0.817) at ts_gsr W30, 0.913 (0.730) at ts_demean W60 and 0.892 (0.678) at ts_demean W30. The lag-dependence results of B3 are where the ACF beyond lag 1 matters.

6. Global fit. The run-level (a_x, a_y, q) give one prediction per run; it is constant across the run's 28 bins, so the bin-level residual DiD equals the observed global-fit DiD by construction (−0.0801 ts_gsr, −0.1035 ts_demean) and no decomposition of the contrast is possible at that estimator. What the global fit does give is the run-level residual: −1.1 % (ts_gsr) and +0.1 % (ts_demean) of the observed run-level sts.
