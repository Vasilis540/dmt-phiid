# The Gaussian-MMI synergy atom of integrated information decomposition is mostly self-prediction: a closed form on the AR(1) family, a per-pair diagnostic, and a within-subject DMT fMRI test

## Abstract

Integrated information decomposition (ΦID) with the minimum-mutual-information (MMI) redundancy function underlies most reports of synergy in fMRI. We derive the sixteen Gaussian-MMI atoms of a bivariate AR(1) pair in closed form and show that the synergy atom, sts, is mostly self-prediction: on the symmetric family sts = xtx + yty + rtr, the two self-prediction atoms plus the double redundancy, balanced by four negative atoms, and with unequal coefficients MMI takes the smaller self-information, so sts falls below the sum. sts therefore inherits the dependence of self-information on lag-1 autocorrelation r₁: at the operating point of BOLD region pairs, 0.01 of r₁ moves sts by 0.061 nats and 0.1 of the lag-0 correlation |q| by 0.019, a ratio of 2 to 1 per standard deviation of the data's own variation, and lagged coupling moves sts non-monotonically. We test the account on within-subject fMRI (14 volunteers, DMT and placebo, TR 2 s) with one confirmatory contrast, the pre/post difference-in-differences of whole-brain MMI-sts, which began as a planned directional test recorded before the primary analysis. sts fell under DMT (−0.081 nats, p = 0.004, 13 of 14 subjects), r₁ fell (−0.015), and the sts change predicted from each pair's measured autocorrelations and lag-0 correlation was −0.092; the two contrasts share their reliable variance across subjects (cross-half r = 0.69, ceiling 0.73). The regional synergy map follows regional r₁ (r = 0.86 over 115 regions), and its sensory–association contrast vanishes when r₁ is partialled out. A per-pair diagnostic, calibrated on simulated ground truth, leaves a residual within its finite-sample expectation, so the data carry no evidence of a change in lagged interaction. Under CCS redundancy the synergy atom is near zero; prewhitening is not a remedy on band-passed data. We recommend reporting r₁ beside sts and reading sts contrasts against the per-pair prediction.

## Author summary

Functional brain imaging asks how regions carry information together, not only which are active. A widely used measure, from integrated information decomposition, is synergy: information that a pair of regions holds jointly and neither holds alone. Synergy has been reported to differ across the cortex and to fall under anaesthesia and in disorders of consciousness, and is read as a mark of integrative processing. We show that, with the estimator almost all of these studies use, the synergy value is mostly something simpler: how well each region's signal predicts its own next sample, its lag-1 autocorrelation. We derive this on a solvable mathematical family and confirm it on functional MRI from fourteen volunteers scanned under the psychedelic DMT and placebo. Under DMT the measured synergy fell, but so did the autocorrelation of the signals, and the apparent fall in synergy was predicted, in sign and size, by the fall in autocorrelation. We had set out to test the opposite hypothesis, that DMT raises synergy, and we report its refutation and what we found instead. We recommend that studies report autocorrelation beside synergy and use the per-pair diagnostic we provide, which says how much of a synergy contrast autocorrelation accounts for.

## Introduction

Integrated Information Decomposition (ΦID; Mediano et al., 2021, 2025) extends partial information decomposition (Williams & Beer, 2010) to the time-delayed mutual information (TDMI) between the past and the future of two variables, splitting it into sixteen atoms that describe how each kind of information in the past (redundant, unique to either source, synergistic) becomes each kind in the future. In fMRI the atom that has drawn most attention is synergy-to-synergy, sts, read as the information that only the joint past of two regions carries about their joint future. On resting-state BOLD it separates a redundancy-dominated sensory core from synergy-dominated association cortex (Luppi et al., 2022). Regions ranked by synergy form a "synergistic workspace" whose integrated information is reduced under propofol and in disorders of consciousness (Luppi et al., 2024). Group differences in the synergy and redundancy atoms have been reported in Alzheimer's disease, schizophrenia and neurodevelopmental conditions (Down et al., 2026; Nago et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025). The studies whose settings could be read (S3 Text) use the Gaussian estimator with the MMI redundancy function (Barrett, 2015; Mediano et al., 2021) at a single lag — one TR where the lag is stated (Luppi et al., 2023; Down et al., 2026) — on BOLD series whose lag-1 autocorrelation is high because of haemodynamic smoothing and band-pass filtering.

That MMI synergy and autocorrelation are entangled is known. Barrett (2015) exhibited positive net synergy — hence positive MMI synergy, since MMI redundancy is non-negative — in dynamical Gaussian examples in which a target's own immediate past is one of the sources (his Example 1, Fig. 4a), vanishing when the infinite pasts are the sources. Varley (2024) showed, for the aggregate forward synergy of a bivariate process (in ΦID terms str + stx + sty + sts), that MMI cannot distinguish a synergistic pair from two independent autocorrelated processes: for a pair "disintegrated" while keeping each process's autocorrelation, the MMI synergy equals the self-information of the less self-predictive of the two (his Eq. 12), and on 1,000 region pairs of one human subject the synergy change on disintegration correlated with pairwise mutual information at r = 0.97. Faes et al. (2025) state the general problem — PID applied to time series rests on an implicit i.i.d. assumption that is 'typically not tested in practice and is often violated', temporal correlations have 'a profound impact' on the lag-zero decomposition, and lagged PIDs on ad hoc variables cover only 'specific portions of the system dynamics' — and propose a rate-based decomposition as the remedy. The ΦID authors have moved towards null-model normalisation of atoms (Liardi et al., 2025) and, in some studies, towards CCS redundancy (Luppi et al., 2023); their own worked example is a coupled AR pair (Mediano et al., 2021, Fig. 5 and Appendix VI: x_{t+1} = 0.4 (x_t + y_t) + ε, y_{t+1} = 0.4 (x_t + y_t) + η), the symmetric coupled family of Methods at coefficient 0.4, whose c = 0 case is the diagonal family used here. Closed-form Gaussian decompositions exist for transfer entropy (Faes et al., 2017) and for static systems (Barrett, 2015; Kay & Ince, 2018), and Varley (2024) gave the aggregate identity; what none of them gives is an account at the level of the atoms actually reported — which of the sixteen carry the self-information, how sts depends on a pair's lag-1 autocorrelation r₁ and lag-0 cross-correlation q, where real region pairs sit on that dependence, whether CCS shares the property, and how much of an observed sts change is autocorrelation change, with the remainder read pair by pair against a calibrated expectation rather than against zero.

This study did not set out to provide these. It began as a planned directional test, recorded before the primary analysis, of whether whole-brain synergy is up-regulated under N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT), a state of intensified conscious content, against the workspace account's prediction that synergy indexes conscious level. Synergy decreased; the decrease was carried by the pair's within-region self-prediction atoms and reproduced by the lag-1 autocorrelation contrast; the closed form was derived afterwards to account for it, and the question became what the estimator measures. We give the sixteen atoms of the AR(1) pair in closed form, with equal and with unequal coefficients and with lagged coupling, and the exchange rates between the atom and its inputs in data units (Results 1); the DMT contrast as the one confirmatory test, with the prediction from the pairs' measured autocorrelations beside it (Results 2); the regional map with regional autocorrelation partialled out (Results 3); the residual diagnostic calibrated on simulated ground truth (Results 4); the lag dependence (Results 5); the CCS comparison (Results 6); and the remedies a reader will ask about, prewhitening, deconvolution and the run-level fit (Results 7). Everything after the confirmatory contrast is exploratory and is reported with its effect size and interval.

## Results

Notation: each ΦID atom is a two-letter code, first letter the kind of information in the past (r redundant, x unique to region X, y unique to Y, s synergistic), last letter its kind in the future; sts is synergy-to-synergy, xtx the self-prediction of X; values are in nats. The DMT contrast of a quantity is its difference-in-differences (DiD): (post − pre) on the DMT run minus (post − pre) on the placebo run, per subject (Methods).

### 1. The sixteen atoms of a bivariate AR(1) pair in closed form

On the bivariate AR(1) family the Gaussian-MMI synergy atom is the two self-prediction atoms plus the double redundancy, balanced by four negative atoms, and the data's atom table has that structure. Let x and y be unit-variance AR(1) processes with lag-1 autocorrelation a (= r₁) and lag-0 correlation q, so that corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) = aq. With S = −½ ln(1 − a²), the self-information of one process, and C = −½ ln(1 − a²q²), the cross-lag mutual information, the Möbius inversion of the ΦID lattice under MMI gives (Methods): the six cross-prediction atoms rtx, rty, xtr, ytr, xty, ytx are zero; xtx = yty = rts = str = S − C; the four mirror atoms xts, yts, stx, sty are −(S − C) each; rtr = C; and

sts = 2S − C = −ln(1 − a²) + ½ ln(1 − a²q²).

Hence sts − (xtx + yty) = rtr, exact for all (a, q), with the excess vanishing at q = 0; rtr + sts = TDMI = 2S, which depends on r₁ alone; ΦR = rtr; and str + stx + sty + sts = S, which is Varley's (2024) aggregate identity. The four mirror atoms are negative, a property of MMI-ΦID that its authors note (Mediano et al., 2025, SI Appendix, Sec. III.A; Luppi et al., 2026; under MMI only the redundancy and synergy atoms are guaranteed non-negative, Down et al., 2026). On this family the pair's entire TDMI is self-prediction, and MMI assigns all of it but the cross-lag mutual information C to sts.

With unequal coefficients a_x ≠ a_y the MMI minima select the less self-predictive member: at q = 0, sts = 2 min(S_x, S_y) exactly and rtr = 0 (S3 Text), so sts lies below xtx + yty = S_x + S_y by |S_x − S_y|; for q ≠ 0 the excess sts − (xtx + yty) is negative and grows with the asymmetry, rts = str lie below xtx and yty, and the mirror atoms sit at about −rts. At a mean coefficient of 0.85 and q = 0.25, sts falls from 1.2588 at equal coefficients to 1.1711 at a difference of 0.03.

The exchange rates in data units are the derivatives at the operating point of BOLD region pairs, (r₁, q) = (0.85, 0.25) (Fig 2a; the pairs' medians on the primary variant are 0.840–0.867 for r₁ and 0.22–0.31 for |q|; 0.82 is the r₁ of ideal band-passed white noise at 0.01–0.08 Hz and TR 2 s). A change of 0.01 in r₁ moves sts by 0.061 nats (∂sts/∂r₁ = +6.07); a change of 0.1 in |q| moves it by 0.019 (∂sts/∂q = −0.19); a change of 0.01 in symmetric lagged coupling at fixed (r₁, q) moves it by 0.017 (∂sts/∂c = −1.74 per unit); a change of 0.01 in the within-pair asymmetry |a_x − a_y| moves it by 0.031, a population rate the finite-sample estimators reproduce only in part (Results 4). Per standard deviation of the data's own variation (0.0125 for regional r₁, 0.196 for pair |q|) the r₁ rate is 0.076 nats and the |q| rate 0.037, a ratio of 2.05 to 1 (4.9 with the window-level scatter of one region's r₁, 0.03, in place of the regional SD). sts is even in q, decreasing in |q| and steeply increasing in r₁ (Fig 2a), and |∂sts/∂r₁| exceeds |∂sts/∂q| at every grid point except the line r₁ = 0, so the family has no region in which q contributes comparably. The dependence is visible pair by pair inside a single window: across the 6,555 pairs of one window MMI-sts correlates with pair r₁ at r = +0.742 (r² = 0.55), and over all 392 windows the regression of pair sts on (r₁, |q|, |a_x − a_y|) has a mean R² of 0.808 (standardised coefficients +0.66, −0.46, −0.43) against 0.462 for r₁ alone and 0.796 for the full prediction from each pair's (a_x, a_y, q); under CCS the same correlation with r₁ is −0.011 (Results 6).

Lagged interaction moves sts non-monotonically. On the symmetric VAR(1) pair x_{t+1} = a x_t + c y_t + ε, y_{t+1} = a y_t + c x_t + η, with a and the innovation correlation re-solved so that r₁ and q stay at (0.85, 0.25) (Methods), sts falls from 1.2588 at c = 0 to 1.2315 at c = +0.02 and 1.2155 at c = +0.05, then rises to 1.2569 at c = +0.10, and for negative c it rises (1.3023 at −0.02, 1.4111 at −0.05): positive lagged coupling lowers the atom called synergy at the data's operating point (Fig 2c). The six cross-prediction atoms stay at zero for every c, and the population cross-lag correlation departs from a_y q by 0.94c.

Table 1 gives the data's whole-brain atom table beside the family's prediction from each pair's measured (a_x, a_y, q) (ts_gsr, W = 60; the sensitivity variant in S3 Text). The family reproduces the structure: the excess sts − (xtx + yty) is negative in the data (−0.0844) as the asymmetric family predicts (−0.1003); rts and str (0.5669, 0.5667) lie below xtx and yty (0.6273, 0.6125) as predicted (0.5756, 0.5750 below 0.6617, 0.6470); the mirror atoms sit at −0.5350 to −0.5358 against a predicted −0.5747 to −0.5750; and under DMT every atom moved as the family predicts. What the family leaves is a definite pattern — the two self-prediction atoms 0.035 below it, the six cross-prediction atoms at ±0.023 where the family has ±0.003, the four mirrors 0.04 above it, sts 0.053 below it — the signature of lagged structure that the diagonal family lacks, which Results 4 locates; the residual DiDs are within ±0.0014 for every atom except sts (+0.0115, the residual of Results 4) and their sum (+0.0092).

### 2. The DMT contrast and its prediction from the pairs' measured autocorrelations

Whole-brain MMI-sts fell under DMT, and the fall is the size that the pairs' own autocorrelation change predicts. The one confirmatory test of this paper is the pre-specified DiD of whole-brain MMI-sts on the primary variant and estimator (Table 2): −0.0809 nats [−0.1261, −0.0377], exact sign-flip p = 0.0038, negative in 13 of 14 subjects, −7.0 % of the pre-injection DMT mean, −0.0649 [−0.0966, −0.0289] after residualisation on framewise displacement. The pre-specified direction was an increase, so under the directional-failure rule recorded with the hypothesis this is a refutation of the up-regulation hypothesis. The contrast held without global signal regression (−0.1031 [−0.1558, −0.0522], p = 0.0026), at W = 30 (−0.0686, p = 0.0042), in all 28 leave-one-out refits, and at the global fit (−0.0801 [−0.1310, −0.0315], p = 0.0071).

The sts change predicted from each pair's measured (a_x, a_y, q) alone, with no free parameter, is −0.0924 [−0.1444, −0.0414], the observed sign and 14 % more than the observed magnitude; per subject the predicted and observed DiDs correlate at r = 0.99 (Fig 3b). The mechanism's input is the autocorrelation contrast: the whole-brain mean regional r₁ fell relative to placebo by −0.0146 [−0.0251, −0.0052] (sign-flip p = 0.0106, negative in 12 of 14; −0.0216 [−0.0319, −0.0116] without global signal regression), while mean pair |q| moved by −0.016, worth 0.003 nats at the operating point. The r₁ contrast is given no physiological reading here; the placebo run's own r₁ and sts rise across the session and carry 0.34 and 0.40 of the two DiDs, so the change on the DMT run alone (sts −0.0485 [−0.0819, −0.0113], p = 0.027; Table 2) is the drug-only estimate. Across subjects the sts and r₁ contrasts share their reliable variance: both are read off the same 60-TR windows, so their full-set correlation, r = 0.953 (Spearman 0.903; 0.846 to 0.973 with any two subjects dropped), contains estimation error they share, and the cross-half correlation — each subject's sts DiD on the odd windows against the r₁ DiD on the even windows, and the reverse — is 0.742 and 0.645, mean 0.694, against a ceiling of 0.729 set by the two quantities' split-half reliabilities (0.717, 0.741): a disattenuated 0.95.

A post-hoc check applied the same DiD to the ratio sts / TDMI in four pre-specified variant × estimator combinations (S8 Table): three are proportional (W = 60, +0.0005 [−0.0081, +0.0083], p = 0.91, and −0.0095, p = 0.22; the global fit on the sensitivity variant, +0.0071, p = 0.56) and the global fit on the primary variant is less than proportional (+0.0204 [+0.0048, +0.0340], p = 0.025; sts carried 0.66 of the TDMI drop against a baseline share of 0.91). Proportionality is what the symmetric family predicts for a common change of r₁ (sts carries 0.99 of a change in TDMI at the operating point, against a baseline share of 0.98); with unequal changes of a_x and a_y (Results 1) that share moves, so the less-than-proportional result is consistent with the account and is not read further.

Table 5 collects every figure this paper gives for the sts change that the autocorrelation change produces, each answering a different question, beside the observed contrast; the windowed estimator absorbs part of any true change through its finite-sample bias (15–45 % of a true sts difference at W = 60 and 52–77 % at W = 30 on simulated VAR(1) pairs, which is why W = 60 was pre-specified over W = 30), so the population rows exceed the estimator rows. On these grounds the global fit is the recommended estimator for between-run contrasts, with the qualification of Results 7; W = 60 stays the primary here because it was pre-specified and the two agree.

### 3. Regional synergy follows regional autocorrelation

On this dataset the sensory–association contrast of the regional synergy map is accounted for by regional r₁. On the placebo run before injection, regional MMI-sts — the mean over a region's 114 pairs of the global-fit local sts, bins 1–8 — correlates with regional r₁ (W = 60, windows 1–4) at Pearson r = 0.863 (Spearman 0.835) over the 115 regions; per subject the correlation is +0.756 [+0.715, +0.790], positive in 14 of 14, and regional r₁, which spans 0.812–0.874, accounts for three-quarters of the between-region variance of the map (r² = 0.745; Fig 6a). The synergy-minus-redundancy map correlates with regional r₁ at +0.792 and rtr at +0.638 (rtr's spread across regions is a fifth of sts's). Partialling regional r₁ out of the group map (least squares, slope +3.09 nats per unit r₁), the contrast between sensory cortex (visual and somatomotor networks, 31 parcels) and association cortex (default-mode and frontoparietal control networks, 37 parcels) is −0.0202 nats (−0.45 SD of the map) before and +0.0037 (+0.16 SD of the residual map) after; for sts − rtr it is −0.0215 (−0.53 SD) before and −0.0014 (−0.06 SD) after; the same contrast of r₁ itself is −0.0078. Per subject the sts contrast is −0.0202 ± 0.0243 (sign-flip p = 0.006) before and +0.0007 ± 0.0203 (p = 0.90) after partialling, and the sts − rtr contrast −0.0215 (p = 0.0035) before and −0.0021 (p = 0.71) after (Fig 6b); every network mean of the residual map lies within ±0.024 nats. Regional r₁ is itself a hierarchical map — the intrinsic timescale of BOLD lengthens from sensorimotor to association cortex (Raut et al., 2020; Ito et al., 2020; the single-unit precedent is Murray et al., 2014) — so the spatial-map exposure has the same form as the reported synergy gradient (Luppi et al., 2022). Which gradient is the cause cannot be told on these data, and nothing here bears on any published map, none of which reports regional r₁ (Discussion). The spin-test p values of the regional correlations are in the note to S5 Table; no spin test is applied to the partialled map, whose two inputs are algebraically linked.

### 4. The residual diagnostic, calibrated

The prediction from each pair's measured (a_x, a_y, q) leaves a residual whose DMT contrast is within its finite-sample expectation, so the data carry no evidence of a change in lagged interaction. The diagnostic compares, per pair, subject, run and window, the observed sts with the sts of the AR(1) matrix built from the pair's measured (a_x, a_y, q), the two cross-lag correlations replaced by a_y q and a_x q (Methods); the residual is observed minus predicted, averaged over pairs. The prediction over-shoots the level by 4.3 % at W = 60 (residual −0.0489; observed 1.1377, predicted 1.1865) and by 1.1 % at the run level (−0.0137), and across the 392 windows the residual's SD is a fifth of the observed sts's (Table 4; Fig 4). Its DiD is +0.0115 [+0.0021, +0.0211], positive in 10 of 14, larger in the early post-injection windows (+0.0179) than the late (+0.0064), and the subjects whose r₁ fell more show the larger over-prediction (r = −0.78).

The diagnostic was calibrated on simulated data with known truth (Table 7; Methods): 14 subjects × 2 runs × 840 samples of 300 pairs at the data's operating point, the same estimators and statistics, and a change from sample 300 of the DMT run only — (i) a fall of the autocorrelation, (ii) a lagged symmetric coupling of either sign, (iii) both, (iv) a within-pair asymmetry with (i) — in 50 replicate datasets. On AR(1) pairs (i) gives an sts DiD of −0.0422 ± 0.0034 at W = 60 against a predicted −0.0471 ± 0.0036 and a residual DiD of +0.0049 ± 0.0017: a pure autocorrelation change produces a positive residual DiD, because the sampling scatter of the cross-lag correlations lowers the observed sts below its AR(1) prediction by an amount that grows with a and |q|, so a fall in a shrinks the over-prediction. Tested against zero, that residual has a sign-flip p below 0.05 in 82 % of replicates (mean 0.056), so a test against zero is anti-conservative under the diagnostic's own null. A coupling change (ii) lowers the residual for either sign of Δc — −0.0010, −0.0052, −0.0109 and −0.0055 at Δc = +0.01, +0.02, +0.03 and −0.02 (± 0.0016 to 0.0018), roughly as Δc², because the first-order response of sts to c is odd in q and averages out over pairs of either sign of q; (iii) is additive to first order (+0.0009 ± 0.0016); and the asymmetry (iv) leaves the residual at its (i) value (+0.0051 ± 0.0018) while lowering the level by only 0.002 at W = 60 and 0.019 at the global fit against a population difference of about 0.045 — the finite-sample estimators reproduce the asymmetry's exchange rate only in part, the windowed one hardly at all, since its per-window estimates of a_x and a_y already scatter by 0.03. The AR(1) family's windowed bias is larger than the data's (simulated W = 60 level 0.715 against 1.155), so the calibration was repeated on a band-passed generator with the data's spectrum and operating point, quoted where the two differ: [TK: B17b — the (i) to (iv) rows of the band-passed calibration, from its committed table].

Read against that expectation, the data's residual DiD, +0.0115 [+0.0021, +0.0211], sits +0.0066 above the AR(1) family's pure-autocorrelation expectation (+0.0049), [TK: B17b] from the band-passed generator, and +0.0061 above a finite-sample null with the data's own autocorrelation function (+0.0054; +0.0037 to +0.0077 over that null's free choices), within its own interval in every case, and a coupling change of either sign would have moved it the other way. It therefore carries no evidence of a change in lagged interaction under DMT, and the pre-specified "near zero" reading of the diagnostic obtains once the reference is the calibrated expectation rather than zero; the residual is not tested against zero anywhere in this paper (Methods).

The run-level residual, −0.0137 [−0.0145, −0.0130] on the primary variant (negative in 14 of 14; +0.0008 without global signal regression), is located. Each pair's two cross-lag correlations depart from a_y q and a_x q by a symmetric part δ_sym and a directed, antisymmetric part δ_anti; the closed-form response of sts to the measured δ_anti alone is −0.0064 [−0.0069, −0.0059] and to δ_sym alone −0.0071, together −0.0137, so the run-level residual is about half directed and half symmetric (−0.0085 and +0.0092, cancelling, without global signal regression). The directed component does not change under DMT (its DiD +0.0004, p = 0.38), so it contributes nothing to the residual DiD. Under a finite-sample null with the data's autocorrelation function and no lead–lag structure the W = 60 residual level is −0.035 (observed −0.049), almost all of it the sampling scatter of the antisymmetric part; nulls with lead–lag asymmetry give −0.065 to −0.074. The symmetric departure itself, the sign(q)-weighted mean cross-lag deviation, exists at +0.0034 on the whole runs (positive in 14 of 14), exceeds finite sampling (null +0.0003 to +0.0005), is mostly present inside 2-minute windows, reverses sign without global signal regression (−0.0026), and is not separable from lagged coupling at τ = 1 (Methods); its budget and the split-half test of the residual's relation to CCS-sts, undetermined at the two quantities' reliabilities, are in S3 Text.

### 5. Lag dependence

A longer lag reduces everything, the artefact included. With the lag-τ four-vector the mean lag-τ autocorrelation r_τ falls from 0.848 at τ = 1 to 0.513, 0.139 and −0.202 at τ = 2, 3 and 5, the sts level falls with it (1.1554, 0.1797, 0.0262, 0.0415) and so does the sts contrast (−0.0809, −0.0429, −0.0043, −0.0024; Table 6, Fig 5), which is significant wherever the lag-τ autocorrelation contrast is (τ = 1, 2, 3) and absent where it is not (τ = 5, p = 0.37). Relative to the sts level at each lag the contrast is −7 %, −24 % and −16 % at τ = 1, 2, 3, so in relative terms it does not weaken; the lag-τ autocorrelation contrast itself grows in absolute size with τ (−0.0146, −0.0468, −0.0681), but the three intervals overlap ([−0.0251, −0.0052], [−0.0754, −0.0174], [−0.1120, −0.0213]), so that growth is a statement about point estimates. Per subject the sts contrast tracks the lag-τ autocorrelation contrast (r = 0.95, 0.97, 0.63 at τ = 1, 2, 3; −0.83 at τ = 5, where r₅ is negative and every AR(1) atom is even in the autocorrelation); at τ = 3 the tracking is partial, either a limit of the map where ∂sts/∂r is small or a second remainder, and the data do not say which. DMT changes the shape of the regional autocorrelation function towards a faster decay, of which r₁ is the lag-1 summary (the in-band spectral centroid rises by +0.0023 Hz, p = 0.022). No lag moves the pairs to a point where q contributes comparably, because no such point exists on the family at BOLD cross-correlations, and a longer lag is not recommended as a remedy.

### 6. CCS: the synergy atom is near zero and its DMT change has a mechanical reading

Under CCS redundancy the synergy atom is near zero and does not follow autocorrelation. With the published double-redundancy definition (Methods), whole-brain CCS-sts is −0.048 nats in the DMT pre-injection windows where MMI-sts is 1.155; the self-prediction atoms and their contrasts are almost unchanged from MMI (xtx 0.615, yty 0.601), rts, str and the four mirror atoms are near zero, so sts ≈ xtx + yty holds under MMI only (S3 Text). Across the 6,555 pairs of a window CCS-sts is uncorrelated with pair r₁ (r = −0.011 and −0.018 where MMI-sts gives +0.742 and +0.697), across the 28 condition × window group means it correlates with mean r₁ at −0.64 where MMI-sts gives +0.98, and per subject its DiD is not significantly correlated with the r₁ DiD in any variant × estimator combination (r = −0.42 to −0.28), whereas the CCS self-prediction contrast is (r = 0.92–0.96); CCS-sts has low reliability (split-half 0.30), so the zero correlations bound its dependence on r₁ rather than exclude it.

CCS-sts rose under DMT in every combination (Table 3): +0.0044 [+0.0004, +0.0081] at the primary estimator (sign-flip p = 0.056, positive in 11 of 14) and +0.0197 [+0.0142, +0.0254] at the global fit (13 of 14), with +0.0120 and +0.0355 on the sensitivity variant, surviving the motion and trend controls. The increase has a mechanical reading. CCS-sts is exactly −(1 − s) c̄_rej per pair, where s is the share of samples at which the five local signs agree (the mask; Methods) and c̄_rej the mean double co-information over the rejected samples, so its DMT contrast splits exactly into a share term, a co-information term and their interaction. At the global fit on the primary variant the increase, +0.0197, is carried by the co-information term, +0.0174 [+0.0127, +0.0224] — c̄_rej fell under DMT by 0.0292, in 14 of 14 subjects — with the share term at +0.0028 (s rose by 0.042) and the interaction at −0.0005; at W = 60 the split is +0.0059 − 0.0018 + 0.0002 (s fell there), and the same reading holds in every combination (S3 Text). CCS-sts rises toward zero under DMT because the double co-information of the sign-disagreeing samples shrinks in magnitude, not because fewer samples are rejected. The result is exploratory: its sign is the direction the study originally pre-specified for synergy, but CCS-sts is a specification chosen after the results were seen, and it is not read as a DMT finding. Its per-subject relation to the diagnostic's residual is r = +0.80 on the primary variant at W = 60, +0.09 at the global fit, and +0.88 and +0.68 on the sensitivity variant (Fig 3c); both are computed from the same windows, and the split-half test meant to tell shared signal from shared window noise could not, at their reliabilities (S3 Text). ΦR, the quantity of the workspace account, did not change (+0.0007 [−0.0078, +0.0100], p = 0.90; Table 3).

### 7. Remedies

Prewhitening does not remove the property on band-passed data, deconvolution leaves it in place at this TR, and the run-level fit is the estimator to prefer for between-run contrasts. Replacing each region's series by the residuals of its own AR(p) fit, p by BIC in 1–5, selects p = 5, the top of the range, for 3,218 of the 3,220 region × run series, and the residuals are not white: their mean lag-1 autocorrelation at W = 60 is 0.2625 and 0.2583 on the two variants. MMI-sts on the whitened series is 0.22 (from 1.155), its DMT contrast −0.026 [−0.059, +0.005] (sign-flip p = 0.14, negative in 8 of 14; −0.027, p = 0.17 on the sensitivity variant), and the diagnostic still accounts for most of what remains: the prediction from each pair's whitened (a_x, a_y, q) gives 0.243 of the observed level 0.277 and −0.019 of the contrast −0.026. The AR(1) residual is no remedy at all: its lag-1 autocorrelation is 0.75 — for a series with autocorrelations ρ₁, ρ₂ the AR(1) residual has lag-1 autocorrelation ρ₁(ρ₁² − ρ₂)/(1 − ρ₁²), 0.75 for the pooled placebo function (0.868, 0.539), whose decay is faster than geometric — and its sts contrast is −0.062 (p = 0.0006). CCS-sts on the AR(p) residuals falls under DMT (−0.0135 [−0.0217, −0.0042], p = 0.016) where the raw CCS-sts rose; exploratory (S11 Table). The remedy is incomplete because the series is band-limited: 99.2 % of its power lies inside 0.01–0.08 Hz, where ideal flat-spectrum noise at TR 2 s has r₁ = 0.82, so a residual with r₁ = 0.26 can only be one in which the AR polynomial has amplified the out-of-band residue (its in-band share of power is [TK: B16b], against 0.28 for white noise), and whitening within the band is not whitening. Prewhitening is also not free: it can remove haemodynamic structure that carries neural information (Honari et al., 2019), on HCP fMRI a mutual-information test's false-positive rate rose from 42 % to over 88 % after AR(p) prewhitening (Cliff et al., 2021), and band-pass filtering itself reintroduces autocorrelation (Arbabshirani et al., 2014). HRF deconvolution, tried post hoc (S2 Text), lowered the window-level r₁ from 0.848 to 0.783 and the sts level from 1.155 to 0.832 and left the contrast in place (−0.0782 [−0.1182, −0.0424], p = 0.0013): at TR 2 s it changes the operating point, not the mechanism.

The run-level fit is preferred for a contrast between separately fitted runs or groups on manufacture grounds (Table 8): between two simulated conditions of equal analytic sts and different covariance, the windowed estimator manufactures a difference of −9 % to +16 % of the observed contrast at W = 60 and up to +33 % at W = 30, while at 840 samples it manufactures ≤ 2 % in three of the four matched pairs (+29 % in the fourth), and its run-level residual is 1.1 % of the level (0.1 % on the sensitivity variant). Two qualifications. First, the global fit's per-bin values are local atoms under a single run-level covariance, so a pre/post contrast within one run — the contrast this paper runs — is exposed to the within-run variance change (the ratio of window variance to run variance goes from 1.28 to 0.89 across the DMT run, and the global-fit sts bins correlate with it at +0.37 per run), which is why the global-fit contrast is quoted as agreement between two estimators and the CCS global-fit values are read with caution. Second, the global fit on the primary variant is where sts fell less than proportionally to TDMI, which unequal changes of a_x and a_y explain (Results 2); the recommendation rests on manufacture alone. Null-model normalisation of atoms (Liardi et al., 2025: each atom read as a quantile against an ensemble of systems with the same total mutual information, developed for PID on MEG and named there as a future extension to ΦID) is the ΦID authors' stated direction; on the family the dependence it addresses is r₁'s route into sts, since TDMI = 2S depends on r₁ alone.

## Discussion

### What the finding is and is not

The analysis says nothing about whether synergy in the intended sense is present in the brain; what it establishes is what the estimator responds to. On the bivariate AR(1) family the Gaussian-MMI synergy atom is the two self-prediction atoms plus the double redundancy, balanced by four negative atoms of the kind the ΦID authors note MMI can produce (Mediano et al., 2025, SI Appendix; Luppi et al., 2026), and with unequal coefficients MMI takes the smaller self-information, so that the atom falls below the sum of the two — which is the pattern the data's own atom table shows once each pair's measured coefficients are put into the family (Table 1). The atom therefore inherits the dependence of self-information on lag-1 autocorrelation, at rates that can be stated in the data's units: at the operating point of BOLD region pairs, 0.01 of r₁ is worth 0.061 nats of sts, 0.1 of |q| is worth 0.019, and per standard deviation of the data's own variation the two stand at 2 to 1. Lagged interaction, the thing the atom is meant to capture, moves it non-monotonically, and at the data's operating point a small positive coupling lowers it. What follows for a contrast between states is narrow and definite: when the compared conditions differ in r₁, the MMI-sts contrast moves with that difference at the stated rate, whether or not anything the word synergy is meant to capture has changed.

On this dataset the prediction from each pair's measured autocorrelations and lag-0 correlation reproduces the DMT contrast in sign and, within 14 %, in size (−0.092 predicted, −0.081 observed), the two contrasts share their reliable variance across subjects (cross-half correlation 0.69 against a ceiling of 0.73), and the regional synergy map follows regional r₁ so closely that its sensory–association contrast vanishes when r₁ is partialled out. The residual the diagnostic leaves is positive, and a test of it against zero would call it significant; the calibration shows that a pure autocorrelation change produces a positive residual of that size, that such a test is anti-conservative, and that a change in lagged coupling of either sign would have lowered the residual. Read against its calibrated expectation the residual DiD is within it, so the data carry no evidence of a change in lagged interaction under DMT. The run-level residual of the level, −0.014 nats, is located: about half of it is the directed (antisymmetric) part of the cross-lag correlations and half the symmetric part, the two cancelling without global signal regression, and the directed part does not change under DMT. What acts inside the 2-minute windows to produce the symmetric part is not identified (lagged coupling and a shared slow component are one account at τ = 1), and its sign reversal without global signal regression is an unknown.

Three limits on the account's reach are stated as such. The closed form is exact on the family and an approximation on these data: with each pair's measured coefficients it reproduces the atom table to within 0.05 nats for sts and 0.04 for the mirror atoms, and the residual pattern (Table 1) is lagged structure that a diagonal family lacks. The exchange rate for within-pair asymmetry is a population statement that the finite-sample estimators reproduce only in part. And the CCS increase under DMT, which has the sign the study originally pre-specified for synergy, stays exploratory: its mechanical reading is what the data show, and a pre-specified test on an independent dataset would settle whether it is a drug effect.

### The literature

Two uses of MMI-sts should be distinguished. A contrast between states, between groups, or across a session is exposed wherever the compared conditions differ in r₁: BOLD lag-1 autocorrelation rises in propofol sedation and falls in deep anaesthesia and in disorders of consciousness (Huang et al., 2018), under DMT as this dataset shows, and within a placebo run with time (the placebo run's r₁ drift carries 0.34 of the DiD here); between-group designs are exposed to any group difference in r₁ from physiology, motion, age or scanner. A spatial map of synergy across regions is exposed through regional differences in r₁: higher r₁ gives higher sts with rtr almost unmoved, so a regional autocorrelation gradient produces a synergy-minus-redundancy gradient of the same spatial form, and on this dataset three-quarters of the between-region variance of the baseline synergy map is regional r₁ and the sensory–association contrast does not survive partialling it out (Results 3). The applicability table (S3 Text), which lists nine empirical studies (Down et al., 2026; Gatica et al., 2024; Luppi et al., 2022, 2023, 2024, 2026; Nago et al., 2026; Tarchi et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025), gives every study — all nine read in full; Tarchi et al. (2026) does not state its redundancy function — the same conditional statement, the sign of r₁ change that would produce its reported effect on the map; that statement is what the map predicts, not evidence about any study, since none of them reports regional r₁, and none is singled out.

Luppi et al. (2022) falls outside the TR range the plan fixed for the table, a scoping rule. By the map it would be the most exposed dataset in the table: at its stated TR of 0.72 s and 0.008–0.09 Hz band (Luppi et al., 2022, Methods) the ideal-band-pass r₁ is ≈ 0.97, where ∂sts/∂r₁ = 32.8 against ∂rtr/∂r₁ = 0.06 (S3 Text); its measured regional r₁ is not reported. Its primary analysis is HRF-deconvolved (Wu et al., 2013), the gradient is replicated without deconvolution (its Extended Data Fig. 2c), and the authors report that deconvolution 'had negligible effects on synergy and redundancy calculations' at that TR; our own evidence that deconvolution leaves the mechanism in place (S2 Text) comes from TR 2 s data and does not transfer to TR 0.72 s. That study also contains the one test of the exposure in the table: its authors recomputed synergy and redundancy on phase-randomised surrogates that keep each region's autocorrelation and remove the correlations between regions (Luppi et al., 2022, Methods; their Table 1). On the family that surrogate sits on the q = 0 line, where MMI sts of a pair is twice the self-information of its less autocorrelated member and rtr is zero, so a region's surrogate synergy strength is a monotone function of its r₁ and the surrogate gradient is, up to noise, the regional r₁ gradient. Its correlations with the macroscale maps were small and, with one exception (gyrification index, ρ = 0.26, spin p = 0.028), not significant, against 0.40–0.54 for the observed gradient. On that dataset, therefore, the autocorrelation-only gradient does not reproduce the observed gradient's macroscale associations; that is evidence against the exposure for those associations, and it is the only such evidence in the table. It leaves open what this paper measures on its own data (Results 3), which that study does not report. Two limits on reach follow from rtr + sts = TDMI and ∂rtr/∂r₁ ≈ 0. A change in q moves synergy and redundancy oppositely by equal amounts while a change in r₁ leaves rtr almost untouched, so on the family synergy and redundancy moving in opposite directions (Down et al., 2026) is the q signature or a mixture, not the r₁ signature — a statement about the family, which the data follow only loosely (here rtr fell by a tenth of the sts fall where the family gives a hundredth), and the one place the table argues against the mechanism. And ΦR = rtr = −½ ln(1 − r₁²q²) on the symmetric family falls with a fall in either factor, so the result for sts does not carry over to ΦR (Luppi et al., 2024, 2026); here ΦR did not change under DMT. Among the studies in the table, Luppi et al. (2022) tested an autocorrelation-preserving null as described above; none of the others addressed autocorrelation, and Varley (2024) is the study whose subject it is.

### Recommendations

Report the mean lag-1 autocorrelation beside sts, per condition and subject, and its contrast beside the sts contrast, with the effective sample size it implies (Bartlett, 1935; Afyouni et al., 2019). Predict each pair's sts from its measured (a_x, a_y, q) and read the residual against a calibration of the diagnostic at the study's own operating point, not against zero. For a contrast between separately fitted runs or groups, prefer the run-level fit; a pre/post contrast within one run under a single fit is exposed to within-run variance change. Do not expect prewhitening to remove the property on band-passed data: an AR(p ≤ 5) residual keeps a lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.26 here, the sts contrast on it is still predicted by that residual autocorrelation, and prewhitening carries its own costs (Honari et al., 2019; Cliff et al., 2021). Use CCS as the comparison whose synergy atom is near zero and does not follow r₁, with the mask stated. Where a null is wanted, use one that preserves each region's autocorrelation and removes the cross-correlations, as Luppi et al. (2022) did; on the family that null sits at q = 0, where the atom is twice the smaller self-information.

### Limitations

One dataset, one drug, one parcellation, N = 14, TR 2 s, no deconvolution in the primary analysis; the DMT contrast is a proof of concept. There is no ground truth for synergy in these data, so the analysis establishes what the estimator responds to, not what the brain does. Two redundancy functions were examined, both Gaussian. On Gaussian systems every PID whose redundancy depends only on the source–target marginals reduces to MMI when the target is univariate (Barrett, 2015; Kay & Ince, 2018), which covers the lattice nodes with a single past or single future variable but not the joint-target nodes or the double redundancy, which are ΦID's own extensions (Mediano et al., 2021), and the multiscale transfer decomposition of Faes et al. (2017) is a different object; so "MMI-specific" is not a licensed phrase, and other Gaussian PIDs could share or not share the property. The diagnostic's calibration is on generators solved to this dataset's operating point and spectrum, not on a second dataset; the AR(1) family does not fit the data beyond lag 1, the coupled family is symmetric, what acts inside the windows to produce the symmetric cross-lag departure is not identified, and the windowed estimator's finite-sample manufacture is not removed by the diagnostic. And the history of the study bears on how its statements should be weighed: the confirmatory test was of a different hypothesis, recorded in a git-tracked record before the primary analysis; its directional-failure rule was written after a 115-region global fit had shown a decrease; the analysis plans of the estimator account were written after an adversarial review's computations had characterised the phenomenon, and every later computation was entered in the record with its rule and prediction before it was run and its outcome appended afterwards; the git record establishes ordering, not blindness. The full history is S5 Text.

## Materials and methods

### Ethics statement

The original study was approved by the National Research Ethics Committee London – Brent and the Health Research Authority and conducted under the revised Declaration of Helsinki (2000), the ICH Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the NHS Research Governance Framework, sponsored by Imperial College London under a Home Office licence for research with Schedule 1 drugs, with written informed consent from all participants. This wording follows the approval statement of Timmermann et al. (2023), which names the committee but gives no reference number [TK: the REC reference number, to be obtained from C. Timmermann]. The present work is a secondary analysis of anonymised, parcellated regional time series and per-TR framewise displacement released by the data authors (Singleton et al., 2025); no new data were collected and no participant was contacted.

### Dataset

Data are the preprocessed Schaefer-116 regional time series released by Singleton et al. (2025), acquired by Timmermann et al. (2023): fourteen subjects (six of twenty excluded by the source authors for head movement), each with a DMT run and a placebo (PCB) run of 840 TRs at TR = 2 s, intravenous injection at TR 240 (20 mg DMT fumarate over 30 s; 3 T). Drug order was counterbalanced across the two testing days, two weeks apart: half of the participants received placebo on the first day and DMT on the second, the other half the reverse (Timmermann et al., 2023; Singleton et al., 2025); the analysed runs are the first, task-free session of each day. The intensity ratings supplied with the data enter no result here (S1 Text); their 28 bins of 30 TRs are the global fit's averaging unit, and framewise displacement (FD) per TR is supplied. Two preprocessing variants were analysed: with global signal regression (ts_gsr, primary) and demeaned without it (ts_demean, sensitivity); both are band-limited to 0.01–0.08 Hz, which alone imposes r₁ ≈ 0.82 on white noise at TR 2 s. The parcellation is Schaefer-100 (Schaefer et al., 2018; Yeo et al., 2011) plus the 16 subcortical parcels of Tian et al. (2020). Region 20 (0-based; LH_DorsAttn_Post_6), constant across the DMT run of subject 8 in every variant and confirmed by the data authors as a mean-filled replacement, was dropped for all subjects and both conditions before any result was computed, leaving 115 regions and 6,555 pairs; one non-finite TR was dropped. The release carries no licence file; the data are used with the written agreement of the data collectors and the derivative authors.

### Estimator

ΦID atoms were computed with phyid (Imperial-MIND-lab, 2026, at the commit pinned in the repository), calc_PhiID(kind='gaussian', tau=1) with redundancy 'MMI' or 'CCS': a Gaussian model of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}), sixteen local atoms per time point, in nats; whole-brain values are means over the 6,555 pairs. Windowed estimator, W = 60: 14 non-overlapping 60-TR windows per run, each an independent fit on its own samples; W = 30 (28 windows) as a second windowed estimator. Global fit: one Gaussian fitted to the full run, local atoms averaged into the 28 bins. By Bartlett's variance formula the effective sample size of a correlation between two series with these autocorrelations is n / (1 + 2 Σ_k ρ_k²) (Bartlett, 1935; Afyouni et al., 2019); over the six lags of the pooled placebo function (0.868, 0.539, 0.172, −0.085, −0.174, −0.145) the denominator is 3.3, so a 60-TR window holds about 18 effective samples. The windowed estimator's bias was characterised before any windowed result on simulated VAR(1) processes with analytic ground truth (2,000 replicate windows per setting; Results 2). The analytic work used a closed-form implementation of the same estimator (window-mean MMI atoms are closed-form functions of the window's 4 × 4 correlation matrix; CCS atoms are evaluated sample by sample), which reproduces phyid to ≤ 2.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ on every saved window and bin mean. Software: Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1, Matplotlib 3.11.1, pinned.

### Redundancy functions

Under MMI each single-target redundancy is the smaller of the two single-source mutual informations (Barrett, 2015) and the double redundancy rtr is the smallest of the four single-source, single-target mutual informations (Mediano et al., 2021, Appendix, Definition 2). Under CCS (Ince, 2017) a single-target redundancy is the local co-information at the samples where the constituent local mutual informations and the co-information share a sign, and zero elsewhere; the ΦID double redundancy under CCS is the pointwise double co-information c(x; y) summed over the samples at which all marginal pointwise mutual informations and the pointwise full mutual information i(x; y) share a sign (Mediano et al., 2021, Appendix, Definition 1; 2025, SI Appendix, Definition 2), and since c ≡ rtr − sts, CCS-sts is 0 at the selected samples and −c elsewhere. phyid applies a different fifth sign condition (that of c itself); the two masks disagree on 6–7 % of samples, their whole-brain CCS-sts levels differ by 0.002–0.012 nats and their DMT contrasts correlate at r = 0.97 per subject; every CCS value here is under the published definition (the check in S3 Text). CCS is computed with respect to the fitted Gaussian of the four-vector, as in phyid and in Mediano et al. (2025), which for two Gaussian sources is Ince's published construction (Ince, 2017, Definition 2).

### The closed form and the coupled family

For unit-variance stationary AR(1) processes x_t = a x_{t−1} + ε_t, y_t = a y_{t−1} + η_t with corr(x_t, y_t) = q, the correlation matrix of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}) is S₄ = [[1, q, a, aq], [q, 1, aq, a], [a, aq, 1, q], [aq, a, q, 1]]. With S = −½ ln(1 − a²) and C = −½ ln(1 − a²q²), its block determinants give I(x_t; x_{t+1}) = S, I(x_t; y_{t+1}) = C, every single-source joint-target and joint-source single-target mutual information S, and TDMI = 2S; MMI sets the four single-source, single-target redundancies to C, the two joint-target ones to S, and rtr = C, and the Möbius inversion of Mediano et al. (2021), a linear system with no sign constraint, gives the atoms of Results 1, with ∂sts/∂a = 2a/(1 − a²) − aq²/(1 − a²q²) and ∂sts/∂q = −a²q/(1 − a²q²); the identities were checked numerically to 4 × 10⁻¹⁵ over 2,000 seeded draws. For unequal coefficients the same pipeline evaluates the matrix with cross-lag entries a_y q and a_x q numerically; the family-predicted atoms of Table 1 are these at each pair's measured (a_x, a_y, q), averaged over pairs. The exchange rates of Results 1 are central differences at (0.85, 0.25); the asymmetry rate holds the mean coefficient at 0.85 and is one-sided at zero. The symmetric VAR(1) pair x_{t+1} = a x_t + c y_t + ε, y_{t+1} = a y_t + c x_t + η was evaluated from its stationary covariance with a and the innovation correlation re-solved for each c so that r₁ and q stay at the operating point. A pair sharing a slow component gives the same τ = 1 signature, and the two are not separable at that lag: any positive-definite stationary 4 × 4 matrix of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}) is the lag-0/lag-1 structure of a stable VAR(1) with A = Γ₁Γ₀⁻¹ and innovation covariance Γ₀ − AΓ₀Aᵀ (S3 Text), so the sixteen τ = 1 atoms of the two processes coincide in population.

### The scope map and the regional maps

sts and its two derivatives were evaluated on r₁ ∈ [0, 0.95] × q ∈ [−0.6, 0.6] (step 0.01) and on |q| ≤ 0.95, and every pair of one subject was placed on the map (r₁ = the mean of the two regions' within-window lag-1 autocorrelations, q = the pair's within-window lag-0 correlation). The within-window regression fits pair sts on (mean r₁, |q|, |a_x − a_y|) across the 6,555 pairs of each of the 392 windows. The regional map is the mean over a region's 114 pairs of the global-fit local sts on the placebo run, bins 1–8, averaged over subjects, against the region's windowed r₁ (W = 60, windows 1–4), with a positive correlation predicted before the computation; the partialled map regresses the group map on regional r₁ (least squares with intercept) and reports the residual map by Yeo-7 network and subcortex and the sensory minus association contrast before and after, with a per-subject version and its exact sign-flip p. The spin test of the regional correlations (Alexander-Bloch et al., 2018, with the rotations of Váša et al., 2018, released with the data; 10,000 rotations of the 99 cortical parcels) gives the p values in the note to S5 Table; none is applied to the partialled map, whose two inputs are algebraically linked.

### The DMT contrast and its inference

Per subject the statistic is (post − pre) on DMT minus (post − pre) on PCB for the whole-brain mean of a quantity: pre-injection windows 1–4 (bins 1–8), post windows 6–14 (bins 11–28). Window 5 was excluded before any windowed result because both arms of the global fit showed an injection response at bins 8–10 (the injection TR 240 is a window boundary at both window lengths); the sensitivity set (windows 5–14), the early and late sets (6–9, 10–14) and two trend corrections were added after the primary result. Inference: an exact sign-flip permutation over the 14 subjects (16,384 assignments, two-sided) and a mean DiD with a subject-bootstrap 95 % percentile interval (10,000 draws); bias-corrected and accelerated intervals for the 29 intervals the text quotes differ from them by less than 10 % of their width (largest ratio 1.04), so the percentile intervals stand. A phase-randomised surrogate of the TR-resolution whole-brain local series per subject and condition (1,000 surrogates; the Fourier surrogate of Theiler et al., 1992), re-averaged into the same windows, is reported in the tables as a stationarity check of the derived series and carries no inference; motion control regressed window-mean values on window-mean FD within each subject and condition and recomputed the DiD on the residuals. The mean regional lag-1 autocorrelation is the sample r₁ of each region within the window, averaged over the 115 regions; ΦR is TDMI − I(X;X′) − I(Y;Y′) + rtr; the lag variants use the lag-τ four-vector for τ = 2, 3, 5. Seed 20261120 throughout.

### Confirmatory and exploratory analyses

The pre-specified DiD of whole-brain MMI-sts on ts_gsr at W = 60 is the single confirmatory test of this paper. Every other quantity is exploratory and is reported with its effect size and interval, without threshold language; a sign-flip p quoted for an exploratory quantity describes the sample and is not a claim. An earlier weighting rule (both nulls at p ≤ 0.005 across variants), adopted at write-up after the results it governed existed, has been withdrawn (S5 Text).

### The residual diagnostic and its calibration

For every pair, subject, run and window, the measured 4 × 4 correlation matrix gives the observed sts; the prediction keeps the pair's measured a_x, a_y and q and replaces the two cross-lag correlations by a_y q and a_x q, and the residual is observed minus predicted, averaged over pairs, with the same inference run on all three series. Because the τ = 1 atoms depend only on lag-0 and lag-1 correlations, the diagnostic depends on the AR(1) assumption only through that substitution. The analysis plan recorded before the run named two outcomes, a residual DiD near zero or one both significant and uncorrelated with r₁; its test against zero, the pre-specified branch rule, is not applied, because the calibration shows it anti-conservative, and the residual is read against its calibrated expectation.

The calibration simulates 14 subjects × 2 runs × 840 samples of 300 independent pairs, runs them through the same estimators (W = 60; the global fit with its run-level prediction and a period-level prediction from the pre and the post samples separately) and statistics, and applies a change from sample 300 of the DMT run only — (i) a fall of the autocorrelation; (ii) a lagged symmetric coupling of +0.01, +0.02, +0.03 and −0.02; (iii) both; (iv) a within-pair asymmetry of 0.03 with (i) — in 50 replicates per condition. Two generators: AR(1) pairs with a_x, a_y ~ N(0.85, 0.0125), q from the data's window-level distribution, Δa = −0.015 and the coupling as a VAR(1) cross-coefficient; and a band-passed generator, a smooth 0.01–0.08 Hz band-pass with a low-frequency tilt exp(−β f²) applied to two white noises with correlation q, the per-pair distributions of β and q solved once so that the W = 60 window-level mean pair a and mean |q| equal the DMT pre-injection operating point (0.8632, 0.2842), a post-injection tilt solved to a window-level mean a of 0.8482 (the data's Δr₁), the change applied by filtering each run's white noises at both parameter sets and splicing at sample 300, the coupling added after filtering on the post samples (re-standardised; those rows are read by their δ_sym DiD) and the asymmetry as a tilt difference solved to a window-level a_x − a_y of 0.03 (S3 Text). Where the two generators differ the text quotes the band-passed one. The same construction with no change, solved to the data's four run × period operating points, is the finite-sample null of the residual, whose free choices give the range quoted in Results 4.

The directed and symmetric parts of the cross-lag deviation are, per pair, d_xy = corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) − a_y q and d_yx = corr(y_t, x_{t+1}) − a_x q, δ_sym = (d_xy + d_yx)/2 and δ_anti = (d_xy − d_yx)/2; the response of sts to each is the closed-form sts of the AR(1) matrix with only that deviation added, per pair, averaged. The sign(q)-weighted mean of d with its budget, null and controls, the split-half test, and the CCS decomposition (CCS-sts = −(1 − s) c̄_rej per pair and period, its change split exactly into c̄_pre Δs, −(1 − s_pre) Δc̄ and Δs Δc̄) are described with their rules in S3 Text.

### Remedies

Prewhitening replaced each region's series, per run, by the residuals of its own AR(p) fit by ordinary least squares (p by BIC over 1–5, or p = 1), the first p TRs dropped, and reran the atoms, the diagnostic and the DMT contrast unchanged; the residual series' power shares inside, below and above 0.01–0.08 Hz were computed from the periodogram of each run's kept samples, with fixed orders 10 and 20 for comparison and no atoms at those orders. HRF deconvolution used rsHRF 1.7.0 (Wu et al., 2013, 2021) with the pipeline rerun unchanged (S2 Text). Manufacture (Table 8) was assessed on matched pairs of simulated conditions with equal analytic sts and different covariance (the four pairs named in the table; S3 Text), the difference estimated over 4,000 independent windows at W = 30, 60 and 840 as a share of the observed contrast.

### Literature search for the applicability table

Published fMRI ΦID studies reporting Gaussian-MMI synergy on BOLD at TR ≈ 1–3 s were identified by web search on 14 September 2026 and their full texts read on 20 September 2026 for the quantity reported, preprocessing, parcellation, redundancy function, lag and any treatment of autocorrelation. The TR range was fixed before the search, a scoping rule that places Luppi et al. (2022) outside the set at its TR of 0.72 s (Discussion). For each study the table states the sign of the r₁ change that, on the map, would produce the reported effect, in the same conditional form for every study, not as a claim about any study's data. The search record and the table are in S3 Text.
