# The Gaussian-MMI synergy atom of integrated information decomposition is mostly self-prediction: a closed form on the AR(1) family and a within-subject DMT fMRI application

## Abstract

Integrated information decomposition (ΦID) with the minimum-mutual-information (MMI) redundancy function underlies most fMRI synergy reports we found. We derive in closed form the sixteen Gaussian-MMI atoms of two first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) processes coupled only at lag 0. With equal coefficients the synergy atom sts is both self-prediction atoms plus the double redundancy; with unequal coefficients it is less. sts inherits self-information's dependence on lag-1 autocorrelation r₁: at the data's operating point 0.01 of r₁ moves it by 0.061 nats, 0.1 of the lag-0 correlation |q| by 0.019 (4.7 to 1 per within-window standard deviation; neither is lagged coupling, which can move it either way). In within-subject fMRI of 14 volunteers given N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) and placebo — a proof of concept, its statistic fixed after whole-run fits of these data showed a decrease — whole-brain MMI-sts fell relative to placebo (difference-in-differences −0.081 [−0.132, −0.031], p = 0.004, 13 of 14; −0.054 adjusted for the runs' pre-injection gap) with r₁, near a generator's −0.094 for that fall; across subjects the two DMT-minus-placebo differences covary (cross-half r 0.69 [0.26, 0.89], disattenuated 0.95), partly through that gap. Regional sts follows regional r₁ (r = 0.86). With AR(1) cross-lags substituted at each pair's measured autocorrelations and lag-0 correlation, sts falls 0.0115 more than observed, 0.006–0.009 above simulated pure autocorrelation changes: a test detecting 0.0155 does not resolve this (p = 0.11–0.25), but across subjects it scales with the r₁ change more steeply than either generator under the subjects' own changes (no replicate as steep); its source is unidentified. Under the common-change-in-surprisal (CCS) redundancy function the atom is near zero and, on the family, nearly flat in r₁; prewhitening removes r₁ only at orders leaving mostly stop-band residue, where a smaller contrast remains. We recommend reporting r₁ beside sts and each contrast beside its AR(1)-substituted estimate.

## Author summary

A widely used measure of how brain regions carry information together, from integrated information decomposition, is synergy: information that a pair of regions holds jointly and neither holds alone. Synergy has been reported to differ across the cortex, and integrated information in synergy-ranked regions to fall under anaesthesia and in disorders of consciousness. We show, on a solvable family and in one dataset, that with the estimator these studies use the synergy value's level, its pattern across regions and its change between conditions follow something simpler: how well each region's signal predicts its own next sample, its autocorrelation. The dataset is fourteen volunteers scanned under the psychedelic DMT and placebo. Under DMT, relative to placebo, the signals' autocorrelation fell and the measured synergy fell by about as much as a simulation with the data's properties gives for that fall; across volunteers the two differences went together (synergy rose in one volunteer, autocorrelation in two). We had set out to test the opposite hypothesis, that DMT raises synergy; it fell instead, and we report why. We recommend that studies report autocorrelation beside synergy and compare each synergy contrast with the estimate our closed form gives from each pair's autocorrelations and correlation.

## Introduction

Integrated information decomposition (ΦID; Mediano et al., 2021, 2025) extends partial information decomposition (PID; Williams & Beer, 2010) to the time-delayed mutual information (TDMI) of two variables, splitting it into sixteen atoms that map each kind of past information (redundant, unique, synergistic) to each kind of future information. In fMRI most attention has gone to synergy-to-synergy, sts, read as information that only the two regions' joint past carries about their joint future. On resting-state blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) signals its regional rank minus redundancy's separates redundancy-dominated sensory and motor cortex from a synergy-dominated association cortex, the 'synergistic core' (Luppi et al., 2022). Regions where synergy predominates over redundancy, by rank, form a 'synergistic workspace' whose integrated information (ΦR) is reduced under propofol and in disorders of consciousness (Luppi et al., 2024, who name their synergy the persistent synergy and give its formula as their Eq. 5, the four-atom sum of Results 1). Of the ten studies read (S20 Table), eight state the minimum-mutual-information (MMI) redundancy function (Barrett, 2015; Mediano et al., 2021), five naming a Gaussian estimator, and one uses the common-change-in-surprisal (CCS) function (Ince, 2017) on binarised signals in its primary analysis (Luppi et al., 2023); where the primary lag is stated it is one repetition time (TR; Luppi et al., 2023; Down et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026), on BOLD series whose lag-1 autocorrelation is high because of haemodynamic smoothing and band-pass filtering.

Barrett (2015) exhibited positive net synergy, hence positive MMI synergy since MMI redundancy is non-negative, in a dynamical Gaussian example with a target's own immediate past among the sources (his Example 1; with the infinite pasts as sources the net synergy vanishes while the MMI synergy stays at ½ ln(1 + α²), his Eqs. 73 and 79–82). MMI's aggregate synergy, the same four-atom sum, cannot tell a synergistic pair from two independent autocorrelated processes (Varley, 2024, Eqs. 4–6 and 12), and PID applied to time series rests on an assumption of independent, identically distributed samples that is often violated (Faes et al., 2025). Exact Gaussian decompositions exist for transfer entropy (Faes et al., 2017), for static systems (Barrett, 2015; Kay & Ince, 2018) and for single-target lagged PIDs of multivariate autoregressive processes with a target's own past among the sources (Barrett, 2015, Sec. V), and the ΦID authors' worked example is a coupled autoregressive pair (Mediano et al., 2021, Fig. 5 and Appendix VI); none gives the sixteen atoms in closed form, and so none says which atoms take their value from the self-information, how sts depends on a pair's lag-1 autocorrelation r₁ and lag-0 correlation q, where real region pairs sit on that dependence, whether CCS shares the property, or how much of an observed sts change the pairs' own autocorrelations carry.

This study did not set out to provide these. It was planned as a directional test of whether whole-brain synergy, measured as MMI-sts, is up-regulated under N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT), a drug that produces a state of intensified conscious content; the predicted increase extrapolates from the synergistic-workspace account, in which loss of consciousness goes with reduced ΦR within the workspace (Luppi et al., 2024), rather than being its prediction. MMI-sts decreased (Results 2; Methods gives the test's history); the decrease was carried by the pairs' self-prediction atoms and went with the lag-1 autocorrelation contrast, and the closed form, for two first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) processes coupled only at lag 0 (Results 1), was derived afterwards, motivated by the same data, to account for it; the rest of the Results apply it to the data. Apart from the primary contrast every analysis is exploratory, and the account itself is post hoc.

## Results

Notation: an atom's letters give the kind of past and future information (r redundant, x or y unique to region X or Y, s synergistic); values are in nats; a difference-in-differences (DiD) is (post − pre) on DMT minus (post − pre) on placebo, per subject. Lag-1 autocorrelation r₁ is estimated two ways: regional r₁, a region's sample lag-1 autocorrelation within a window (its mean over regions is whole-brain r₁), and pair r₁, the mean of a pair's two lag-1 correlations, a_x and a_y, in its window's 4 × 4 correlation matrix, the quantity the simulations report and the AR(1)-substituted estimate uses; on the family both equal a. The AR(1)-substituted estimate of an atom is its value in the pair's 4 × 4 correlation matrix with the two lag-0 entries set to their mean q and the two measured cross-lag correlations replaced by their AR(1) values, a_y q and a_x q (Methods). W = 60 denotes the primary estimator's 60-TR windows, and a global fit one fit over a whole run.

### 1. The sixteen atoms in closed form

Let x and y be unit-variance AR(1) processes with lag-1 autocorrelation a (= r₁) whose innovations are correlated, so that their lag-0 correlation is q and corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) = aq, with no lagged interaction. With S = −½ ln(1 − a²), the self-information of one process, and C = −½ ln(1 − a²q²), the cross-lag mutual information, the Möbius inversion of the ΦID lattice under MMI gives (Methods)

rtx = rty = xtr = ytr = xty = ytx = 0,   xtx = yty = rts = str = S − C,   xts = yts = stx = sty = −(S − C),   rtr = C,

sts = 2S − C = −ln(1 − a²) + ½ ln(1 − a²q²).

Hence, exactly for all (a, q),

sts − (xtx + yty) = rtr,   rtr + sts = TDMI = 2S,   ΦR = rtr,

with TDMI depending on r₁ alone, and str + stx + sty + sts = S for every q: the whole-minus-max synergy (Luppi et al., 2024, Eq. 5) and Varley's (2024) aggregate synergy (his Eq. 3), which at q = 0 is his identity for a disintegrated pair (his Eq. 12). The four mirror atoms, xts, yts, stx and sty, are negative. That MMI-ΦID can produce negative atoms is noted by its authors (Mediano et al., 2025, SI Appendix, Sec. III.A; Luppi et al., 2026).

With unequal coefficients the MMI minima select the less self-predictive member. At q = 0, sts = 2 min(S_x, S_y) exactly and rtr = 0, so sts lies below xtx + yty = S_x + S_y by |S_x − S_y|. On a grid of mean a 0.80–0.90 and q 0.05–0.70, str equals the smaller self-prediction atom, rts is at least as large, and sts ≤ xtx + yty + rtr, with equality only at equal coefficients; the excess sts − (xtx + yty), which is rtr at equal coefficients, falls with the asymmetry and changes sign at |a_x − a_y| = 0.008 at (0.85, 0.25) (S3 Text).

At the operating point of these data's region pairs, (r₁, q) = (0.85, 0.25) (Fig 1a), 0.01 of r₁ moves sts by 0.061 nats (∂sts/∂r₁ = +6.07), 0.1 of |q| by 0.019 (∂sts/∂q = −0.19; sts is even in q), and 0.01 of within-pair asymmetry |a_x − a_y| by 0.031. Per standard deviation (SD) of the pairs' variation within a window (0.0284 for pair r₁ and 0.196 for |q|; S3 Text), the r₁ rate is 4.7 times the |q| rate, and 3.3 times across pairs averaged over subjects and windows. In the windowed estimator's own within-window regression of pair sts on r₁, |q| and |a_x − a_y| (S3 Text) the slopes, +5.13 per unit of r₁ and −0.53 per unit of |q|, make the per-SD ratio 1.4; the |q| slope is 2.8 times the population rate, consistent with a finite-sample bias that grows with |q|.

### 2. The DMT contrast

Whole-brain MMI-sts fell under DMT relative to placebo, and across subjects the two DMT-minus-placebo differences, of sts and of lag-1 autocorrelation, went together. The primary contrast is the pre-specified DiD of whole-brain MMI-sts on the primary variant and estimator (Table 2): −0.0809 nats [−0.1317, −0.0310], exact sign-flip p = 0.0038, negative in 13 of 14 subjects, −7.0 % of the pre-injection DMT mean. It was planned as a test of an increase: the increase failed whatever the two-sided p, and under the directional-failure rule the significant decrease refutes the hypothesis as operationalised. Its two-sided statistic was fixed after global fits on the same data had shown the decrease (Methods), and on the account of Results 1 it bears on MMI-sts, not on synergy in the intended sense. The DiD is one of three readings (Table 2): the runs' pre-injection gap, DMT minus placebo, +0.0176 (p = 0.2307), lies in the direction that enlarges the DiD (the scrutiny S2 Text applies to the deconvolved ΦR contrast); the post-injection gap is −0.0633 (p = 0.0001, negative in 14 of 14); and the baseline-adjusted contrast, the post-injection gap expected at a pre-injection gap of zero, is −0.0544 [−0.0806, −0.0283]. The DiD's between-subject variance is mostly the gap's (the DiD's SD 0.0887 against the post-injection gap's 0.0485; r(DiD, pre-injection gap) = −0.888, r² = 0.79), and the one positive DiD belongs to the subject with the most negative gap. The DiD stays the primary statistic, as pre-specified; it estimates the drug's effect only if the runs' pre-injection difference would have persisted. The contrast held on the sensitivity variant (Table 2), at W = 30 (−0.0686, p = 0.0042), on windows 5–14 (−0.0733, p = 0.0070; S1 Table) and, post hoc, in each leave-one-out refit on each variant (p ≤ 0.008); the global fit, seen before the test was written, gives −0.0801 [−0.1362, −0.0251], p = 0.0071.

Whole-brain r₁ (a post hoc quantity) changed relative to placebo by −0.0146 [−0.0261, −0.0037] (p = 0.0106, negative in 12 of 14; −0.0216 without global signal regression), and pair r₁ by −0.0155; the phase-randomised p of the r₁ contrast is 0.073, against 0.002 for sts (S3 Text). Mean pair |q| fell by 0.0164 (10 of 14 negative; the count is logged, the per-subject vector not saved), which would raise sts by 0.003 nats at the population rate and by 0.009 at the windowed estimator's own rate (Results 1). r₁'s three readings are negative too (Table 2: the post-injection gap −0.0122, in 13 of 14; the baseline-adjusted contrast −0.0110), its pre-injection gap +0.0025 (p = 0.4417). Both runs contribute: the placebo run's r₁ and sts rose across the session and carry 0.34 and 0.40 of the two DiDs, which assume that drift would have occurred on the DMT run too; the DMT run's own change (sts −0.0485, p = 0.027; Table 2) contains time in the scanner as well as the drug. Residualising the window means on framewise displacement removes a fifth of the sts contrast (−0.0649 [−0.1010, −0.0260]) and a sixth of r₁'s (−0.0123), and removes with motion any drug effect that coincides with the post-injection rise of framewise displacement (Table 2).

Across subjects the two contrasts covary (Fig 3a, b). Both are read off the same windows, so their full-set correlation, r = 0.953 [0.854, 0.985], contains shared estimation error; the cross-half correlation, each subject's sts DiD on one half of the windows against the r₁ DiD on the other, is 0.694 on average (approximately [0.258, 0.895]; Fig 3b), against a ceiling of 0.729: disattenuated, 0.95 [0.62, 0.99] (a subject bootstrap of 10,000 draws, the 366 in which the split-half reliability of either DiD was not positive left out; the ratio is not bounded by one), so that at the interval's lower limit they share 38 % of their reliable variance. Part of the covariation is the runs' pre-injection difference rather than two drug-induced changes: the sts pre-injection gap correlates with the r₁ DiD at r = −0.844, the two post-injection gaps at +0.939, and the two DiDs' partial correlation given the sts pre-injection gap is +0.824. The relation shows that the estimator tracks r₁ in every window, not that the subjects' drug effects on sts and on r₁ covary. ### 3. The regional map

On the placebo run before injection, regional MMI-sts (global fit; S3 Text) correlates with regional r₁ (windowed, windows 1–4) at r = 0.863 over the 115 regions (r² = 0.745, Spearman 0.835; Fig 4a) and at r = 0.807 over the 99 cortical parcels (Spearman 0.771, spin p < 1/10,000: no rotation reached the observed value); per subject +0.756 [+0.715, +0.790] (a subject-bootstrap percentile interval), positive in 14 of 14; with the windowed estimator's own regional atoms, on the same windows, r = 0.898 (post hoc; S3 Text §4). The map's slope on regional r₁, 3.09 nats per unit (2.645 per subject; 2.30 for the windowed atoms), is about half the family's 6.07 for a change of both members' r₁: with unequal coefficients sts follows the less self-predictive member (Results 1), so a region's r₁ moves a pair's sts at nearly the full rate where that region is the less autocorrelated member and hardly at all where it is the more, and the regional slope averages over both. Partialling regional r₁ out of the map, the contrast of sensory (visual, somatomotor) against association cortex (default-mode, frontoparietal control), fixed in the record before the partialled map was computed (S19 Table), goes from −0.0202 nats to +0.0037, and per subject from −0.0202 [−0.0342, −0.0062] to +0.0007 [−0.0110, +0.0124] (t intervals from the saved mean and SD): it vanishes at its point estimate, but the interval admits just over half of it (Fig 4b). Network structure remains in the residual map: its eight-class F lies in the upper 0.1 % of its own spin distribution (p = 0.0009, below the range predicted for it, S19 Table; not comparable with the unpartialled map's 0.0564, since partialling a smooth covariate changes the map's spatial autocorrelation; the 16 subcortical values are fixed in every rotation), though its somatomotor − default and visual − default contrasts do not stand out (Fig 4c). rtr follows regional r₁ at r = 0.638 with a slope of 0.50 nats per unit, about nine times the family's 0.056, which these data do not explain. The intrinsic timescale of BOLD lengthens from sensorimotor to association cortex (Raut et al., 2020; Ito et al., 2020; in macaque single units, from sensory to prefrontal cortex, Murray et al., 2014), but here the network means of regional r₁ differ little (somatomotor 0.849, default 0.848, visual 0.840, the lowest cortical network); which gradient is the cause cannot be told on these data.

### 5. Lag dependence

A longer lag reduces everything, the r₁-dependence included (Fig 6; S14 Table): with the lag-τ four-vector, as the mean lag-τ autocorrelation r_τ falls from 0.848 at τ = 1 to 0.513, 0.139 and −0.202 at τ = 2, 3 and 5, the sts level follows |r_τ| (1.1554, 0.1797, 0.0262, 0.0415) and the sts contrast shrinks (−0.0809, −0.0429, −0.0043, −0.0024; p = 0.0038, 0.0037, 0.0018, 0.37), tracking the lag-τ autocorrelation contrast per subject, only partly at τ = 3 and with the sign reversed at τ = 5, where r₅ is negative. Relative to placebo, the regional autocorrelation function moves under DMT towards a faster decay, of which r₁ is the lag-1 summary (the in-band spectral centroid rises by +0.0023 Hz, p = 0.022; post hoc). No lag moves the pairs to where q contributes comparably, and a longer lag is not a remedy.

### 6. CCS: a near-zero synergy atom, nearly flat in r₁

Under CCS whole-brain sts is −0.048 nats (MMI 1.155): the CCS synergy atom is negative in the data and on the family (−0.015 to −0.036 nats on the grid of S18 Table); CCS is examined as a second redundancy function, not offered as a replacement. On the symmetric AR(1) family at fixed q it is nearly flat in r₁ (−0.013 nats per unit at (0.85, 0.25), against +6.07 for MMI-sts, and not monotone; S18 Table). In the data its relation to r₁ is weak and, where estimated, negative: across the pairs of one window of one subject CCS-sts does not track pair r₁ (r = −0.011; MMI-sts +0.742); across the 28 condition × window group means, which follow the drug's time course, it moves inversely with mean r₁ (r = −0.64; MMI-sts +0.98); and its per-subject DiD correlates with the r₁ DiD at −0.43 to −0.27 in the four variant × estimator cells, each Fisher-z interval including zero at N = 14, with a split-half reliability of 0.30.

### 7. Remedies

Prewhitening trades the dependence for the stop-band (Table 4). At TR 2 s any series confined to 0.01–0.08 Hz has r₁ ≥ cos(2π × 0.08 Hz × 2 s) = 0.54 (flat in-band noise has 0.82), so a whitened series with r₁ below 0.54 must carry power outside the band, which the whitening filter can only have amplified from the stop-band residue. AR(1) residuals stay in the band and keep most of the level and of the contrast; AR(p ≤ 5) residuals carry a third of their power above the band, a fifth of the level and a third of the contrast, whose interval then includes zero and whose correlation with the whitened r₁ DiD across subjects is +0.495; at p = 10 and 20 most of what remains of the series lies outside the band it was filtered to, so the atoms are those of the amplified residue rather than of the band-limited signal, and a contrast remains there: at p = 20, −0.0127 [−0.0203, −0.0052] (p = 0.0002, negative in 13 of 14), 18 % of the whitened level against 7 % for the raw series; its correlation with the whitened series' own r₁ DiD (−0.0927) is +0.333 (the Fisher-z interval of neither correlation excludes zero), and the AR(1)-substituted estimate carries −0.0037 of it (S3 Text §8). The remedy the released derivatives cannot test is whitening before the band-pass, or no band-pass. Prewhitening's other reported costs are in S3 Text §8 (Cliff et al., 2021; Honari et al., 2019; Arbabshirani et al., 2014).

## Discussion

### What the finding is and is not

The analysis says nothing about whether synergy in the intended sense is present in the brain: these data have no ground truth; what it establishes is what the estimator responds to. On the family of two AR(1) processes coupled only at lag 0 the Gaussian-MMI synergy atom is the two self-prediction atoms plus the double redundancy, and with unequal coefficients it falls below that sum, the pattern the data's atom table shows (Results 1); it inherits the dependence of self-information on lag-1 autocorrelation, which outweighs |q| 4.7-fold per within-window standard deviation in population and 1.4-fold in the windowed estimator, while lagged interaction, the thing the atom is meant to capture, can move it either way. What follows for a contrast between states is narrow and definite: when the compared conditions differ in r₁, the MMI-sts contrast moves with that difference, at a rate that depends on the estimator (S13 Table), whether or not anything the word synergy is meant to capture has changed.

On this dataset MMI-sts fell with r₁ relative to placebo (−0.081 as a difference-in-differences, −0.054 adjusted for the runs' pre-injection gap); across subjects the two DMT-minus-placebo differences covary (cross-half r = 0.69, disattenuated 0.95), in part through that gap (partial r = +0.82 given the sts pre-injection gap); the group-level fall is close to a generator's for the same fall of pair r₁ (−0.094); the regional map follows regional r₁; and the AR(1)-substituted estimate carries the contrast. The remainder, the 0.0115 nats by which that estimate falls more than the observed sts, is not distinguished from the 0.003–0.005 of a simulated pure autocorrelation change (excesses of 0.006–0.009) by a test that detects 0.0155; across subjects it scales with the r₁ change at −0.75 per unit, more steeply than either generator when each simulated subject's autocorrelation change is the data's, as a step or as a ramp (no replicate as steep). What it is — lagged interaction, a weakening of slow shared fluctuations, or an autocorrelation change of a shape the generators lack — is not identified (Results 4).

### The fall of r₁ under DMT

Why r₁ fell is not identified here, and the fall is not an artefact: the atom's dependence on r₁ is the estimator's property; the fall of r₁ is a property of the data and may be the drug's effect on the signal. On the same dataset the data authors refer to DMT's "decreased autocorrelation between adjacent BOLD activity" and "increased temporal entropy" (Singleton et al., 2025, SI Figure 14); under DMT the EEG's signal diversity rises and its alpha power falls (Timmermann et al., 2019, 2023), as MEG signal diversity rises under psilocybin, ketamine and LSD, most in its temporal form (Schartner et al., 2017); here the regional autocorrelation function decays faster under DMT (Results 5), and within the DMT run the whole-brain TDMI, a function of r₁ alone on the family, fell as the EEG's Lempel–Ziv complexity rose (per-subject Spearman ρ over 28 bins −0.24, percentile interval [−0.40, −0.08], one-sided p = 0.002 against a phase-randomised null; on the placebo run −0.05, p = 0.22, or −0.09, p = 0.045, without global signal regression), a prediction recorded before it was computed (S1 Text; S6 Table), though a coherence the shared drug time course alone could produce. A faster-decaying BOLD autocorrelation is the fMRI form of that family of findings; we found no published application of ΦID to psychedelic data (Methods; S3 Text §10; Liardi et al., 2025, is a PID on MEG under LSD, ketamine and psilocybin). Non-neural routes are open too: DMT raises blood pressure and heart rate on the time course of its subjective effects (Strassman & Qualls, 1994); framewise displacement was higher under DMT than under placebo after the injection (Timmermann et al., 2023, Methods and SI Appendix, Fig. S4; its DiD here +0.0143, p = 0.25) in series that are motion-censored with replaced volumes (Methods), and residualising on it removes a fifth of the sts contrast (Results 2). The account needs only that r₁ changed; an MMI-sts contrast reports that change, whatever its origin.

### The literature

Two uses of MMI-sts should be distinguished. A contrast between states, between groups or across a session is exposed wherever the compared conditions differ in r₁: BOLD lag-1 autocorrelation rises in propofol sedation and falls in deep anaesthesia and in disorders of consciousness (Huang et al., 2018); between-group designs are exposed to any group difference in r₁ from physiology, motion, age or scanner. A spatial map of synergy across regions is exposed through regional differences in r₁: higher r₁ gives higher sts with rtr moving much less (∂rtr/∂r₁ = 0.06 on the family; in these data's regional map rtr's slope on r₁ is 0.50, still a sixth of sts's 3.09), so a regional autocorrelation gradient produces a synergy-minus-redundancy gradient of the same spatial form. The applicability table (S20 Table) lists the ten empirical studies we found (Down et al., 2026; Gao et al., 2026; Gatica et al., 2024; Luppi et al., 2022, 2023, 2024, 2026; Nago et al., 2026; Tarchi et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025); the search was not systematic until the database search of 1 October 2026 (Methods). For every study within the table's scope it gives the same conditional statement, the sign of r₁ change that would produce the reported effect on the sts surface of Fig 1a; the primary quantity of Luppi et al. (2023), CCS emergence capacity on binarised signals, is outside the scope (S3 Text §11), and the main text of Tarchi et al. (2026) does not state its redundancy function. The statement is what that surface predicts, not evidence about any study: none reports regional r₁.

The human (HCP) data of Luppi et al. (2022), at TR 0.72 s, fall outside the table's TR range, but per unit of regional spectral difference the exposure is almost the same at every TR (S3 Text §4, §10). That study's phase-randomised surrogates, which on the family give the regional r₁ gradient up to noise, are the table's one test of the exposure, and they argue against it for the six macroscale associations of their Table 1 on that dataset (surrogate |ρ| ≤ 0.26, one significant, against 0.22–0.54 observed; S20 Table), with less force than that comparison suggests, since at q = 0 the redundancy term of their rank gradient is finite-sample noise (S3 Text §10). On the family a change in q moves synergy and redundancy oppositely by equal amounts and a change in r₁ moves mainly sts, so synergy and redundancy moving in opposite directions (Down et al., 2026; in part, in the regional classification of Tarchi et al., 2026) is the q signature or a mixture, not the r₁ signature, while higher whole-brain synergy with redundancy unchanged (Gao et al., 2026, in heart failure) is the pattern a group difference in r₁ gives; and ΦR = rtr on the symmetric family falls with a fall in either factor, so the result for sts does not carry over to ΦR (Luppi et al., 2024, 2026). Of the studies in the table, in the texts we read, only Luppi et al. (2022) addressed temporal autocorrelation (in part); it is the subject of Varley (2024). The one lead this dataset offers for an independent test is CCS-sts's rise under DMT (Results 6), whose per-subject change at W = 60 correlates with the residual's at r = 0.80 (its global-fit change at 0.09; a split-half test left undetermined whether what they share is signal; S3 Text §6): a specification chosen after the results, a hypothesis for a pre-specified test elsewhere.

### Recommendations

Report the mean lag-1 autocorrelation beside sts, per condition and subject, and its contrast beside the sts contrast, with the effective sample size computed from the auto- and cross-correlation functions (Afyouni et al., 2019). Report each sts contrast beside its AR(1)-substituted estimate from each pair's measured (a_x, a_y, q); the difference between the two is not a measure of lagged interaction unless a calibration reproduces the study's own relation between that difference and the autocorrelation change. Compare exposure across datasets per unit of spectral difference, not per unit of r₁, since ∂sts/∂r₁ grows without bound as r₁ approaches 1. On band-passed data the prewhitening orders that remove r₁ leave mostly stop-band residue, with a lower level and a smaller contrast (Results 7): whiten before band-pass filtering or do not band-pass, a remedy the released derivatives cannot test, and report the in-band power share beside any prewhitened atom. Check CCS contrasts against r₁ too (Results 6). Where a null is wanted, simulate surrogate series that preserve each region's autocorrelation and the pair's lag-0 correlation — from each pair's AR(1)-substituted matrix, or from the two spectra with the measured lag-0 correlation — and pass them through the same estimator; the AR(1)-substituted value itself is not a null, since its difference from the observed sts has a non-zero finite-sample expectation (Results 4). A null that removes every cross-correlation sits at q = 0, where sts is at its maximum for a given r₁, and so tests lag-0 correlation rather than lagged interaction.

### Limitations

One dataset, one drug, one parcellation, N = 14, TR 2 s, no deconvolution in the primary analysis; the DMT contrast is a proof of concept, and the runs' pre-injection gap gives it three readings (Results 2). Why r₁ fell (neural, haemodynamic, cardiac, respiratory or motion effects) is not identified (Discussion). The released series are motion-censored: each volume with framewise displacement above 0.4 was replaced by the mean of its neighbours (Methods); 23.6 TRs per run crossed the threshold under DMT and 10.1 under placebo, the count per window changed by +1.12 after the injection relative to placebo (p = 0.2166, positive in 7 of 14), its DiD correlates with the r₁ DiD at r = −0.107 across subjects (+0.037 without global signal regression) and a window's count with its r₁ at r = −0.124 within runs (−0.025; S3 Text §5): the replacement, which raises r₁ where it acts, works against the observed fall of r₁ rather than for it (the positive within-run relation it predicts was not found). Each subject's session order, counterbalanced by the source study, is not among the released files, so the placebo run's drift (Results 2) could not be examined by order. Two redundancy functions were examined on the data, both Gaussian; whether other Gaussian PIDs share the property is open, Barrett's (2015) reduction of such PIDs to MMI holding for univariate targets and not for ΦID's joint-target nodes or its double redundancy (S3 Text §2). The calibration of the AR(1)-substituted estimate is on generators solved to this dataset's operating point and spectrum, their change a step or a two-window ramp, not on a second dataset or the placebo series with a known change written in; the AR(1) family does not fit the data beyond lag 1 (S14 Table), the coupled family is symmetric, what produces the symmetric cross-lag departure inside the windows is not identified (S9 Table), and the substitution does not remove the windowed estimator's manufacture. The closed form, exact on the family, matches the data's atom table with each pair's measured coefficients only to within 0.053 nats for sts and 0.04 for the mirror atoms, and its exchange rate for within-pair asymmetry is a population statement that the estimators recover only in part (S10 Table). The account was derived after the primary contrast, on the same data (Methods); several recorded predictions failed, among them the calibration's for a coupling change, after which a reading of the residual was built and has been withdrawn (S19 Table; S5 Text §1). The git record establishes ordering, not blindness.

## Materials and methods

### Ethics statement

The original study was approved by the National Research Ethics Committee London – Brent and the Health Research Authority, with written informed consent from all participants (Timmermann et al., 2023; S1 Text) [TK: the REC reference number, and the basis on which the approval or the release covers secondary use, to be obtained from C. Timmermann]. This is a secondary analysis of derivatives released by the data authors (Singleton et al., 2025); the quantities it uses carry no demographic, image or identifying field, no new data were collected and no participant was contacted (S1 Text).

### Dataset

Data are the preprocessed regional time series released by Singleton et al. (2025), acquired by Timmermann et al. (2023): fourteen subjects, each with a DMT and a placebo run of 840 TRs at TR = 2 s, injection at TR 240, with global signal regression (ts_gsr, primary) and without it (ts_demean, sensitivity) (S1 Text), both band-passed to 0.01–0.08 Hz; Schaefer-100 parcels (released with Schaefer et al., 2018) in Yeo-7 networks (Yeo et al., 2011) and 16 subcortical ones (Tian et al., 2020), one region, mean-filled for one subject, dropped for all: 115 regions and 6,555 pairs (COBIDAS checklist: S4 Text). The release's preprocessing replaced each volume with framewise displacement above 0.4 by the mean of the surrounding volumes (Timmermann et al., 2023; Singleton et al., 2025, Methods and Reporting Summary), and six of the twenty participants were excluded for more than 20 % of such volumes (Singleton et al., 2025); one non-finite TR, the last of subject 3's placebo run, at the end of window 14 (on ts_gsr the only one; S3 Text §5), is dropped (S1 Text).

### Estimator

Atoms were computed with phyid (Imperial-MIND-lab, 2026; calc_PhiID, Gaussian, τ = 1) and averaged over pairs, in 14 non-overlapping, separately fitted 60-TR windows per run (W = 60; W = 30 as a second) or in one global fit per run, its local atoms averaged into 28 bins. For two series with these autocorrelations and zero true correlation, the effective sample size of their correlation, n / (1 + 2 Σ_k ρ_k²) (the global form reviewed by Afyouni et al., 2019, their Eq. 8; Bartlett, 1935, gave the AR(1) case), is about 18 in a 60-TR window over six lags of the pooled placebo function (S3 Text). A second implementation, computing the atoms directly from each window's 4 × 4 correlation matrix, reproduces phyid to ≤ 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ on every saved window and bin mean, ≤ 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ on the regional values and 1.0 × 10⁻¹³ on the TR-local series checked.

### Redundancy functions

MMI takes each single-target redundancy as the smaller single-source mutual information (Barrett, 2015; Mediano et al., 2021) and rtr as the smallest of the four (Mediano et al., 2021, Appendix, Definition 2). CCS (Ince, 2017) is a single-target redundancy function; its ΦID extension (Mediano et al., 2021, Appendix, Definition 1; 2025, SI Appendix, Definition 2) takes the double redundancy as the pointwise double co-information at the samples whose marginal and full pointwise mutual informations share a sign; phyid applies a different fifth condition (S3 Text). The prewhitened CCS values of S11 Table, and those S3 Text, S17 and S19 Tables label as phyid's, use phyid's mask; every other one uses the published definition.

### The closed form

For unit-variance stationary AR(1) processes the correlation matrix of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}) is

R₄ = [[1, q, a, aq], [q, 1, aq, a], [a, aq, 1, q], [aq, a, q, 1]];

its block determinants give I(x_t; x_{t+1}) = S, I(x_t; y_{t+1}) = C, every single-source joint-target and joint-source single-target mutual information S, and TDMI = 2S. MMI sets the four single-source single-target redundancies to C and the two joint ones — the redundancy of the two separate pasts about the joint future, min(I(x_t; x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}), I(y_t; x_{t+1}, y_{t+1})), and its time-reverse — to S, even at q = 0, where x and y are independent: under MMI equal amounts of information count as redundant whatever their source, its redundancy not depending on the correlation between the sources (Barrett, 2015, Fig. 3). With rtr = C, the Möbius inversion of Mediano et al. (2021) puts S − C at rts and str, balances it with the four negative mirror atoms and leaves 2S − C to sts (checked to 4 × 10⁻¹⁵; S3 Text). With unequal coefficients the cross-lag entries are a_y q and a_x q and the minima select the less self-predictive member; on a grid of mean a 0.80–0.90, q 0.05–0.70 and |a_x − a_y| up to 0.10 the statements of Results 1 hold to 7 × 10⁻¹⁶ (S18 Table). A pair sharing a slow component gives the same τ = 1 signature as lagged coupling, and the two are not separable at that lag: any positive-definite stationary 4 × 4 matrix of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}) is the lag-0/lag-1 structure of a stable VAR(1) (S3 Text).

### Regional maps

The partialled map is the residual of the regional map's regression on regional r₁ (S3 Text). The correlations were spin-tested with 10,000 rotations of the 99 cortical parcels (Alexander-Bloch et al., 2018; rotations by the method of Váša et al., 2018, released with the data; S5 Table), and the residual map's network structure with the same rotations, subcortex fixed (S3 Text).

### Inference

Pre-injection windows are 1–4 (bins 1–8), post windows 6–14 (bins 11–28). Inference is an exact sign-flip permutation over the 14 subjects (16,384 assignments, two-sided, a reading fixed with the test statistic after the global fit had shown the decrease: Pre-registration and deviations), which assumes the subjects' DiDs independent and symmetric about the tested mean, with a 95 % interval obtained by inverting that test (the values μ for which the test of a mean of μ has p > 0.05), so that an interval excludes zero exactly when p ≤ 0.05; the pre-specified subject-bootstrap percentile intervals were narrower, by a median of 12.5 % (S17 Table). Correlations across subjects carry Fisher-z intervals; slopes and intercepts across subjects t intervals on n − 2 degrees of freedom (12 for the 14 subjects), which assume normal residuals of constant variance; ratios of group means Fieller intervals, which assume the two means jointly normal, whose width grows with g = (t · SE/mean)² of the denominator (0.61 for the whole-brain r₁ DiD) and which are finite for g < 1. A phase-randomised surrogate (Theiler et al., 1992), pre-specified as a second null, is reported as a check of stationarity; motion control residualised window means on framewise displacement. ΦR is TDMI − I(X;X′) − I(Y;Y′) + rtr. Seed 20261120.

### The primary contrast and the exploratory analyses

The pre-specified DiD of whole-brain MMI-sts on ts_gsr at W = 60 is the primary contrast, not a blind confirmation (Pre-registration and deviations). Every other quantity is exploratory and is reported with its effect size and interval, without threshold language; a p value quoted for one describes the sample, and S19 Table says of each computation whether it had a recorded prediction or was post hoc (the text repeats it for several). The original pre-specified analysis and its exploratory results are in S1 Text and S1–S7 Tables.

### The AR(1)-substituted estimate and its calibration

The AR(1)-substituted estimate does not exist where no AR(1) pair has a window's (a_x, a_y, q); those pair-windows, 4 of 2,569,560 at W = 60 on each variant, are excluded from its means (S3 Text §6). The calibration simulates the design (14 subjects × 2 runs × 840 samples × 300 pairs, the DMT run changed from sample 300, 50 replicates) on two generators: AR(1) pairs with coefficients drawn around the data's mean window-level regional r₁ (a_x, a_y ~ N(0.85, 0.0125²)), whose windowed sts level (0.715) lies far below the data's (1.155) and whose pair r₁ in 60-sample windows is 0.786 at a = 0.85 (S18 Table), and a band-passed generator solved to the data's spectrum and to its mean pair r₁ and |q| in 60-sample windows (S3 Text; S10 Table). A third expectation comes from a finite-sample null, a band-pass filter model whose cross-lag correlations equal the AR(1) values in population, fitted to the data's autocorrelation function and solved to its window-level pair r₁ and |q| in each run × period cell (its own pair-r₁ DiD −0.0146); its +0.0054 is from a single simulation, without a replicate SD (S3 Text). The aligned statistic A_other, the residual's response to five constructions of a change in lagged structure and their pure-autocorrelation expectations on the band-passed generator (S3 Text §6; S18 Table), and the generators' own per-subject slopes under a change that differs between simulated subjects as the data's r₁ DiDs differ, as a step at the start of window 6 and as a ramp over windows 6 and 7 (S3 Text §6), were computed under rules and predictions recorded beforehand.

### Remedies

Prewhitening replaced each region's series by the residuals of its own AR(p) fit (p by BIC over 1–5, or p fixed at 1, 10 or 20; S11 Table; Table 4). HRF deconvolution used rsHRF 1.7.0 (Wu et al., 2013, 2021; S2 Text). Manufacture was assessed on four matched pairs of simulated conditions (S16 Table).
