## Abstract

Integrated information decomposition (ΦID) with the minimum-mutual-information (MMI) redundancy function underlies most fMRI synergy reports. We derive in closed form the sixteen Gaussian-MMI atoms of two first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) processes correlated only at lag 0. With equal coefficients the synergy atom sts equals both self-prediction atoms plus the double redundancy, balanced by four negative atoms; with unequal coefficients MMI takes the smaller self-information and sts falls below that sum. sts therefore inherits self-information's dependence on lag-1 autocorrelation r₁: at these data's operating point, 0.01 of r₁ moves it by 0.061 nats and 0.1 of the lag-0 correlation |q| by 0.019 (4.7 to 1 per standard deviation across pairs; 1.4 to 1 for the windowed estimator), while lagged coupling moves it non-monotonically. In within-subject fMRI of 14 volunteers given N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) and placebo, the primary contrast, the pre/post difference-in-differences of whole-brain MMI-sts, was planned to test an increase; its statistic was fixed after global fits on the same data had shown a decrease. MMI-sts fell (−0.081 nats [−0.132, −0.031], p = 0.004, 13 of 14) with r₁, close to the −0.094 of a generator with the data's spectrum and the same fall of r₁; across subjects the two changes share most of their reliable variance (disattenuated r 0.95 [0.62, 0.99]). The regional sts map follows regional r₁ (r = 0.86). Recomputed with AR(1) cross-lags at each pair's measured autocorrelations and lag-0 correlation, sts falls by 0.0115 more than observed, against 0.003–0.005 in simulated pure autocorrelation changes (p = 0.11–0.25); the difference cannot be attributed on these data. Under the common-change-in-surprisal (CCS) redundancy function the synergy atom is near zero and nearly flat in r₁ on the family, and prewhitening cannot fully remove the dependence from band-passed data. We recommend reporting r₁ beside sts and each sts contrast beside its AR(1)-substituted estimate.

## Author summary

Functional brain imaging asks how regions carry information together, not only which are active. A widely used measure, from integrated information decomposition, is synergy: information that a pair of regions holds jointly and neither holds alone. Synergy has been reported to differ across the cortex, and integrated information, in regions ranked by their synergy, to fall under anaesthesia and in disorders of consciousness. We show, on a solvable family and in one brain-imaging dataset, that with the estimator most of these studies use the synergy value is mostly something simpler: how well each region's signal predicts its own next sample, its autocorrelation. The dataset is from fourteen volunteers scanned under the psychedelic DMT and placebo. Under DMT the autocorrelation of the signals fell, and the measured synergy fell by about as much as a simulation with the data's properties gives for that fall; across volunteers the two falls went together. We had set out to test the opposite hypothesis, that DMT raises synergy as this estimator measures it; the measure fell instead, and we report why. We recommend that studies report autocorrelation beside synergy and compare each synergy contrast with the estimate our closed form gives from the regions' autocorrelations.

## Introduction

Integrated information decomposition (ΦID; Mediano et al., 2021, 2025) extends partial information decomposition (PID; Williams & Beer, 2010) to the time-delayed mutual information (TDMI) of two variables, splitting it into sixteen atoms that map each kind of past information (redundant, unique, synergistic) to each kind of future information. In fMRI most attention has gone to synergy-to-synergy, sts, read as information that only the two regions' joint past carries about their joint future. On resting-state blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) signals it separates redundancy-dominated sensory and motor cortex from a synergy-dominated association cortex, the 'synergistic core' (Luppi et al., 2022). Regions where synergy — in that study the whole-minus-max synergy (their Eq. 5), in ΦID terms str + stx + sty + sts — predominates over redundancy, by rank, form a 'synergistic workspace' whose integrated information (ΦR) is reduced under propofol and in disorders of consciousness (Luppi et al., 2024). Of the nine studies read (S20 Table), seven state the minimum-mutual-information (MMI) redundancy function (Barrett, 2015; Mediano et al., 2021), six with a Gaussian estimator, and one uses the common-change-in-surprisal (CCS) function (Ince, 2017) on binarised signals (Luppi et al., 2023); where the lag's value is stated it is one repetition time (TR; Luppi et al., 2023; Down et al., 2026), on BOLD series whose lag-1 autocorrelation is high because of haemodynamic smoothing and band-pass filtering.

Barrett (2015) exhibited positive net synergy — hence positive MMI synergy, since MMI redundancy is non-negative — in a dynamical Gaussian example in which a target's own immediate past is one of the sources (his Example 1); the net synergy vanishes when the infinite pasts are the sources, while the MMI synergy stays at ½ ln(1 + α²) (his Eqs. 73 and 79–82). MMI's aggregate synergy, the same four-atom sum, cannot tell a synergistic pair from two independent autocorrelated processes (Varley, 2024, Eq. 12), and PID applied to time series rests on an assumption of independent, identically distributed samples that is often violated (Faes et al., 2025). Closed-form Gaussian decompositions exist for transfer entropy (Faes et al., 2017), for static systems (Barrett, 2015; Kay & Ince, 2018) and for single-target lagged PIDs of multivariate autoregressive processes with a target's own past among the sources (Barrett, 2015, Sec. V), Varley (2024) gave the aggregate identity, and the ΦID authors' worked example is a coupled autoregressive pair (Mediano et al., 2021, Fig. 5 and Appendix VI); what none of them gives is which of the sixteen atoms carry the self-information, how sts depends on a pair's lag-1 autocorrelation r₁ and lag-0 correlation q, where real region pairs sit on that dependence, whether CCS shares the property, and how much of an observed sts change the pairs' own autocorrelations carry.

This study did not set out to provide these. It was planned as a directional test of whether whole-brain synergy, measured as MMI-sts, is up-regulated under N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT), a drug that produces a state of intensified conscious content; the predicted increase was motivated by the synergistic-workspace account, in which loss of consciousness goes with reduced ΦR within the workspace (Luppi et al., 2024). The record states the hypothesis before its first check on the data, an order the version history cannot confirm; the test statistic was fixed after whole-run ('global') fits on the same data had already shown a decrease (Methods). MMI-sts decreased; the decrease was carried by the pairs' self-prediction atoms and went with the lag-1 autocorrelation contrast, and the closed form was derived afterwards, motivated by the same data, to account for it. The Results give the closed form for two first-order autoregressive (AR(1)) processes correlated at lag 0, with its exchange rates in data units (1), the DMT contrast (2), the regional map (3), an estimate that keeps each pair's measured autocorrelations and lag-0 correlation (4), the lag dependence (5), CCS (6) and remedies (7). Apart from the primary contrast every analysis is exploratory, and the account itself is post hoc.

## Results

Notation: an atom's letters give the kind of past and future information (r redundant, x or y unique to region X or Y, s synergistic); values are in nats; a difference-in-differences (DiD) is (post − pre) on DMT minus (post − pre) on placebo, per subject. Lag-1 autocorrelation r₁ is estimated two ways: regional r₁, a region's sample lag-1 autocorrelation within a window (its mean over regions is whole-brain r₁), and pair r₁, the mean of a pair's two lag-1 correlations in its window's 4 × 4 correlation matrix, the quantity the simulations report and the AR(1)-substituted estimate uses; on the family both equal a. W = 60 denotes the primary estimator's 60-TR windows.

### 1. The sixteen atoms in closed form

Let x and y be unit-variance AR(1) processes with lag-1 autocorrelation a (= r₁) whose innovations are correlated, so that their lag-0 correlation is q and corr(x_t, y_{t+1}) = aq, with no lagged interaction. With S = −½ ln(1 − a²), the self-information of one process, and C = −½ ln(1 − a²q²), the cross-lag mutual information, the Möbius inversion of the ΦID lattice under MMI gives (Methods)

rtx = rty = xtr = ytr = xty = ytx = 0,   xtx = yty = rts = str = S − C,   xts = yts = stx = sty = −(S − C),   rtr = C,

sts = 2S − C = −ln(1 − a²) + ½ ln(1 − a²q²).

Hence, exactly for all (a, q),

sts − (xtx + yty) = rtr,   rtr + sts = TDMI = 2S,   ΦR = rtr,

with TDMI depending on r₁ alone, and str + stx + sty + sts = S for every q: Varley's (2024) aggregate synergy (his Eq. 3), which at q = 0 is his identity for a disintegrated pair (his Eq. 12). The four mirror atoms, xts, yts, stx and sty, are negative. That MMI-ΦID can produce negative atoms is noted by its authors (Mediano et al., 2025, SI Appendix, Sec. III.A; Luppi et al., 2026).

With unequal coefficients the MMI minima select the less self-predictive member. At q = 0, sts = 2 min(S_x, S_y) exactly and rtr = 0, so sts lies below xtx + yty = S_x + S_y by |S_x − S_y|. On a grid of mean a 0.80–0.90 and q 0.05–0.70, str equals the smaller self-prediction atom, rts is at least as large, and sts ≤ xtx + yty + rtr, with equality only at equal coefficients; the excess sts − (xtx + yty), which is rtr at equal coefficients, falls with the asymmetry and changes sign at an asymmetry |a_x − a_y| of 0.008 at (0.85, 0.25) (S3 Text).

At the operating point of these data's region pairs, (r₁, q) = (0.85, 0.25) (Fig 1a), 0.01 of r₁ moves sts by 0.061 nats (∂sts/∂r₁ = +6.07), 0.1 of |q| by 0.019 (∂sts/∂q = −0.19; sts is even in q), and 0.01 of within-pair asymmetry |a_x − a_y| by 0.031. Per standard deviation (SD) of the pairs' variation within a window (0.0284 for pair r₁ and 0.196 for |q|; S3 Text), the r₁ rate is 4.7 times the |q| rate, and 3.3 times across pairs averaged over subjects and windows. In the windowed estimator's own within-window regression of pair sts on r₁, |q| and |a_x − a_y| (S3 Text) the slopes, +5.13 per unit of r₁ and −0.53 per unit of |q|, make the per-SD ratio 1.4, the |q| slope being 2.8 times the population rate, consistent with a finite-sample bias that grows with |q|.

### 2. The DMT contrast

Whole-brain MMI-sts fell under DMT, and across subjects its fall went with the fall of lag-1 autocorrelation. The primary contrast of this paper is the pre-specified DiD of whole-brain MMI-sts on the primary variant and estimator (Table 2): −0.0809 nats [−0.1317, −0.0310], exact sign-flip p = 0.0038, negative in 13 of 14 subjects, −7.0 % of the pre-injection DMT mean; −0.0649 [−0.1010, −0.0260] after residualisation on framewise displacement. It was planned as a test of an increase of whole-brain MMI-sts at W = 60, and under the directional-failure rule the significant decrease refutes that hypothesis as operationalised; on the account of Results 1 it bears on MMI-sts, not on synergy in the intended sense. The rule and the test statistic were written after global fits on the same data had shown a decrease (Methods), so the windowed contrast measures, with a second estimator, an effect already seen. The contrast held without global signal regression (−0.1031 [−0.1629, −0.0435], p = 0.0026), at W = 30 (−0.0686, p = 0.0042), on windows 5–14 (−0.0733, p = 0.0070; S1 Table) and in each of the 14 leave-one-out refits on each variant (p ≤ 0.008); the global fit, the estimate seen before the test was written, gives −0.0801 [−0.1362, −0.0251], p = 0.0071.

### 3. The regional map

On the placebo run before injection, regional MMI-sts (global fit; S3 Text) correlates with regional r₁ at r = 0.863 over the 115 regions (Spearman 0.835) and at r = 0.807 over the 99 cortical parcels (Spearman 0.771, spin p < 0.0001); per subject +0.756 [+0.715, +0.790] (a subject-bootstrap percentile interval), positive in 14 of 14; regional r₁ accounts for three-quarters of the map's variance (r² = 0.745; Fig 4a). The map's slope on regional r₁, 3.09 nats per unit, is about half the family's 6.07 for a change of both members' r₁: a region's r₁ is one member of each of its pairs, and a pair's sts follows its less autocorrelated member (Results 1). Partialling regional r₁ out of the map, the pre-defined contrast of sensory (visual, somatomotor) against association cortex (default-mode, frontoparietal control) goes from −0.0202 nats to +0.0037, and per subject from −0.0202 [−0.0342, −0.0062] to +0.0007 [−0.0110, +0.0124] (t intervals from the saved mean and SD): the contrast vanishes at its point estimate, but the interval admits up to half of it (Fig 4b). Partialling does not remove all network structure: the residual map's eight-class F (8.15) lies in the upper 0.1 % of its spin distribution (p = 0.0009, against 0.056 for the unpartialled map), with network means from −0.024 (subcortex) to +0.021 (dorsal attention), though its somatomotor − default and visual − default contrasts do not stand out (spin p = 0.22 and 0.31; Fig 4c). rtr follows regional r₁ at r = 0.638, with a slope of 0.50 nats per unit, about nine times the family's 0.056, a difference these data do not explain. In the literature the intrinsic timescale of BOLD lengthens from sensorimotor to association cortex (Raut et al., 2020; Ito et al., 2020; in single units, Murray et al., 2014), but here the network means of regional r₁ differ little (somatomotor 0.849, default 0.848, visual 0.840, the lowest cortical network). Which gradient is the cause cannot be told on these data, and none of the studies in the applicability table (S20 Table) reports regional r₁.

### 4. What the pairs' own autocorrelations and lag-0 correlations carry

For each pair, subject, run and window, the AR(1)-substituted estimate is the sts of the 4 × 4 correlation matrix that keeps the pair's measured lag-1 autocorrelations a_x and a_y, sets its two lag-0 entries to their mean q and its two cross-lag correlations to a_y q and a_x q (Methods): the sts the pair would have if its lagged structure were that of two AR(1) processes with its measured autocorrelations and lag-0 correlation. It is the same estimator applied to the same windows with four entries replaced, so its agreement with the observed sts across subjects (r = 0.99) is largely built in and is not evidence for the account; what it measures is how much of a contrast the pairs' own (a_x, a_y, q) carry. It exceeds the observed level by 4.3 % at W = 60 and by 1.1 % at the run level (S12 Table). Its DiD is −0.0924 [−0.1503, −0.0348] against the observed −0.0809; the residual, observed minus estimate, has DiD +0.0115 [+0.0005, +0.0226], positive in 10 of 14, larger in the early post-injection windows (+0.0179) than in the late (+0.0064) (Fig 5).

### 5. Lag dependence

A longer lag reduces everything, the artefact included (Fig 6; S14 Table). With the lag-τ four-vector, as the mean lag-τ autocorrelation r_τ falls from 0.848 at τ = 1 to 0.513, 0.139 and −0.202 at τ = 2, 3 and 5, the sts level follows |r_τ| (1.1554, 0.1797, 0.0262, 0.0415), and the sts contrast shrinks (−0.0809, −0.0429, −0.0043, −0.0024; p = 0.0038, 0.0037, 0.0018, 0.37), which per subject tracks the lag-τ autocorrelation contrast, only partly at τ = 3 and with the sign reversed at τ = 5, where r₅ is negative (S14 Table). Relative to placebo, the regional autocorrelation function moves under DMT towards a faster decay, of which r₁ is the lag-1 summary (the in-band spectral centroid rises by +0.0023 Hz, p = 0.022). No lag moves the pairs to where q contributes comparably, and a longer lag is not a remedy.

### 6. CCS: a near-zero synergy atom, nearly flat in r₁

Under CCS whole-brain sts is −0.048 nats (MMI 1.155). On the symmetric AR(1) family at fixed q it is nearly flat in r₁ (−0.013 nats per unit at (0.85, 0.25), against +6.07 for MMI-sts, and not monotone; S18 Table). In the data its relation to r₁ is weak and, where estimated, negative: across the pairs of one window of one subject CCS-sts does not track pair r₁ (r = −0.011, where MMI-sts gives +0.742); across the 28 condition × window group means, which follow the drug's time course, it moves inversely with mean r₁ (r = −0.64, where MMI-sts gives +0.98); and its per-subject DiD correlates negatively with the r₁ DiD in all four variant × estimator cells (r = −0.43 to −0.27), each Fisher-z interval including zero at N = 14 and a split-half reliability of 0.30.

CCS-sts's DMT contrast was positive in every combination (S15 Table): +0.0044 [−0.0001, +0.0088] at the primary estimator (exact p = 0.056, positive in 11 of 14) and +0.0197 [+0.0133, +0.0261] at the global fit (p = 0.0002, 13 of 14). An exact decomposition of CCS-sts locates the global-fit increase in the double co-information of the samples whose local signs disagree, not in their share (S3 Text). CCS-sts is a specification chosen after the results were seen; its contrast is not read as a DMT finding. ΦR, the quantity of the workspace account, changed by +0.0007 [−0.0093, +0.0112] (p = 0.90) at the primary variant and estimator, an interval that excludes changes larger than 0.0112 nats in either direction, about 12 % of its pre-injection level (0.096); no cell's sign-flip p is below 0.05 (sensitivity variant, W = 60: +0.0157 [−0.0024, +0.0341]), and the three phase-randomised p values below 0.05 disagree in sign across variants (S15 Table).

### 7. Remedies

Prewhitening cannot fully remove the dependence from band-passed data. BOLD band-passed to 0.01–0.08 Hz holds 99.2 % of its power in the band, and at TR 2 s any series confined to the band has r₁ ≥ cos(2π × 0.08 Hz × 2 s) = 0.54 (flat in-band noise has 0.82), so a whitened series with r₁ below 0.54 must carry power outside the band, which the whitening filter can only have amplified from the stop-band residue. AR(p) residuals (p by the Bayesian information criterion, BIC, in 1–5, at the cap for 3,218 of 3,220 series) keep r₁ = 0.26 and 34 % of their power above the band, against 0.1 % in the raw series; MMI-sts on them is 0.22, its contrast −0.026 [−0.064, +0.011] (p = 0.14); AR(1) residuals keep r₁ = 0.75 and a contrast of −0.062 (p = 0.0006). Prewhitening also has costs: on Human Connectome Project (HCP) fMRI a mutual-information test's false-positive rate rose from 42 % to over 88 % after AR(p) prewhitening (Cliff et al., 2021); making a BOLD series entirely uncorrelated may remove neuronal information carried by the haemodynamic response, a concern Honari et al. (2019) raise although their own simulation found no detrimental effect; and band-pass filtering itself reintroduces autocorrelation (Arbabshirani et al., 2014).

Deconvolution of the haemodynamic response function (HRF; S2 Text) lowered r₁ from 0.848 to 0.783 and sts from 1.155 to 0.832 and left the contrast in place (−0.0782, p = 0.0013). At TR 2 s it changes the operating point, not the mechanism.

Neither estimator is free of manufacture, a difference it returns between two simulated conditions of equal true sts and different covariance: on the two data-like matched pairs the W = 60 estimator returned +7 % ± 8 % and −8 % ± 6 % of the observed contrast and the 840-sample fit +29 % and +2 % ± 2 % (S16 Table). The global fit's pre/post contrast within a run is also exposed to the within-run variance change, and its period-level residual is biased when the pairs are asymmetric (S10 Table). The paper reports both estimators and prefers neither. Null-model normalisation of atoms is a direction the ΦID authors have named (Liardi et al., 2025, for PID on magnetoencephalography); on the family the dependence it addresses is r₁'s route into sts, since TDMI = 2S depends on r₁ alone.

## Discussion

### What the finding is and is not

The analysis says nothing about whether synergy in the intended sense is present in the brain; what it establishes is what the estimator responds to. On the family of two AR(1) processes correlated only at lag 0 the Gaussian-MMI synergy atom is the two self-prediction atoms plus the double redundancy, balanced by four negative atoms of the kind MMI can produce (Results 1), and with unequal coefficients MMI takes the smaller self-information, so that the atom falls below xtx + yty + rtr — the pattern the data's own atom table shows. The atom therefore inherits the dependence of self-information on lag-1 autocorrelation: per standard deviation of the pairs' variation within a window r₁ outweighs |q| 4.7-fold in population and 1.4-fold in the windowed estimator's own dependence, and lagged interaction, the thing the atom is meant to capture, moves it non-monotonically. What follows for a contrast between states is narrow and definite: when the compared conditions differ in r₁, the MMI-sts contrast moves with that difference, at a rate that depends on the estimator (S13 Table), whether or not anything the word synergy is meant to capture has changed.

On this dataset MMI-sts fell with r₁, the two contrasts share most of their reliable variance, the group-level fall is close to what a generator with the data's spectrum gives for the same fall of pair r₁ (−0.081 against −0.094), the regional map follows regional r₁, and the AR(1)-substituted estimate carries the contrast. The remainder, the 0.0115 nats by which that estimate falls more than the observed sts, is not distinguished from the 0.003–0.005 of a simulated pure autocorrelation change; across subjects it scales with the r₁ change at −0.75 per unit (t interval [−1.13, −0.37]), which excludes the band-passed generator's −0.18, contains the AR(1) generator's −0.39 and ends at the null's −0.37, and at the group level the data do not distinguish among the three. What it is — lagged interaction, a weakening of slow shared fluctuations, or an autocorrelation change of a shape the generators lack — is not identified (Results 4).

### The literature

Two uses of MMI-sts should be distinguished. A contrast between states, between groups, or across a session is exposed wherever the compared conditions differ in r₁: BOLD lag-1 autocorrelation rises in propofol sedation and falls in deep anaesthesia and in disorders of consciousness (Huang et al., 2018); in these data it fell under DMT relative to placebo and rose within the placebo run; between-group designs are exposed to any group difference in r₁ from physiology, motion, age or scanner. A spatial map of synergy across regions is exposed through regional differences in r₁: higher r₁ gives higher sts with rtr moving much less (∂rtr/∂r₁ = 0.06 on the family), so a regional autocorrelation gradient produces a synergy-minus-redundancy gradient of the same spatial form. The applicability table (S20 Table) lists nine empirical studies (Down et al., 2026; Gatica et al., 2024; Luppi et al., 2022, 2023, 2024, 2026; Nago et al., 2026; Tarchi et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025), whose main texts were read in full. For every study within the map's scope it gives the same conditional statement, the sign of r₁ change that would produce the reported effect on the map; the primary quantity of Luppi et al. (2023), CCS emergence capacity on binarised signals, is outside the scope, and the main text of Tarchi et al. (2026) does not state its redundancy function. The statement is what the map predicts, not evidence about any study, since none of them reports regional r₁.

The human (HCP) data of Luppi et al. (2022), at TR 0.72 s, fall outside the table's TR range; there the ideal-band-pass r₁ is 0.97 and ∂sts/∂r₁ = 32.8, but per unit of regional spectral difference the exposure is almost the same at every TR (S3 Text), so the derivative does not rank datasets and the exposure is not specific to TR 2 s data. That study's phase-randomised surrogates, which on the family give the regional r₁ gradient up to noise, are the table's one test of the exposure: the surrogate gradient's correlations with the six macroscale maps of their Table 1 were small (|ρ| ≤ 0.26; one significant) against 0.22–0.54 for the observed gradient (S20 Table). On the family a change in q moves synergy and redundancy oppositely by equal amounts and a change in r₁ moves mainly sts, so synergy and redundancy moving in opposite directions (Down et al., 2026; in part, in the regional classification of Tarchi et al., 2026) is the q signature or a mixture, not the r₁ signature. And ΦR = rtr on the symmetric family falls with a fall in either factor, so the result for sts does not carry over to ΦR (Luppi et al., 2024, 2026). Of the studies in the table only Luppi et al. (2022) addressed autocorrelation, which is the subject of Varley (2024).

### Recommendations

Report the mean lag-1 autocorrelation beside sts, per condition and subject, and its contrast beside the sts contrast, with the effective sample size computed from the autocorrelation function (Afyouni et al., 2019). Report each sts contrast beside its AR(1)-substituted estimate from each pair's measured (a_x, a_y, q); the difference between the two is not a measure of lagged interaction unless a calibration reproduces the study's own relation between that difference and the autocorrelation change. Compare exposure across datasets per unit of spectral difference, not per unit of r₁, since ∂sts/∂r₁ grows without bound as r₁ approaches 1. Do not expect prewhitening to remove the dependence from band-passed data, and check CCS contrasts against r₁ too (Results 6). Where a null is wanted, simulate surrogate series that preserve each region's autocorrelation and the pair's lag-0 correlation — from each pair's AR(1)-substituted matrix, or from the two spectra with the measured lag-0 correlation — and pass them through the same estimator; the AR(1)-substituted value itself is not a null, since its difference from the observed sts has a non-zero finite-sample expectation (Results 4). A null that removes every cross-correlation sits at q = 0, where sts is at its maximum for a given r₁, and so tests lag-0 correlation rather than lagged interaction.

### Limitations

One dataset, one drug, one parcellation, N = 14, TR 2 s, no deconvolution in the primary analysis; the DMT contrast is a proof of concept. There is no ground truth for synergy in these data, so the analysis establishes what the estimator responds to, not what the brain does. Two redundancy functions were examined, both Gaussian. On Gaussian systems every PID whose redundancy depends only on the source–target marginals reduces to MMI when the target is univariate (Barrett, 2015; Kay & Ince, 2018), which covers the lattice nodes with a single past or single future variable but not the joint-target nodes or the double redundancy, ΦID's own extensions (Mediano et al., 2021); so "MMI-specific" is not a licensed phrase, and other Gaussian PIDs could share or not share the property. The calibration of the AR(1)-substituted estimate is on generators solved to this dataset's operating point and spectrum, not on a second dataset; the AR(1) family does not fit the data beyond lag 1 (S14 Table), the coupled family is symmetric, what acts inside the windows to produce the symmetric cross-lag departure is not identified (S9 Table), and the windowed estimator's manufacture is not removed by the substitution. The closed form, exact on the family, matches the data's atom table with each pair's measured coefficients only to within 0.053 nats for sts and 0.04 for the mirror atoms, and its exchange rate for within-pair asymmetry is a population statement that the estimators recover only in part (S10 Table). The primary contrast was planned as a test of a different hypothesis and its statistic was fixed after whole-run fits on the same data had shown a decrease; the account was derived afterwards on the same data; most later computations were entered in the record with a rule and a prediction before they were run, but not all (Methods); and several recorded predictions failed, among them the calibration's predictions for a coupling change, after which a reading of the residual was built and has been withdrawn (Methods; S19 Table). The git record establishes ordering, not blindness.

(2023): fourteen subjects (six of twenty excluded for head movement by Singleton et al., 2025), each with a DMT and a placebo run of 840 TRs at TR = 2 s, injection at TR 240 (S1 Text); with global signal regression (ts_gsr, primary) and without it (ts_demean, sensitivity), both band-passed to 0.01–0.08 Hz; Schaefer-100 parcels (Schaefer et al., 2018; Yeo et al., 2011) and 16 subcortical ones (Tian et al., 2020), one region, mean-filled for one subject, dropped for all: 115 regions and 6,555 pairs (COBIDAS checklist: S4 Text).

### Estimator

Atoms were computed with phyid (Imperial-MIND-lab, 2026; calc_PhiID, Gaussian, τ = 1) and averaged over pairs, in 14 non-overlapping, separately fitted 60-TR windows per run (W = 60; W = 30 as a second) or in one global fit per run, its local atoms averaged into 28 bins. For two series with these autocorrelations and zero true correlation, the effective sample size of their correlation, n / (1 + 2 Σ_k ρ_k²) (the global form of the estimator reviewed by Afyouni et al., 2019, their Eq. 8; Bartlett, 1935, gave the AR(1) case), is about 18 in a 60-TR window over six lags of the pooled placebo function (S3 Text). A second implementation of the same estimator, computing the atoms directly from each window's 4 × 4 correlation matrix, reproduces phyid to ≤ 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ on every saved window and bin mean, ≤ 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ on the regional values and 1.0 × 10⁻¹³ on the TR-local series checked.

### Redundancy functions

MMI takes each single-target redundancy as the smaller single-source mutual information (Barrett, 2015) and rtr as the smallest of the four (Mediano et al., 2021, Appendix, Definition 2). CCS (Ince, 2017) is a single-target redundancy function; its ΦID extension (Mediano et al., 2021, Appendix, Definition 1; 2025, SI Appendix, Definition 2) takes the double redundancy as the pointwise double co-information at the samples whose marginal and full pointwise mutual informations share a sign; phyid applies a different fifth condition (S3 Text). The prewhitened CCS values of S11 Table were computed with phyid's mask; every other CCS value in the paper uses the published definition.

### The closed form

For unit-variance stationary AR(1) processes the correlation matrix of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}) is

R₄ = [[1, q, a, aq], [q, 1, aq, a], [a, aq, 1, q], [aq, a, q, 1]];

its block determinants and the Möbius inversion of Mediano et al. (2021), a linear system with no sign constraint, give the atoms of Results 1, checked to 4 × 10⁻¹⁵ (S3 Text). With unequal coefficients the cross-lag entries are a_y q and a_x q; on a grid of mean a 0.80–0.90, q 0.05–0.70 and |a_x − a_y| up to 0.10 the statements of Results 1 hold to 7 × 10⁻¹⁶ (S18 Table). A pair sharing a slow component gives the same τ = 1 signature as lagged coupling, and the two are not separable at that lag: any positive-definite stationary 4 × 4 matrix of (x_t, y_t, x_{t+1}, y_{t+1}) is the lag-0/lag-1 structure of a stable VAR(1) (S3 Text).

### Regional maps

The partialled map is the residual of the regional map's regression on regional r₁ (S3 Text). The correlations were spin-tested with 10,000 rotations of the 99 cortical parcels (Alexander-Bloch et al., 2018; rotations of Váša et al., 2018, released with the data; S5 Table), and the residual map's network structure with the same rotations, subcortex fixed (S3 Text).

### Inference

Pre-injection windows are 1–4 (bins 1–8), post windows 6–14 (bins 11–28). Inference: an exact sign-flip permutation over the 14 subjects (16,384 assignments, two-sided), which assumes that the subjects' DiDs are independent and symmetric about the tested mean, and a 95 % interval obtained by inverting that test — the set of values μ for which the test of a mean of μ has p > 0.05 — so that an interval excludes zero exactly when p < 0.05. The pre-specified subject-bootstrap percentile intervals were narrower, by a median of 12.5 % (S17 Table). Correlations across subjects carry Fisher-z intervals, and slopes across subjects t intervals on 12 degrees of freedom. A phase-randomised surrogate (Theiler et al., 1992), pre-specified as a second null, is reported as a check of stationarity; motion control residualised window means on framewise displacement. ΦR is TDMI − I(X;X′) − I(Y;Y′) + rtr. Seed 20261120.

### The primary contrast and the exploratory analyses

The pre-specified DiD of whole-brain MMI-sts on ts_gsr at W = 60 is the primary contrast; its statistic was fixed after global fits on the same data had shown a decrease (Pre-registration and deviations), so it is not a blind confirmation. Every other quantity is exploratory and is reported with its effect size and interval, without threshold language; a p value quoted for an exploratory quantity describes the sample and is not a claim. The original pre-specified analysis and its exploratory results are in S1 Text and S1–S7 Tables.

### The AR(1)-substituted estimate and its calibration

The AR(1)-substituted estimate keeps a window's two lag-1 autocorrelations, sets its two lag-0 entries to their mean q and replaces its two cross-lag correlations by a_y q and a_x q (S3 Text). The calibration simulates the design (14 subjects × 2 runs × 840 samples × 300 pairs, the DMT run changed from sample 300, 50 replicates) on two generators: AR(1) pairs with coefficients drawn around the data's mean window-level regional r₁ (a_x, a_y ~ N(0.85, 0.0125²)), whose windowed sts level (0.715) lies far below the data's (1.155) and whose pair r₁ in 60-sample windows is 0.786 at a = 0.85 (S18 Table); and a band-passed generator solved to the data's spectrum and to its mean pair r₁ and |q| in 60-sample windows (S3 Text; S10 Table). A third expectation comes from a finite-sample null, a band-pass filter model whose cross-lag correlations equal the AR(1) values in population, fitted to the data's autocorrelation function and solved to its window-level pair r₁ and |q| in each run × period cell, so that it carries the data's fall of pair r₁ (its own DiD −0.0146); its +0.0054 is from a single simulation, without a replicate SD (S3 Text). The aligned statistic A_other (defined with Table 3), the residual's response to five constructions of a change in lagged structure, and their pure-autocorrelation expectations on the band-passed generator were computed under rules and predictions recorded beforehand (S18 Table).

### Remedies

Prewhitening replaced each region's series by the residuals of its own AR(p) fit (p by BIC over 1–5, or p = 1; S11 Table). HRF deconvolution used rsHRF 1.7.0 (Wu et al., 2013, 2021; S2 Text). Manufacture was assessed on four matched pairs of simulated conditions (S16 Table).

### Literature search

Published empirical fMRI ΦID studies reporting synergy, redundancy or quantities built on them were identified on 14 September 2026 by web searches run from a session of the AI system (the queries were not recorded), and their main texts were read in full on 20 September for the quantity reported, preprocessing, parcellation, redundancy function, lag and any treatment of autocorrelation. The scope, fixed before the search, is Gaussian-MMI ΦID synergy on BOLD without HRF deconvolution at TR ≈ 1–3 s; studies outside it are listed and marked (S20 Table).
